# Legends and alt text: Figures 1–2

**Figure 1. How ARBITER works.** A reviewer defines the outcome and approves the proposed result before domain assessment. Guided by `/arbiter-assess`, the LLM agent reads reports and registry records and submits signalling answers with evidence. The deterministic ARBITER server makes no model calls: it captures and versions sources, validates submissions, binds evidence spans, proposes judgements using the official RoB 2 algorithms, records history and exports a verifiable `.arbiter.zip` bundle. The assessing model can adopt a different domain judgement through a recorded, reasoned override. Monospace labels name representative MCP tools; italic labels summarize responses. The server saves nothing from an incomplete or unbound answer; across the 102 trials it rejected 53 of 637 `save_domain_judgment` calls (32 for evidence or citation structure, 21 for other input errors), and every affected domain was saved after resubmission. The saved bundle contains source identities and bound evidence, rather than copies of the trial PDFs. MCP, Model Context Protocol.

Alt text: Four phases—Capture, Propose and approve, Assess each domain, Review and export—span Reviewer, lavender LLM agent, mint ARBITER server and pale-yellow Bundle lanes. An orthogonal approval connector through arbiter review unlocks domain work. Tool calls descend from agent to server; responses return upwards. The server validates and binds evidence, computes proposed judgements, records history and packages the assessment. The reviewer receives a report and a verifiable bundle.

**Figure 2. Engineering an assessment: one AVATAR trace.** AVATAR, all-cause death, is illustrative, not representative. **A**, a sequence diagram traces D5 question 5.1 through six black numbered badges: get guidance, read sources, reason from context, save answers and citations, check source bindings and compute the judgement, and review/export. Lavender identifies the LLM agent, mint the deterministic ARBITER server, and pale yellow the exported bundle. Monospace pills name real MCP tools, evidence handles and searchable-projection hash prefixes. Source tabs identify the report or registry, page and saved line span; these can be narrower than the requested read windows. Italic quotations and justification excerpts are verbatim, with ellipses for omissions and normalized extraction whitespace. Pale-grey highlights link context evidence to the agent’s composite-outcome interpretation. The second reasoning sentence states the recorded evidence gap. Question 5.1 remains No information. Two of four cited handles and their hash prefixes are shown, with +2 indicating additional citations. The server applies the official RoB 2 algorithm to 5.1 NI, 5.2 PN and 5.3 PN; the proposed Some concerns judgement was adopted without override. The seven-source count, evidence, answers and archived independent-verifier receipt are loaded and asserted at build time. The verifier’s verified receipt concerns structure and evidence binding, not clinical correctness. The public extension is .arbiter.zip; the input archive retains its historical name.

**B**, the same machinery across D1–D5. One short evidence tag per domain leads to the complete answer pattern in official question order and an aligned judgement pill. Yellow denotes direct-support evidence; pale grey denotes context. Diagonal hatching and grey question numbers denote questions not asked through RoB 2 branching. D1–D4 yield Low; D5 and the Overall rule yield Some concerns. Low has a white fill and heavy outline; Some concerns has a pale-blue fill. All AVATAR domain judgements were adopted without override. Source details and full bound spans are recorded in the provenance notes.

**C**, paired trajectories for the same trial’s D5 assessment. Blue identifies ARBITER and orange Unassisted. The ARBITER blocks show selected whole-trial tool counts, explicitly labelled “this trial”: 15 get_domain_context, 14 read_pages and seven save_domain_judgment calls. Counts use unique tool-use IDs and include failed attempts and retries; they are not D5-only counts or the full MCP inventory. Both displayed D5 passages were returned by a read_pages call during D5. The Unassisted Thought box reproduces its full claim and flags “registered 5-year time frame” with an orange outline; “no citation” refers to that output’s unsupported claim. Its D4 4.3 skip is separately noted as an invalid answer path. The check (✓) and cross (✗) mean agreement or disagreement with the adjudicators’ **consensus preferred D5 judgement, Some concerns**; they do not denote verifier results. ARBITER returned Some concerns, Unassisted Low, and the published D5 label was Low. Overall was not adjudicated. The full trace appears in **eTable 31**.
