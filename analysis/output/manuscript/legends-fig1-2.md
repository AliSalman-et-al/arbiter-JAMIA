# Legends and alt text: Figures 1–2

**Figure 1. How ARBITER works.** A reviewer chooses a trial and outcome and approves the result to assess. The AI model (LLM agent) follows the ARBITER assessment guide, reads trial reports and registry records, and supplies answers and quotations. ARBITER is rule-based software with no AI: it stores unchangeable source records, checks quotations, applies the official RoB 2 rules, and packages the audit file. Plain labels describe each action; small grey monospace labels identify representative tools, and italic labels summarize responses. The reviewer approves through `arbiter review` and receives a report and `.arbiter.zip` audit file. The assessing model can record a reasoned override of a computed judgement; there were no overrides in this example. The server saves nothing from an incomplete or unbound answer; across the 102 trials it rejected 53 of 637 `save_domain_judgment` calls (32 for evidence or citation structure, 21 for other input errors), and every affected domain was saved after resubmission. Stored source identities and evidence quotations, rather than complete trial PDFs, are packaged with answers and history for an independent verifier.

Alt text: Four horizontal lanes show Reviewer, lavender AI model, mint ARBITER server labelled rule-based software with no AI, and yellow Audit file. Across Capture, Propose and approve, Assess each domain, and Review and export, the model reads sources, picks an outcome and analysis for approval, answers questions with citations, and inspects the assessment. The reviewer approves the result before domain work. ARBITER records the choice, checks every quotation, applies RoB 2 rules, and packages a checkable audit file. Trial reports and registry enter at Capture. The reviewer receives the report and audit file.

**Figure 2. AVATAR, assessed by ARBITER, domain by domain.** AVATAR compares early surgery with watchful waiting in asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis; the assessed outcome is all-cause death. This trial is illustrative, not representative. Each row links a plain-language question to source quotations, the AI model's answer and reason, ARBITER's checks and complete answer pattern, and the computed domain rating. Questions are paraphrased; official wording and the full trace appear in **eTable 31**. Paper pages refer to the Circulation report (CIRCULATIONAHA.121.057639.pdf); the registry is NCT02436655. Quotations are verbatim with ellipses for omissions and normalized extraction whitespace. Yellow marks quotations directly supporting answers; grey marks background context; a dashed box marks reasoning without a quotation. Y, yes; PY, probably yes; PN, probably no; N, no; NI, no information. Hatched chips are questions not needed under RoB 2 branching. Lavender identifies the AI model and mint identifies ARBITER, rule-based software with no AI.

The rows illustrate verified allocation evidence and a recorded open point; registry evidence of treatment awareness and branching; near-complete outcome data; unblinded assessors with a separate reasoning basis for whether knowledge could affect death recording; and the decisive analysis-plan question. For question 5.1, the report's “prespecified” wording and registry composite-outcome entry do not show a dated plan for this measurement and analysis. The saved answer is No information. With 5.2 and 5.3 Probably no, the official rule yields Some concerns; the other four domains are Low, so the overall rating is Some concerns. The Overall sentence describes this example, in which no domain is High.

The orange strip compares the same AI model with the same instructions, without ARBITER: every domain was Low, and its quoted 5.1 claim had no source. The registry lists death only within a combined outcome. Both expert adjudicators preferred Some concerns for D5; the check (✓) and cross (✗) mean agreement and disagreement with their **consensus preferred D5 judgement**, not verifier success or clinical correctness. The published review's D5 judgement was Low. Overall was not adjudicated. The footer reports the archived independent-verifier result, “verified”, for audit structure and evidence binding; it does not certify the clinical judgement. The audit file retains source identities, evidence quotations, answers and decisions.
