# Legends and alt text: Figures 1–2

**Figure 1. How ARBITER works.** A reviewer defines the outcome and approves the proposed result before domain assessment. Guided by `/arbiter-assess`, the LLM agent reads reports and registry records and submits signalling answers with evidence. The deterministic ARBITER server makes no model calls: it captures and versions sources, validates submissions, binds evidence spans, proposes judgements using the official RoB 2 algorithms, records history and exports a verifiable `.arbiter.zip` bundle. The assessing model can adopt a different domain judgement through a recorded, reasoned override. Monospace labels name representative MCP tools; italic labels summarize responses. The server saves nothing from an incomplete or unbound answer; across the 102 trials it rejected 53 of 637 `save_domain_judgment` calls (32 for evidence or citation structure, 21 for other input errors), and every affected domain was saved after resubmission. The saved bundle contains source identities and bound evidence, rather than copies of the trial PDFs. MCP, Model Context Protocol.

Alt text: Four phases—Capture, Propose and approve, Assess each domain, Review and export—span Reviewer, lavender LLM agent, mint ARBITER server and pale-yellow Bundle lanes. An orthogonal approval connector through arbiter review unlocks domain work. Tool calls descend from agent to server; responses return upwards. The server validates and binds evidence, computes proposed judgements, records history and packages the assessment. The reviewer receives a report and a verifiable bundle.

**Figure 2. Engineering an assessment: one AVATAR trace.** AVATAR, all-cause death, is an illustrative cardiology trial with mixed domain judgements, not a representative case. Five aligned domain rows connect three columns from left to right: white Evidence cards bound by the server, a lavender LLM-agent lane containing answers, citations and explicitly labelled justification excerpts, and a mint deterministic-server lane. Source chips give the file or registry name, page, complete bound line range and searchable projection-hash prefix. Displayed quotations may be shorter than their bound spans; ellipses indicate omissions. Two PDF extraction word breaks are joined without changing words. Solid yellow highlights denote direct support, pale grey denotes context, and an unfilled dashed outline denotes inference. Evidence colours link quotations to corresponding words in the agent’s recorded justification. Lavender identifies the agent, not an evidence type. The complete verbatim D1 unknown is recorded with a question-mark marker.

The server column displays every signalling answer in official question order. Grey-numbered chips with a solid grey outline and diagonal hatch fill identify questions not asked under RoB 2 branching; heavier outlines identify the questions illustrated in the agent lane, including both D4 questions. Right-pointing arrows lead to a fixed right-hand judgement column aligned across D1–D5 and Overall: plain white Low for D1–D4 and pale ARBITER-blue Some concerns for D5. All AVATAR domain judgements were adopted without override. D4 4.3 uses the report’s statement that the data and safety monitoring board was not blinded; 4.4 rests on an explicitly identified inference about an outcome involving no judgement. D5 5.1 is No information, with two context quotations: the report names time to death as prespecified, while the registry names death within composite outcomes. Context does not by itself establish the absence of a dated analysis plan. The Overall line applies the official rule that any Some concerns yields Overall Some concerns in this case.

The pale-yellow bundle strip reports seven sources captured and hashed, 16 archived evidence records, 15 signalling questions answered and seven not asked, and the archived independent-verifier result, verified. Counts and status are read and asserted at build time. The verifier receipt concerns structural and evidence-binding checks, not clinical correctness. `.arbiter.zip` is the public bundle extension; the archived input retains its historical name.

The pale-orange strip, “Unassisted, same trial (same prompt, no ARBITER)”, contains only two contrasts. D4 shows 4.1 N, 4.2 N and an orange-outlined 4.3 NA alongside its own words, “Assessors were not blinded to allocation”, and the author annotation “invalid answer path”. D5 reproduces its claim, “Time to death was a prespecified secondary endpoint with a registered 5-year time frame”; the registered-time-frame phrase has an orange outline. The adjacent author annotation states that the registry lists all-cause death only within a composite outcome. These flags are not evidence-type labels. The footer compares **D5**: Published Low, Adjudicators Some concerns and Unassisted Low. Overall was not adjudicated. Published is dark grey, Adjudicators black and Unassisted orange.
