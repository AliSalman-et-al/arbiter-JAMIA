# Legends and alt text: Figures 1–2

**Figure 1. ARBITER splits the work: an AI model reads and answers; rule-based software checks quotes, applies RoB 2 rules and records everything.** Lanes show the reviewer, AI model, ARBITER server and audit file across capture, approval, domain assessment and export. Plain labels show actions; grey text names the underlying tool calls. The reviewer receives the HTML report and audit file, both checkable by an independent verifier. RoB 2 is the Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomised trials.

Alt text: The reviewer chooses a trial and outcome and approves the result to assess. The AI model reads reports, selects evidence and answers questions. ARBITER stores source records, checks quotes and applies the RoB 2 rules. The reviewer receives an HTML assessment report and an audit file containing sources, quotes, answers and decisions; both can be checked by an independent verifier.

**Figure 2. Each answer is tied to stored sources, and RoB 2 rules compute the rating across every domain.** AVATAR compares early surgery with watchful waiting in asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis; the outcome is all-cause death. This trial was chosen to illustrate the process, not as representative. Rows link paraphrased questions, source excerpts, AI answers, software checks and domain ratings. Letter/page tabs identify sources; “Weighed against” and “Open point” give counter-evidence and unknowns. A missing dated analysis plan leads to Some concerns overall. Full answers and official question wording appear in the AVATAR complete trace in the supplement. Y, yes; PY, probably yes; PN, probably no; N, no; NI, no information.

Alt text: AVATAR's five risk-of-bias domains connect source excerpts to the AI model's answers and the ratings computed by ARBITER. Four domains are Low. For selection of the reported result, the stored sources do not establish a dated plan for this analysis. The model answers No information, and the RoB 2 rules yield Some concerns for that domain and overall. Counter-evidence and unresolved questions remain visible alongside the answers.

Figure 3 Every domain cell received two blinded independent ratings. The nested subsets identify disputed cells, arm-discordant cells and equal forced choices. Side notes identify cells with three concordant labels, which give no forced-choice information, and Overall derived from each expert’s domain ratings. Independent ratings and signalling answers were frozen before labels were shown. The three excluded trials had unsupported designs. Alt text: Included trials lead to two complete expert assessments, followed by nested preference subsets.

Figure 4 Adjudicators preferred ARBITER in most arm disagreements. Rows are grouped as Primary (agreed choice), Sensitivity (each adjudicator; split cells counted as losses), and Post hoc strata. The primary row retains equal forced choices; expert-specific rows retain all arm disagreements. Split decisions count as ARBITER losses without assigning a win to Unassisted. Blue circles show ARBITER, with no marker encoding for adjudicator. Right-hand labels show ARBITER count/denominator and the Unassisted count; ties can credit both arms. The dashed line marks equal preference. Alt text: Grouped forest of primary, sensitivity and strictness estimates of ARBITER preference. CI, confidence interval; 10,000 percentile bootstrap replicates, trials resampled within meta-analysis; seed 20261018.

Alt text: Domain and Overall agreement against each expert, above three-part directional disagreement bars. Figure 6 ARBITER generally agreed more often with the experts on individual signalling questions. Question-level paired ARBITER − Unassisted differences compare collapsed agreement with each expert’s independent answer. Blue circles encode the ARBITER contrast, with expert identity and n printed on each row, grouped by domain. Collapsed agreement combines Y/PY and N/PN, with NI separate. Only explicit matched answers evaluable in both arms contribute. Alt text: Collapsed per-question paired differences and intervals for both experts, grouped by domain. Within-direction choices (Y↔PY or N↔PN) accounted for about half of five-level disagreements against Adjudicator 1 (ARBITER 322/617, 52.2%; Unassisted 377/732, 51.5%) and about three-quarters of five-level disagreements against Adjudicator 2 (ARBITER 368/504, 73.0%; Unassisted 388/566, 68.6%) (eTable 5); cross-direction counts include NI versus either direction. CI, confidence interval; 10,000 percentile bootstrap replicates, trials resampled within meta-analysis; seed 20261018.
