The benchmark covered 102 trials assessed in two arms, with 154 domain disagreements reviewed independently (Figure 3).

Both models agreed poorly with published review labels (weighted κ 0.34 for ARBITER and 0.28 for Unassisted; Table 1; Figure 4A); where they disagreed, they were usually stricter: ARBITER 88/124 and Unassisted 69/115.

Adjudicators usually upheld stricter model ratings: 73/77 for ARBITER and 48/59 for Unassisted; for more lenient ratings, the corresponding counts were 22/28 and 19/38 (Figure 4B).

Among adjudicated cells where the two arms disagreed, both adjudicators preferred ARBITER’s rating in 54 of 62 cells (87.1%; 95% CI 79.0–94.3; Figure 5); their independent ratings agreed with ARBITER in 107 and 120 of 154 reviewed ratings (Table 2).

Among the 47 eligible Unassisted ratings the adjudicators did not choose, 29 hinged on at least one question that alone changed the rating; the analysis-plan question (5.1) did so in 18 (Figure 6). In the post hoc rules-only recomputation, 3 of the original 62 arm disagreements changed, each to ARBITER’s rating, which the adjudicators had preferred; the other 59 were unchanged (eTable 23).

In the post hoc description of the 18 question 5.1 losses, 13 had a supplied registry record, protocol or SAP, but none of the Unassisted justifications mentioned any of these documents (eTable 21).

Without ARBITER, 65 of 509 evaluable domain ratings broke the RoB 2 rules (12.8%; 95% CI 10.0–15.6; Table 3); ARBITER had zero domain rule breaks by design and no recorded overrides, and every quotation was found in its content-hashed stored source.

ARBITER’s system runtime was a median [IQR] of 7.6 [6.1–10.5] minutes per trial versus 0.5 [0.4–0.5] for Unassisted; API list-price equivalents were US$3.78 [3.46–4.10] versus US$0.09 [0.07–0.13]. The runs used a subscription. Paired mean differences were 11.0 (8.4–14.1) minutes and US$3.68 (3.61–3.75) (95% CIs; eTable 28).
