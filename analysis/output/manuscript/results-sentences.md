The benchmark covered 102 trials assessed in two arms, with 154 domain disagreements reviewed independently (Figure 3).

Both models agreed poorly with published review labels (weighted κ 0.34 for ARBITER and 0.28 for Unassisted; Table 1; Figure 4A).

Adjudicators usually upheld stricter model ratings in the post hoc analysis: 73/77 for ARBITER and 48/59 for Unassisted; for more lenient ratings, the corresponding counts were 22/28 and 19/38 (Figure 4B).

When the two arms disagreed, both adjudicators chose ARBITER’s rating in 54 of 62 cells (87.1%; 95% CI 79.0–94.3; Figure 5); their independent ratings agreed with ARBITER in 107 and 120 of 154 reviewed ratings (Table 2).

Among the 47 eligible Unassisted ratings the adjudicators did not choose, 29 hinged on at least one question that alone changed the rating; the analysis-plan question (5.1) did so in 18 (Figure 6). In the post hoc rules-only recomputation, 3 of the original 62 arm disagreements changed: Unassisted wins changed from 7 to 10, published-label agreement from 395 to 385 of 510, and agreement with the adjudicators’ agreed choice from 69 to 64 of 129 (eTable 22).

Without ARBITER, 65 of 509 evaluable domain ratings broke the RoB 2 rules (12.8%; 95% CI 10.0–15.6; Table 3); ARBITER had no domain rule breaks or overrides, and every quoted text record was linked to its stored source.

ARBITER took a median [IQR] of 7.6 [6.1–10.5] minutes per trial versus 0.5 [0.4–0.5] for Unassisted; API list-price equivalents were US$3.78 [3.46–4.10] versus US$0.09 [0.07–0.13]. Paired mean differences were 11.0 (8.4–14.1) minutes and US$3.68 (3.61–3.75) (95% CIs; eTable 27).
