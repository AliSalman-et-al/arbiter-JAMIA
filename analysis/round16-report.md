## Full results sentences

Exploratory severity distributions showed that both experts rated Low less often than any other source: Published 427/510 (83.7%); ARBITER 375/510 (73.5%); Unassisted 407/510 (79.8%); Expert 1 277/510 (54.3%); Expert 2 346/510 (67.8%). Both experts assessed the complete benchmark independently: 102 trials from 16 meta-analyses and 15 specialties contributed 510 domain cells (Figure 3; Table 1; eTable 1). The experts agreed on 388/510 domain ratings (76.1%; weighted κ 0.59), with the lowest agreement in D3 (54/102; eTable 2).

The prespecified forced-choice endpoint favoured ARBITER: the experts’ agreed choice matched its rating in 53/60 cells (88.3%; 95% CI 79.7–96.3) where the arms disagreed (Figure 4; eTable 3).

Prespecified independent-rating comparisons favoured ARBITER against both experts. Expert 1 agreed with ARBITER in 371/510 cells (72.7%), Unassisted in 332/510 (65.1%) and Published in 301/510 (59.0%). Expert 2 agreed with ARBITER in 433/510 cells (84.9%), Unassisted in 400/510 (78.4%) and Published in 362/510 (71.0%). The paired ARBITER − Unassisted differences (95% CIs) were 7.6 (5.1–10.2) and 6.5 (3.5–9.4) percentage points, respectively. ARBITER’s weighted κ values (0.51, 0.69) fell either side of expert-to-expert κ 0.59. Unassisted (0.35, 0.53) and Published (0.21, 0.29) fell below that yardstick. Derived Overall comparisons on the 81 trials with a published Overall gave paired differences of 13.6 (6.2–21.0) and 17.3 (8.6–25.9) percentage points. Absolute Overall agreement was lower for every source; the experts agreed with each other on 42/81 matched Overall ratings (κ 0.33), and ARBITER’s matched Overall agreement was 32/81 against Expert 1 and 60/81 against Expert 2 (Table 2; Figure 5A; eTables 4–6; eFigure 1).

Exploratory analyses showed that ARBITER’s higher sensitivity for Some concerns or High against each expert came without lower specificity. Specificity was 97.8% versus 95.7% against Expert 1 and 95.1% versus 95.1% against Expert 2 for ARBITER versus Unassisted. Against Expert 1, sensitivity was 55.4% versus 39.1% for ARBITER versus Unassisted; paired sensitivity and specificity differences were 16.3 (10.6–22.1) and 2.2 (0.7–3.9) percentage points, respectively. Against Expert 2, sensitivity was 72.0% versus 52.4% for ARBITER versus Unassisted; paired sensitivity and specificity differences were 19.5 (12.8–26.4) and 0.0 (−2.5 to 2.6) percentage points, respectively (95% CIs; eTables 7–8). When ARBITER was stricter than Published, Expert 1 matched ARBITER in 64/88 cells and Published in 5/88; Expert 2 matched ARBITER in 65/88 and Published in 14/88 (Figure 5B).

The prespecified published-label benchmark showed no clear difference between the arms in exact agreement or κ. The paired exact-agreement difference was −1.8 (−5.1 to 1.6) percentage points and the weighted κ difference 0.06 (−0.03 to 0.14) (95% CIs; Table 2; Figure 5A; eTables 9–10). Against published labels, ARBITER’s higher sensitivity came with lower specificity: paired differences were +15.7 (6.8–24.4) and −4.4 (−7.6 to −1.2) percentage points, respectively (95% CIs); against the experts it did not.

Prespecified signalling-answer comparisons favoured ARBITER. Rules-only recomputation did not raise Unassisted’s agreement. Paired collapsed signalling differences were 5.0 (3.6–6.4) and 3.3 (1.9–4.7) percentage points against Experts 1 and 2, respectively (Figure 6; eTable 11; eFigure 2). Recomputation changed 26 Unassisted domain ratings, including 6 original arm disagreements. Among 60 agreed forced choices, Unassisted matched 7 before and 8 after recomputation. Its independent-rating agreement changed from 332/510 to 324/510 against Expert 1 and from 400/510 to 391/510 against Expert 2 (eTable 12).

The prespecified process audit found rule breaks in Unassisted ratings, while ARBITER enforced the rules. Unassisted had 65/509 evaluable domain ratings with a rule break (12.8%; 95% CI 10.0–15.6). ARBITER had zero domain rule breaks by design and no recorded domain overrides; all 1538 required answers cited evidence, 1407/1407 quotations were found in the stored source, and all 102 audit files passed the independent verifier (Table 3; eTables 13–14).

The prespecified time and cost analyses showed greater resource use for ARBITER. Median [IQR] runtime was 7.6 [6.1–10.5] minutes per trial for ARBITER and 0.5 [0.4–0.5] for Unassisted, with a paired mean difference of 11.0 (8.4–14.1) minutes. API list-price equivalents were US$3.78 [3.46–4.10] and US$0.09 [0.07–0.13] per trial, respectively, with a paired mean difference of US$3.68 (3.61–3.75) (95% CIs; Table 3; eTable 15).
