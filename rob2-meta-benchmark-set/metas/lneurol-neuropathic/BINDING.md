# lneurol-neuropathic

Pharmacotherapy and non-invasive neuromodulation for neuropathic pain: SR and MA
doi: 10.1016/s1474-4422(25)00068-7

Binding: stated: rated for the meta's primary outcome, responders (>=50% pain reduction)

Contrast: Reconciliation: 'risk of bias of the primary outcome' (N3) means the META's primary efficacy outcome, defined as the proportion of responders (>=50%, else 30%, else at least moderate pain relief; N1, N2). That is exactly what Appendix 7 Table 1 'Responder Analyses' holds (N6), with the Outcome column naming which threshold was used for each trial. Table 2 (N7) carries ratings for trials that contributed only continuous data; trial names overlap between the two tables only once (Rice et al., 2021), so each of our 7 trials has a single rating, for its responder result. Time point: data taken at the trial's primary-endpoint time point, or the first time point after week 3 (N4). Contrast: active vs placebo/sham; multi-dose arms pooled (Allen); for Attal 2021 only M1 vs sham. Denominators are randomised/ITT numbers, and events were sometimes rebuilt from printed percentages (Allen, Arezzo, Attal 2021). Our 7 TRIALS.csv labels match the rendered colour grid on mmc1 p57.

- N1: "The primary efficacy outcome was the proportion of responders (50% or 30% reduction in baseline pain intensity or moderate pain relief)." (binding-fetched/fetched/lneurol-neuropathic/Soliman_Pharmacoterapy_2025.pdf, physical p1 (journal p413), Summary Methods)
- N2: "The primary efficacy outcome was the proportion of responders (at least 50% reduction in baseline pain intensity, alternatively 30%, or at least moderate pain relief). Where available, continuous pain outcomes were also extracted." (binding-fetched/fetched/lneurol-neuropathic/Soliman_Pharmacoterapy_2025.pdf, physical p3 (journal p415), Methods > Data analysis)
