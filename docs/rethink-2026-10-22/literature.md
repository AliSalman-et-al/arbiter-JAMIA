# Literature reassessment for the ARBITER manuscript

**Verdict: ISSUES.** The paper has a defensible contribution, but several broad novelty statements do not survive the closest primary studies. Position ARBITER as a specific constrained workflow and comparative evaluation, not as the first RoB automation, first code-applied RoB 2 algorithm, first rule-conformance analysis, first retrieval workflow, or first agentic RoB tool. This report supports discussion and framing only. It makes no recommendation to change analysis or manuscript text before the authors settle the story. Figures 1 and 2 can retain their current distinct roles: architecture and a worked trace. The literature does not require replacing either figure.

## Defensible novelty and claim boundaries

The strongest contribution is the combination of a **separate deterministic service that validates answer completeness, binds evidence to stored sources, computes official RoB 2 ratings, and emits a checkable record**, evaluated against an unassisted run of the same model and underlying trial files, with independent ratings for all 510 D1-D5 cells and blinded forced-choice comparison. The sources reviewed do not establish that this exact combination has already been tested. This is a bounded literature-search conclusion, not proof of global priority.

Phrase novelty as a combination and study design. For example: “We evaluated an auditable RoB 2 workflow in which a separate rule-based service validates evidence-linked answers and computes ratings, comparing it with the same model using the same trial sources without that service; two blinded experts independently rated every domain and compared blinded labels where the arms disagreed.” If the Introduction needs a gap sentence, state that existing systems and evaluations have studied retrieval, code-based aggregation, and rule-conformance separately, while the authors found no evaluation combining enforced evidence-linked computation with a matched model/system comparison and independent expert ratings across every domain. Keep “we found no evaluation” tied to a documented search and refresh that search before submission.

Do not claim that ARBITER proves its server, evidence binding, or rule engine caused the observed difference. The current comparator changes the whole workflow, including RoB 2 instructions, processing steps, and time. The causal unit is the ARBITER package versus this minimal-prompt condition. The analysis plan also says no overrides occurred in 510 ARBITER domains. Therefore discuss override capability as a design affordance, not an observed source of the result.

Do not claim quotation presence proves evidential support. The project reports 1,407 of 1,407 quotations were found in stored sources; that verifies occurrence, not whether each passage justifies the assigned answer. Liang et al. directly shows why source-support evaluation is a separate construct. Use “source-verifiable quotations” or “quotation occurrence checked,” not “all answers supported by evidence.”

Do not say ARBITER is more accurate than published review labels based on this study. The primary endpoint is expert preference on 60 cells where the two arms differed and both experts made the same forced choice. It is a conditional preference result, not accuracy across all 510 cells. The all-cell independent-rating agreement comparisons and the published-label comparisons are distinct secondary evidence. The manuscript’s current abstract reports 53/60 (88.3%) preference for ARBITER; the wider forced-choice sensitivity gives 63/80 and 61/80 ARBITER matches for each expert separately, while treating split choices as ARBITER losses yields 53/80. Preserve those denominators and estimands when presenting the headline.

## Comparator fairness and interpretation

The comparison has useful controls: same model and effort, same underlying trial source files, no fresh web retrieval in Unassisted, and no new advice to Unassisted on the RoB 2 algorithm. But ARBITER receives structured RoB 2 guidance, a server, and a multi-step workflow; Unassisted receives only the task and output-format instructions. As the manuscript already acknowledges, this estimates a **complete-system contrast**. It does not isolate the server, guidance, tools, evidence binding, or extra reasoning time. Huang, Eisele-Metzger, Nyrhi, and Gandhi all used structured RoB guidance, so Unassisted is not a representative comparator for prior prompted LLM studies. Keep the contrast, but label it accurately and avoid suggesting an ablation or “same prompt with/without ARBITER.”

The cost and time gap is material to the comparison. The manuscript reports median 7.6 versus 0.5 minutes per trial and US$3.78 versus US$0.09 in API list-price equivalents. Use these as package-level process costs, not as evidence that the better judgments are cost-effective: the model input, cached tokens, steps, and checks differ. Existing studies also show that prompt design, repeated runs, source availability, and trial-level outcome selection affect performance. A future stronger comparator could give the no-server arm the same RoB 2 instructions and files, then vary only deterministic validation and rating computation. That would answer a component question, but is a different experiment and not required to interpret the current system-level result.

## Three methodological issues informing the OCAR story

OCAR here means Opening, Challenge, Action, Resolution, as defined by Schimel. The three issues below are methodological constraints on that story, not alternative expansions of OCAR. See book-principles.md for the book-based narrative framework.

1. **Objective: preference is not accuracy.** Choi, Eisele-Metzger, Huang, Nyrhi, and Gandhi largely report agreement or accuracy against one chosen reference; Choi also shows a reference transcription can violate the algorithm. ARBITER's 53/60 result asks which label experts prefer only in arm-discordant cells with agreed forced choices. Keep that as the primary objective and call it expert preference. Use independently frozen expert ratings across all cells for agreement, and present published-label agreement as a separate, noisy-comparator analysis. Do not rewrite the primary endpoint as “accuracy” or imply the published label is ground truth.

2. **Comparator: the no-system arm is deliberately minimal.** Prior studies commonly provide RoB 2 rules, structured signalling questions, or implementation guidance; ROBoto2 already retrieves evidence and aggregates via flowcharts. Therefore the current same-model comparison is fair for an incremental **package-level** question under identical source availability, but it does not benchmark ARBITER against best-practice prompting or identify the contribution of each component. Keep the exact comparator definition in the objective and abstract. State that observed differences cannot be attributed to the server alone; any stronger-prompt or component-ablation arm belongs in future validation.

3. **Analysis and reference: disagreement selection plus human uncertainty.** The primary endpoint is conditional on model-arm discordance and expert consensus, and excludes 20/80 discordant cells with split expert choices in its main estimand. The two experts also disagree on 122/510 independent domain ratings, and recent literature documents low or variable RoB 2 reliability; published-label studies do not settle which source is correct. Retain the prespecified conditional estimand, show separate-expert and split-choice sensitivities, report independent-rating and expert-reliability results alongside it, and call the experts a blinded reference process rather than ground truth. Do not turn all cells into a forced-choice comparison or pool away the disagreement. The manuscript already has the needed evidence and Figure 4 structure; this is chiefly a framing discipline.

## Suggested central claims

**Preferred central claim.** In this benchmark, experts preferred the auditable ARBITER workflow over a same-model minimal-prompt run in the subset of cells where their labels differed and both experts chose the same label; independent full-cell comparisons also favored ARBITER, while published-label agreement showed no clear difference. The result supports evaluating workflow performance against independent expert assessments as well as published labels. It is not a general accuracy or component-causality claim.

**Architecture claim.** ARBITER enforces answer validation, evidence-to-source binding, and algorithmic rating computation through a separate deterministic service and retains information for independent review. Label this as an implemented system property. The retrieved quotation count establishes source occurrence, not semantic support.

**Secondary process claim.** The workflow reduced algorithm violations relative to this minimal-prompt run, with 0 recorded ARBITER domain rule breaks versus 65/509 for Unassisted; rules-only recomputation did not erase the independent-rating gap. Choi shows code-based consistency measurement already exists, so the novelty is the enforced path and its evaluation, not the rule-check concept. The 65 comprises 26 ratings contradicting their answers and 39 required-question omissions. One of 510 cells is not evaluable; 509 is the denominator. There is no separate verified 65/510 endpoint in this review.

**Story to avoid.** “First agentic RoB 2 tool,” “first use of code to apply RoB 2 rules,” “first study to check rule violations,” “verified citations prove correctness,” or “better than humans/published truth.” ROBoto2, Choi, Liang, and the known variability of human references contradict or substantially qualify those claims.

## Remaining uncertainties

- The Joseph et al. preprint was only available as an indexed abstract in this pass; its full methods and system behavior remain unverified.
- A claim that no study combines the stated architecture and reference design needs a refreshed, reproducible search through submission date. This targeted comparison is not a systematic review.
- “ARBITER prefers better judgments” needs care because the forced-choice reference permits a split vote and conditional selection, and independent ratings vary. The evidence supports a benchmark-specific preference and agreement comparison, not universal superiority.
- Source quotation occurrence, semantic support, image interpretation, and completeness of the document set are separate properties. The project materials explicitly say image descriptions are not checked against image content, and document availability varied by trial.

## Worker summary

**Verdict: ISSUES.** The result supports a contribution centered on an enforced evidence-linked RoB 2 workflow evaluated against a matched-model minimal-prompt package and blinded independent assessments. ROBoto2, Choi, and Liang directly disprove or narrow claims of first retrieval/aggregation, first code-based algorithm checking, and first RoB evidence verification. Eisele-Metzger and Gandhi show that imperfect published references and blinded human assessments are already recognized in the field. The key remaining distinction is their combination with pre-acceptance answer validation, stored-source binding, deterministic rating computation, and a checkable audit trail. Keep Figures 1 and 2; align the objective, comparison, estimand, and reference language with that bounded claim before changing implementation or manuscript text.
