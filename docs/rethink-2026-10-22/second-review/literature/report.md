# Second review: literature, contribution, and story

**PASS, with a material novelty and comparator qualification.** The system-and-evaluation direction fits JAMIA Research and Applications, but the manuscript should claim a bounded evaluation contribution, not a first auditable or human-in-the-loop RoB 2 system. ARBITER is an installable workflow whose complete Sonnet configuration was compared against the same model under a minimal prompt, with judgment agreement and process properties evaluated separately. This pairing, the full independent expert cohort, and transparent conditional primary endpoint are the credible differentiators. This is an inference from inspected literature and the accepted specification, not an exhaustive search of every tool.

## Overview

Prior work has already established automated RoB assessment, extractive evidence display, interactive LLM-assisted RoB 2 review, algorithm-derived judgments, and multi-model evaluations. Recent papers also report structured-prompt RoB 2 performance and compare model outputs with human or published assessments. Consequently, novelty cannot rest on “auditable,” “evidence-grounded,” “human-centered,” “first LLM RoB 2 workflow,” or the use of the RoB 2 signaling-question algorithm. Those features overlap directly with ROBoto2 and adjacent work.

What appears distinct in this evaluation is the controlled, same-model contrast of one complete installable workflow against that model with a minimal task prompt and the same supplied files; two independent-first expert assessments over all 510 domain cells; and paired, reference-specific reporting alongside procedural conformance checks. Say “we evaluated” these properties, not “we prove” that the workflow is accurate, improves reviewers, or saves time. One run per trial limits generalization to run variability; the contrast bundles instructions, tool use, and computation.

### Main counterarguments to novelty and rationale

1. **“Inspectable evidence already exists.”** ROBoto2 returns supporting evidence and allows human edits; RobotReviewer identifies supportive sentences. ARBITER must show what is materially different in its answer-to-source relationship, audit record, and rule handling. “We preserve a checkable record” is safer than claiming unique inspectability.
2. **“The comparison gives the structured arm more than the comparator.”** Correct. It is the practical system-level contrast the author chose, but it cannot distinguish the server, prompt, extra model effort, or orchestration. The paper must call it a complete-workflow comparison. A future matched structured-prompt control would answer component attribution, but it is outside this evaluation.
3. **“Two experts do not define truth.”** Correct. Two separate references and their agreement are valuable evidence, but not an adjudicated or validated gold standard. The all-cell expert comparison improves on a single consensus label while leaving uncertainty explicit.
4. **“A conditional forced-choice win is selected and constrained.”** Correct. Keep its formal primary status and report 53/60 among 60 agreed choices from 80 arm-discordant cells, with the 20 split-choice cases visible and sensitivity analyses. It is preference conditional on eligibility and choice agreement, not population accuracy or overall performance. The complete independent-reference evidence should carry the explanatory narrative without retroactively changing the prespecified hierarchy.
5. **“Audit checks can create false confidence.”** Correct. Rule-path conformance, source occurrence, and bundle integrity are procedural properties. They are not evidence that the source passage supports the answer, that interpretation was valid, or that the clinical judgment is correct.
6. **“The paper promises practical utility without observing a person using it.”** Avoid claims about human efficiency, saved work, clinical impact, or deployment. Availability and reproducibility are system contributions; this offline benchmark measures recorded model outputs and computational resources.

## Recommended story and display logic

### Four-paragraph introduction argument

1. **Opening.** Risk-of-bias assessments shape how readers interpret trial evidence. RoB 2 makes the judgment outcome- and effect-specific, so a defensible appraisal needs a visible basis. Cite RoB 2 primary guidance. Avoid generic “AI is transforming healthcare” throat-clearing.
2. **Problem.** An automated label alone does not show which trial evidence informed the judgment or how signaling answers map to a domain conclusion. Prior work has automated RoB 1 and developed LLM RoB 2 systems, including ROBoto2. Therefore, frame the unmet need narrowly as a practical, inspectable workflow whose judgment performance and procedural behavior are both evaluated, not a literature-wide absence of tools.
3. **Evaluation gap and question.** Structured prompting, algorithm-derived labels, model comparisons, and human-assisted systems already exist. But the inspected closest work does not answer the study’s chosen question: how does this complete installable Sonnet workflow compare with the same model given a minimal prompt and identical supplied files, when agreement is assessed against two separate independent experts across all available domain cells? State the conditional formal endpoint as an additional prespecified question, not as the only question.
4. **Contribution.** Name ARBITER, explain model interpretation versus software-enforced source/record and RoB 2 rule functions, state the paired design, and preview the bounded findings after final verified numerics are selected. End with a clear purpose: evaluate a usable system and report what agreement, process checks, and reference choice do and do not establish. Mention no alternative-model experiment.

This OCAR structure follows the accepted brief. Schimel’s inspected account of story arc supports an introduction that narrows to a challenge and a discussion that resolves it. Here the resolution is whether the workflow showed closer expert agreement while exposing limits. Heard supports choosing the question before the evidence and using hindsight without rewriting chronology. Zinsser supports one central argument and removing duplicate displays rather than qualifications that change interpretation. These are editorial applications, not quotations.

### Discussion sequence

1. Answer the question with the full-workflow contrast, both expert-specific agreement results, and the conditional primary endpoint, using verified values only.
2. Interpret the uneven domain pattern. Explain D5’s gains and D3’s expert dependence without allowing a pooled estimate to erase either.
3. Place published-label results beside the independent ratings. Call labels an imperfect comparator; state target uncertainty qualitatively, without target-match subgroups.
4. Use diagnostics to explain patterns, not component effects. Preserve five-level and collapsed coding and unfavorable findings. Include a verified vignette only if it explains a systematic result.
5. Separate rule/source/record checks and computational resources from semantic validity and human workflow effects.
6. Close with limits: single runs/model, bundled intervention, two disagreeing experts, selected forced-choice population, possible target mismatch, and no live human-use or evidence-support audit. Name repeated-run, component, semantic-support, and human-use work as future questions.

### Visual and editorial recommendations

Figures 1 and 2 already tell the system and example story and are protected. Their captions should make clear that the example illustrates the record, not representative performance. Figure 3 should explain the paired design and the distinct 80-to-60 conditional endpoint pathway. Figure 4 should carry the reader’s core judgment: expert-specific absolute agreement, paired percentage-point differences, uncertainty, and expert-to-expert context, with a compact full-cohort published-label comparison only if legible. Figure 5 should show systematic diagnostics, not cherry-picked examples. Move the full published-reference display to a sixth figure only if adding it to Figure 4 makes either hard to read.

JAMIA’s current guidance classifies system formulation, implementation, and evaluation as Research and Applications. It permits six figures and four tables, up to 4,000 words and a 250-word structured abstract with five specified headings; it also requires Background and Significance and a Data Availability Statement, with analyses repeatable from archived data. The accepted five-figure/three-table target fits. Recheck guidance at submission. Each table should answer a distinct question rather than repeat figure rows.

Use one stable encoding for both experts across displays, consistent domain order, direct labels, and explicit denominators. Separate absolute rates from paired differences. Preserve the five-level/collapsed distinction and show forced-choice selection steps. Captions should state population, reference, estimand, uncertainty method, and whether the quantity is absolute or paired. These recommendations follow the brief and Heard’s inspected figure-design guidance; exact graph choices depend on finalized data and rendering.

For narrative practice, the [RobotReviewer JAMIA paper](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/23/1/193/2379921) joins system description and evaluation; [ROBoto2](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-demos.2/) joins platform, dataset, and challenges. Their tools differ. Liu et al.’s claim-level annotations help distinguish source presence from support.

## What to change in the current brief

The brief is sound on hierarchy, protected displays, and claim boundaries. Make these edits:

- Narrow novelty to the installable workflow and its same-model complete-workflow evaluation; compare ROBoto2 and RobotReviewer directly. Avoid “first” claims for auditability, evidence display, human review, or rule use.
- Acknowledge recent structured RoB 2 work and algorithm-derived labels. State the specific comparator gap, not a broad absence of tools. Explain that source presence is not semantic evidence support.
- Define the same Sonnet/minimal-prompt/same-files contrast in the abstract. Call it a workflow comparison and avoid component effect language.
- Keep the 53/60 endpoint’s selection pathway visible beside the 510-cell independent-reference evidence. Use current exports, not stale HTML.
- Report runtime and cost only as computational resources. Before submission, resolve the data archive/statement and evaluated-version citation, and provide methods for reproducibility. JAMIA asks authors to disclose related published material for overlap assessment.

The reviewer should remember one sentence: **ARBITER is an available, inspectable RoB 2 workflow that showed greater agreement with two independent expert references than a same-model minimal-prompt workflow in this benchmark, while procedural checks and agreement did not establish clinical correctness or human time savings.** The methods and captions should identify the 510 cells as clustered within 102 trials, separate five domains from derived Overall, name both experts, and keep published-label target uncertainty qualitative.
