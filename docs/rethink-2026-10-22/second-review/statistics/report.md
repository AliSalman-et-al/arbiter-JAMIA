## What the numbers support

The 102 trials come from 16 fixed reviews and contribute 510 trial-domain ratings. Against Expert 1, ARBITER matches 371/510 (72.7%) and Unassisted 332/510 (65.1%), a paired gain of 7.6 percentage points (95% CI 5.1 to 10.2). Against Expert 2, the counts are 433/510 (84.9%) and 400/510 (78.4%), a gain of 6.5 points (3.5 to 9.4). R uses the same trial bootstrap weights for both arms. These are agreement gains against two experts, not accuracy or clinical correctness.

The paired counts help explain those gains. Against Expert 1, ARBITER alone matches in 50 cells, Unassisted alone in 11, both in 321, and neither in 128. Against Expert 2, the counts are 55, 22, 378 and 55. Thus the net gains are 39/510 and 33/510, exactly the observed agreement differences. This is a descriptive partition of already analysed paired outcomes, not another test. It can clarify what “paired” means in one supplement sentence or a small supplement row. It does not need a new main display because the paired differences and intervals already give the inferential summary.

Pooling gives each domain equal nominal weight, not equal clinical importance. Five ratings from a trial are not independent. The bootstrap resamples whole trials within each fixed review and carries all five ratings together. Its percentile intervals do not cover new reviews, experts or repeated runs. There was one run per arm, and these two experts are fixed references. State this scope next to the intervals. Do not use overlap between reference-specific intervals to claim an interaction.

The domain profile makes the pooled contrast interpretable. D5 is the clearest gain: ARBITER agrees with Expert 1 on 75/102 (73.5%) versus 55/102 (53.9%), a paired gain of 19.6 points (11.8 to 27.5); for Expert 2 it is 84/102 (82.4%) versus 72/102 (70.6%), a gain of 11.8 points (2.9 to 20.6). D3 is the caution: ARBITER agrees with Expert 1 on 43/102 (42.2%) and Expert 2 on 80/102 (78.4%). The experts themselves agree on D3 in only 54/102 (52.9%). ARBITER's paired D3 gains are 5.9 points (0.0 to 11.8) and 9.8 points (2.9 to 16.7). Show the two references separately. Do not average them into one judge or present Expert 2's higher match as evidence that Expert 1 is wrong.

Linearly weighted Cohen's kappa adds an ordinal, chance-adjusted view: pooled ARBITER estimates are 0.51 against Expert 1 and 0.69 against Expert 2, with paired coefficient gains of 0.16 against each (95% CIs 0.10 to 0.22 and 0.09 to 0.23). Expert-to-expert pooled exact agreement is 388/510 (76.1%) and weighted kappa is 0.59. The 76.1% is context for reference variability, not a performance ceiling. The agreed-reference subset is selected on expert agreement and is not an independent validation set. Its higher rates must stay secondary.

Show kappa with exact counts and category distributions. Published labels are 427/510 Low (83.7%), 69 Some concerns (13.5%) and 14 High (2.7%); an all-Low rule therefore also matches 427/510 (83.7%). Raw match alone can reward the dominant category. Kappa also depends on prevalence and rater marginals, so keep it secondary. Against Published, ARBITER matches 386/510 (75.7%) versus 395/510 Unassisted (77.5%), paired difference −1.8 points (95% CI −5.1 to 1.6). This imperfect reference comparison proves neither published-label error nor a difference between references.

The primary population is narrower by design. Of 80 cells where the arms disagreed, the experts chose the same label in 60 and split on 20. ARBITER matches the agreed preferred label in 53/60 (88.3%; trial-cluster bootstrap 95% CI 79.7 to 96.3); Unassisted matches in 7/60. Separate-expert sensitivities are 63/80 for Expert 1 and 61/80 for Expert 2. Counting each of the 20 split choices as an ARBITER loss gives 53/80 (66.2%), 7/80 Unassisted and 20/80 neither. State the selection path and keep “preference” or “match to the agreed choice” in the label. The 53/60 is not accuracy over the 510 cells, not a population success rate, and not the rate among all disputed cells. The frozen plan and current results retain this primary contrast; the planned count was an expectation, not the observed result.

Five-level signalling agreement and collapsed agreement are distinct constructs. Collapsing Y/PY and N/PN treats probability differences as matches. Against Expert 1, paired D3 exact-answer agreement is −10.3 points (95% CI −17.2 to −3.2; 165 paired components), while collapsed agreement is +8.5 (3.5 to 13.7). For D5 those are +30.9 (24.8 to 36.7; n=301) and +11.3 (8.2 to 14.6). Against Expert 2, D3 is −1.2 (−9.9 to 8.3; n=164) and +7.9 (2.8 to 13.6); D5 is +7.0 (−1.3 to 15.1; n=300) and +3.7 (−0.3 to 7.4). These paired n values are the common evaluable components for both arms. Source-specific absolute rates have different denominators, so their simple subtraction is not the paired estimate. Keep missing, inactive, not-applicable, unmatched and unresolved components out of the evaluable denominator and show eligibility in the supplement.

The verifier reproduces all 510 ARBITER domain outputs from their answers and the RoB 2 algorithm; the table marks violations as 0 by design because the workflow computes the rating. Unassisted has 26 contradictions and 39 required-question omissions among 509 evaluable cells, 65/509 (12.8%); one cell is not evaluable. This supports conformance, not good evidence interpretation. Rules-only repair changes 26 labels but leaves omissions and invalid paths in place. It is a limited arithmetic check, not a causal ablation. The full contrast also changes instructions, input handling, evidence requirements and computation.

ARBITER has 1,538/1,538 active answers with a passage or image record, 1,407 text spans and 14 image records linked to source locations, and 102/102 bundles that pass the structural verifier. Eight trials use visual evidence. These counts show record coverage, not that a passage entails an answer or the judgment is correct. “Not available” for Unassisted means no audit record, not zero evidence.

Derived Overall stays secondary because each expert's Overall was calculated from their five domain ratings rather than rated as an independent sixth decision. For the 102 trials, ARBITER matches Expert 1 on 39/102 and Unassisted on 27/102, a paired gain of 11.8 points (95% CI 4.9 to 18.6); against Expert 2 the rates are 72/102 and 57/102, a gain of 14.7 points (5.9 to 22.5). Only 81 trials have an observed Published Overall. On that subset, ARBITER matches Expert 1 on 32/81 versus 21/81 (gain 13.6 points, 6.2 to 21.0), and Expert 2 on 60/81 versus 46/81 (17.3 points, 8.6 to 25.9). The 21 missing Published Overall judgments are omitted, not scored as errors. Keep all these estimates in the supplement or a compact secondary result, separate from domain-level primary explanatory evidence.

Resource estimates describe the evaluated runs. Median system runtime is 7.6 minutes per trial (IQR 6.1 to 10.5) for ARBITER and 0.5 (0.4 to 0.5) for Unassisted. The paired mean difference is 11.0 minutes (95% CI 8.4 to 14.1), not the difference between those medians. API list-price equivalents are $3.78 (IQR $3.46 to $4.10) and $0.09 ($0.07 to $0.13) per trial, with paired mean difference $3.68 ($3.61 to $3.75). Label the summary statistics and API estimate explicitly. They are not invoice costs, human review time, cost-effectiveness, or evidence of reviewer time saved.

## Book principles and reviewer impression

I checked the local HTML books rather than relying on `book-principles.md` alone. Schimel's *Writing Science*, chapters 4 and 9, defines resolution as what changed after the action. The conclusion should say that an inspectable workflow improved expert agreement in this benchmark while showing remaining disagreement. Heard's *The Scientist's Guide to Writing*, chapter 7, advises selecting essential figures and rounding to useful precision. This supports five figures, three tables, one decimal place for percentages and paired effects, and two decimals for κ. Zinsser's *On Writing Well*, chapters 2, 3 and 8, argues for one organizing idea and removing material that does no work. Here the organizing idea is an installable, auditable system with a bounded evaluation.

These are presentation recommendations, not results. The proposed figure set is not yet rendered, so I cannot judge its actual typography, color contrast, label density or JAMIA page-size legibility. If Figure 4 becomes cramped when it combines two expert comparisons and a Published-reference panel, move the Published comparison to a sixth figure or the supplement rather than compressing estimates and denominators.
