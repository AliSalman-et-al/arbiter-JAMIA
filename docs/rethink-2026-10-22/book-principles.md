## Applications

1. **Schimel: the resolution changes understanding.** The conclusion needs to answer the larger problem introduced at the opening. A list of favorable agreement coefficients is an action/result, not a resolution. Candidate resolution: a checkable assessment workflow can improve agreement with independent experts, but procedural guarantees and interpretive validity require separate evaluation.
2. **Schimel: OCAR is a causal sequence of ideas.** Opening establishes why accountable evidence appraisal matters. Challenge identifies the uncertainty about obtaining judgments that are both inspectable and defensible. Action describes the system and matched evaluation. Resolution states what this evaluation establishes, including its boundary.
3. **Heard: write the central question and answer before the outline.** Draft a two-sentence question/answer pair, choose evidence that answers it, and only then decide the displays. Do not preserve an analysis in the main text merely because it already has a figure.
4. **Heard: hindsight can improve exposition.** Reorganizing the story after seeing results is different from retroactively describing new analyses as prespecified. Preserve the dated plan and describe amendments accurately.
5. **Zinsser: unity requires a chosen scope.** The system contribution, reference-standard lesson, and human workflow usefulness are different potential organizing ideas. Choose one main claim. Other findings either support it, qualify it, or belong in the supplement.
6. **Zinsser: teach in steps.** Figures 1 and 2 already establish the division between model interpretation and software checks. Later displays should build on that understanding, introducing one scientific question at a time.
7. **All three: simplification is structural.** Moving duplicate estimates out of the main text is more effective than shortening captions around an overloaded figure. Preserve unfavorable and qualifying evidence; simplify repetition, not the scientific record.

## Application after decisions Q1–Q8

The author reiterated that these books must guide the session. Apply them to architecture, not merely sentence polish:

- **Schimel:** the opening's need for inspectable, defensible appraisal returns in the resolution. The result is a concrete tool that preserves an inspectable record, with a bounded evaluation showing closer expert agreement and revealing difficult judgments. A high forced-choice percentage alone cannot resolve that larger problem. Reference choice is an interpretive turn within this story, not a competing paper about defective published reviews.
- **Heard:** give each main display a necessary question. Architecture explains the intervention; the worked trace makes it concrete; design explains what was compared; performance establishes the size and limits of the benefit; diagnostics make the remaining disagreements intelligible. Formal endpoint reporting can be clear and prominent in a table without owning the entire narrative.
- **Zinsser:** enforce unity by reporting the Sonnet workflow only, as the author directed. Remove the main-text literature checklist and duplicate metric displays. Keep the empirical qualifications that change interpretation. A compact supplement should be organized by reader questions, not by the order analyses were written.

Working resolution, for later prose refinement: ARBITER makes the basis of an AI-assisted assessment available for inspection and showed closer agreement with independent experts in this benchmark. Software checks provide reproducible procedural safeguards; difficult evidence judgments remain visible and require expert scrutiny. The evaluation reference influences what one concludes about performance.

A proposed target of five figures and three tables leaves room for this progression. Readability takes precedence over an arbitrary count: a sixth figure is preferable to an overcrowded performance display. Do not use an allowed maximum as a reason to add another analysis.
