## Estimands and denominator audit

The frozen 18 October plan says it was fixed after data collection and includes expected adjudication counts. The later brief says it preceded the final expert ratings. The chronology may involve different assessment stages and needs reconciliation before making timing claims. The frozen plan labels signalling agreement exploratory and locates independent expert comparisons in disputed cells; the current plan applies comparisons to all 510 cells and describes several categories differently. Preserve the historical record and distinguish analysis specification, population amendments and timing. The original plan also proposed a composite reference with incorporation and selection limitations; Round 16 instead uses the full independent ratings and conditional forced choice.

The primary forced-choice mechanics are correctly separated in `analysis/R/expert-agreement.R` and the frozen `primary-endpoint.csv`:

- 510 D1–D5 cells were assessed by each expert.
- 154 were disputed, meaning at least one Sonnet arm differs from Published.
- 80 were discordant, meaning the two Sonnet arms differ. This is the eligible primary population; it is not 154.
- The experts agreed on their chosen label in 60 of these 80 cells. The consensus primary estimate is 53/60 ARBITER (88.3%; cluster-bootstrap 95% CI 79.7–96.3), 7/60 Unassisted, 0/60 neither.
- Each expert separately made 80 choices: Expert 1 selected the ARBITER label in 63/80, Unassisted in 13/80, and neither in 4/80. Expert 2 selected ARBITER in 61/80 and Unassisted in 19/80.
- In the split-choice sensitivity, the 20 split cells are ARBITER losses: 53/80 ARBITER, 7/80 Unassisted, 20/80 neither. This conservative sensitivity is not another consensus estimate.
- The manuscript's 53/60 is supported. Avoid describing the primary result as 53/154 or as a preference over the whole benchmark. The offered labels constrain this preference task, and neither arm gets credit when the chosen label matches neither.

The independent-reference estimand has a different denominator and answers a broader question. The workbook contains an independent rating for both experts in every 510 D1–D5 cell. The sources agree with Expert 1 in ARBITER 371/510 and Unassisted 332/510, and with Expert 2 in ARBITER 433/510 and Unassisted 400/510. Paired agreement differences are 7.6 percentage points (95% CI 5.1–10.2) and 6.5 points (3.5–9.4). Experts agree with one another on 388/510 (76.1%; linearly weighted κ 0.59); this is a reliability yardstick, not a ceiling. These all-cell contrasts are not forced choices and should not be conflated with the 60-cell primary denominator.

For Overall, the workbooks derive Overall from each source's five domain ratings. Published Overall is absent for 21 of 102 trials, leaving n=81. The current analysis compares ARBITER and Unassisted over 102 when comparing to expert-derived Overall, also reports the matched n=81 comparisons, and excludes absent Published labels instead of counting them wrong. This denominator discipline is correct. However, derived Overall is exploratory and is not a direct expert Overall judgment.

Rules-only recomputation replaces Unassisted labels that conflict with the official algorithm applied to Unassisted answers; it preserves the original arm-discordant set. The stored output says 26 domain labels change, including 6 originally discordant cells. Primary consensus matches change from 7/60 to 8/60 Unassisted, with one neither. Independent-rating agreement drops from 332/510 to 324/510 against Expert 1 and from 400/510 to 391/510 against Expert 2. This tests arithmetic repair while holding the Unassisted answers fixed. It does not isolate the causal effect of ARBITER's rule engine, because ARBITER also changes prompts, input handling, answer validation and evidence requirements. State that rules-only repair did not account for the observed advantage in this data; do not treat it as a component attribution experiment.

## Binding and reference limitations

`LABEL-TARGETS.csv` has 105 benchmark-level binding classifications: 33 same, 33 same inferred, 11 one-rating-covering-several-outcomes, 10 unknown, 7 partly, 2 partly inferred, 5 different and 4 different inferred. These are benchmark counts, including the three excluded designs; the 102-trial count was not recomputed in this audit. The data README says 43/105 targets moved away from the primary outcome when the meta-rated result differed. The Lancet clopidogrel note documents a concrete high-risk mismatch: trial-level meta ratings are shown without a clear result tie, the analysis targets long follow-up IPD MACCE and includes subsets/time horizons unavailable in trial reports, and the paper's method pages are paywalled. That subset's labels cannot be treated as verified result-specific ground truth for the arm target.

The current manuscript discloses label binding uncertainty and notes a De Waal registry identity mismatch. Keep that caveat near published-label results. The existing `LABEL-TARGETS.csv` structure can support a future stratified display of exact, inferred, partial/different, and unknown binding, but the current Round 16 brief expressly says not to add a label-mismatch sensitivity or emphasize aggregate counts. For this reconsideration, retain the constraint: disclose the boundary, do not create an unplanned subgroup or imply all 510 Published comparisons evaluate the exact same result.

The two expert sheets show independent, forced-choice and revised ratings for all 510 cells and signalling detail for both experts. Since the experts could consult sources beyond the model inputs, they are a useful independent expert reference but not an input-matched ground truth. The forced-choice triad includes Published, ARBITER and Unassisted in random order and gives no rationale, but source blinding success is not established from the files alone. The manuscript properly says their ratings were frozen before label exposure and avoids a universal accuracy claim.

## Rules, answer and output-family checks

The loader enforces 102 trials and 510 unique expert-domain rows and verifies workbook labels against arm exports. `domain-algorithm` transcribes the 2019 official RoB 2 logic; D2 uses effect of assignment; invalid required paths are reported as rule breaks; wholly missing answers are separately not evaluable. The manuscript and Round 16 report record ARBITER proposal validation and full 102-bundle structural verification. Unassisted has 65/509 evaluable domain cells with a rule break (12.8%). One Unassisted cell is not evaluable. Direct CSV counts are 444 consistent, 26 ratings contradicting answers, 39 required questions skipped and 1 not evaluable; the first three sum to 509. These numbers support the narrow claim that the ARBITER workflow enforces the specified decision rules and that the comparator sometimes departs from them. The 1,407/1,407 quoted strings found in stored sources establish occurrence only. They do not establish that the quote entails or justifies the answer.

The four arm answer files have 2,266 ARBITER-Sonnet rows across 103 trials, 2,235 Unassisted-Sonnet rows across 102, 2,222 ARBITER-Haiku rows across 101, and 2,202 Unassisted-Haiku rows across 102. Each complete trial has 22 question rows. Unassisted-Sonnet has one 23-row trial (`attal-2021`) and one 12-row trial (`cadet`); Unassisted-Haiku has uneven 18–23 rows per trial. No stored answer field is blank, but absent question rows and `not_applicable` or inactive answers are different forms of non-evaluability. The analysis plan says comparisons retain only explicit, aligned, active, evaluable question and component pairs and export eligibility counts. That is the right denominator rule; answer completeness should not be summarized by a single all-question percentage.

Output families in `analysis/output/tables/` cover forced choice and independent references; published-label agreement and confusion; Overall and domain consistency; signalling match eligibility, parse issues and answer changes; rules-only changes; binding and benchmark documents; provenance traces; bundles; evidence and quotation counts; time, tokens and cost; and report/display versions of tables and supplements. `analysis/data-derived/` contains four arm-level scoring extracts, two run-metric tables, API trial/turn data, expert signalling component/comparison files, bundle binding/path/reverification data, and AVATAR trace/rationale JSON. `analysis/output/manuscript/` holds Figure 1–6 and eFigure 1–5 publication files, Tables 1–4, Supplement, checklist, legends and results sentences. ## Literature verification for the story

- The RoB 2 primary methods paper states that assessments target a single outcome/result and asks assessors to specify the numerical result. The full BMJ article was accessible. This supports treating result binding as a design criterion, rather than viewing all label disagreement as model error. [RoB 2 original methods paper](https://www.bmj.com/content/366/bmj.l4898).
- The original Huang JMIR paper was accessible in full. It used structured prompts, independent reviewer assessments and derived domain judgments from model answers. Its reported derived ratings improved agreement in some domains. This is directly relevant to the comparator: ARBITER versus a minimal prompt estimates a whole workflow difference, not a tool-only effect against an equivalently guided model. [Huang et al., JMIR 2025](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e70450/).
- The original ROBoto2 conference paper and full text were accessible. It already combines LLM signaling answers with code-computed RoB 2 judgments and human review. Avoid a broad novelty claim that ARBITER is the first to compute RoB 2 judgments in code; focus the distinction on constrained answer acceptance, source-bound quote checks before acceptance, and independently verifiable audit bundles, if comparisons substantiate those differences. [Hevia et al., ACL Anthology 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-demos.2/).
- The original TRIPOD-LLM consensus paper was accessible in full and describes a 19-item, 50-subitem reporting checklist for LLM studies. It supports completing the checklist, not the study's effectiveness claims. [Gallifant et al., Nature Medicine 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03425-5).
- The original Gandhi PLOS One paper was accessible in full. Its masked expert panel rated the 50-trial benchmark, making it a precedent for an expert reference in this literature. It differs from this project because its panel rated every trial rather than only selected disagreement cells; it does not remove the current system's binding or comparator constraints. [Gandhi et al., PLOS One 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0353155).
- Nyrhi et al. is in the local literature synthesis, but I could not retrieve the publisher full text in this check. Treat its interpretation and details as unverified here; do not rely on the local summary alone for a submission claim. [Nyrhi et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2026.106238).

## Recommended lean analysis architecture

Keep a four-layer Results story, with Figures 1 and 2 left intact.

1. **System and benchmark.** Keep Figure 1's architecture and Figure 2's worked trace. State 105 trials, exclusions, 102 supported trials, source/result-binding caveat and 510 independently assessed domain cells. Report expert reliability once, as context for interpreting reference-based comparisons.
2. **Primary preference result.** Lead with 53/60 on consensus choices among 80 discordant cells, its trial-cluster interval, and the predeclared expert-specific and split-choice sensitivities. State plainly that forced choice is conditional on arm disagreement and the three labels offered.
3. **Full-cohort independent ratings and Published reference.** Show each expert separately, paired differences, weighted κ and exact agreement across all 510 cells; put Published in a parallel column, identify result-binding limitations, and keep the 21 absent Overall labels out of Published denominators. The parallel references reveal that Published gives little separation while experts favor ARBITER. Keep conditional preference and cohort-wide independent agreement distinct.
4. **Mechanism and implementation cost.** In a compact secondary block, show answer-level comparison, rule departures, and rules-only recomputation as limited arithmetic evidence. Follow with verifiable evidence checks, time and API-equivalent cost. Retain the semantic support caveat.

Do not make Haiku the central story. If the manuscript scope expands to use all four arms, add a separate model-by-workflow table of descriptive D1–D5 counts/agreement, explicitly display missing outputs, and say the expert forced-choice/reference pipeline was built only for Sonnet. Those data cannot retroactively support the primary Sonnet endpoint. Keep derived Overall, directional subsets and rules-only repair exploratory. No analysis build or output mutation is warranted before the authors agree on the revised story.

## Decision and follow-up

The core conclusion survives with narrower wording: ARBITER-Sonnet's complete workflow was preferred over Unassisted-Sonnet on the agreed forced choices and agreed more often with each expert across all cells, while the Published-label benchmark did not distinguish the arms. It is a system-level result within this benchmark. The study does not establish isolated rule-engine effects, clinical correctness, outcome-wide validity, human–AI team benefit, or superiority to a structured-prompt baseline.

Before implementation, resolve these items in the discussion:

1. Keep 53/60 as the primary denominator and explain the 80 discordant, 60 consensus and 20 split relationship in one compact flow.
2. Treat the all-cell independent reference as complementary evidence, not as a forced-choice denominator or a gold standard.
3. Keep the published-label result-binding caveat adjacent to its comparison and avoid interpreting disagreement as error where binding is uncertain.
4. Describe rules-only recomputation as a narrow arithmetic repair check and avoid component attribution.
5. Keep Haiku omitted from the headline unless the manuscript explicitly broadens scope and labels Haiku analyses descriptive.
6. Verify citation details and full text for any prior-work claim before submission; Nyrhi remains unverified in this audit.

No data, analysis, manuscript, glossary or artwork files were changed. This report is the only owned artifact.
