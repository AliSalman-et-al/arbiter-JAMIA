## Key sources (8–20)

1. **Hadgu A. The discrepancy in discrepant analysis.** *Lancet*. 1996;348:592–593. PMID 8774575; DOI [10.1016/S0140-6736(96)05122-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(96)05122-7). Full text: no (abstract). Explains that resolving only tests that disagree with the candidate test biases performance estimates upward, even if resolution is perfect. The estimand here is different from diagnostic sensitivity/specificity, but the design warning applies directly to selected-cell win share.

2. **Hadgu A. Discrepant analysis: a biased and an unscientific method for estimating test sensitivity and specificity.** *J Clin Epidemiol*. 1999;52:1231–1237. DOI [10.1016/S0895-4356(99)00101-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0895-4356(99)00101-8). Full text: abstract/references only. Methodological argument against using test results under evaluation to select which cases receive the reference assessment. This is the strongest classic citation for the concern that conditioning on a disagreement creates favorable selection.

3. **Lipman HB, Astles JR. Quantifying the bias associated with use of discrepant analysis.** *Clin Chem*. 1998;44:108–115. PMID 9550567. DOI [10.1093/clinchem/44.1.108](https://doi.org/10.1093/clinchem/44.1.108). Full text: abstract only. Shows bias is usually positive (inflated performance) and its direction/magnitude depend on candidate, comparator and resolver error rates and prevalence. This cautions against claiming a universal numeric correction for ARBITER win share.

4. **Miller WC. Can we do better than discrepant analysis for new diagnostic test evaluation?** *Clin Infect Dis*. 1998;27:1186–1193. PMID 9827267; DOI [10.1086/514996](https://doi.org/10.1086/514996). Full text: no (PubMed record and cited editorial response). Directly addresses alternatives to discrepant analysis and underscores that its apparent advantage can be manufactured by resolving only disagreements. Supports separating reference determination from the candidate test result.

5. **Naaktgeboren CA, et al. Value of composite reference standards in diagnostic research.** *BMJ*. 2013;347:f5605. PMID 24162938; DOI [10.1136/bmj.f5605](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.f5605). Full text: yes. Composite standards can be practical but may misclassify; their use may over- or underestimate candidate accuracy, and their apparent performance varies with prevalence. They make a fixed classification and suppress uncertainty in the component evidence. Our consensus-plus-Published rule is a composite reference, so its agreement metrics describe agreement with that rule, not truth.

6. **Dendukuri N, et al. Concerns about composite reference standards in diagnostic research.** *BMJ*. 2018;360:j5779. DOI [10.1136/bmj.j5779](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.j5779). Full text: yes. Shows adding tests to a composite does not guarantee better classification and can worsen reference accuracy; latent models that represent uncertainty/dependence are an alternative. Not a direct numerical model for ordinal RoB judgments, but a direct warning against over-interpreting the composite score.

7. **Bertens LCM, et al. Use of expert panels to define the reference standard in diagnostic research: a systematic review of published methods and reporting.** *PLoS Med*. 2013;10:e1001531. PMID 24143138; DOI [10.1371/journal.pmed.1001531](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001531). Full text: yes. Documents substantial variation and incomplete reporting in panel construction, information access, independence, and resolution. A panel diagnosis is a defensible reference process only when the panel procedure and inputs are reported; “expert consensus” alone does not establish validity.

8. **de Groot JAH, et al. Latent Class Models in Diagnostic Studies When There is No Reference Standard—A Systematic Review.** *Am J Epidemiol*. 2014;179:423–431. DOI [10.1093/aje/kwt286](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwt286). Full text: abstract. Latent class models estimate test properties by treating status as unobserved and combining multiple tests, but depend on identifiability and assumptions such as conditional independence and stable test accuracy. Not a plug-in rescue for this small, dependent, ordinal adjudication dataset.

9. **Collins J, Huynh M. Estimation of diagnostic test accuracy without full verification: a review of latent class methods.** *Stat Med*. 2014;33:4141–4169. DOI [10.1002/sim.6218](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.6218). Full text: yes. Reviews LCA for imperfect/incomplete verification. Relevant lesson: estimates can be obtained without declaring any test perfect, but they require enough independent indicators, identifiable structure, and sensitivity checks over dependence assumptions. Here ARBITER and Unassisted share model family, input corpus, task and rule system; the adjudicators see the same evidence and labels. Conditional independence is implausible, while only 154 selected cells have panel data.

10. **Kohn MA. Studies of diagnostic test accuracy: Partial verification bias and test result-based sampling.** *J Clin Epidemiol*. 2022;145:179–182. PMID 35124189; DOI [10.1016/j.jclinepi.2022.01.022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2022.01.022). Full text: abstract. Recommended design: verify all positives and a random sample of negatives, then account for the negative sampling fraction. This maps closely to a random audit of the currently unadjudicated concordant cells.

11. **Whiting PF, et al. A systematic review classifies sources of bias and variation in diagnostic test accuracy studies.** *J Clin Epidemiol*. 2013;66:1093–1104. DOI [10.1016/j.jclinepi.2013.05.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2013.05.014). Full text: yes. Review found consistent evidence of effects from reference-standard choice and partial/differential verification; direction varies by mechanism and is not captured by a blanket correction.

## What this literature says about our story

### Supports

- The Published labels are an imperfect comparator. The design’s purpose-built, blinded human assessment of disputed cells is more informative about these disagreements than automatically treating meta-analysis labels as error-free. This follows the reference-standard literature (Miller; Naaktgeboren) and is consonant with the broader RoB field’s existing acknowledgement of reference noise described in `docs/research/llm-rob2/SYNTHESIS.md`.
- Independent first-pass judgments before the offered-label choice are a meaningful safeguard against direct label anchoring. Dual adjudication also allows reporting inter-adjudicator reliability and per-adjudicator estimates. It does not remove anchoring at the later forced-choice stage or make consensus a gold standard.
- Adjudicating only disputed cells is an efficient design precedent (Gartlehner), appropriate for the primary *conditional* question: among the cells on which these arms disagree and at least one differs from Published, which offered label do adjudicators prefer?

### Complicates

- Disagreement-conditioned win share is not population accuracy. Because adjudication inclusion is determined using model outputs and Published, the 54/62-type comparison is selected on the very labels being compared. Classic discrepant-analysis literature shows this can favor candidate/comparator performance and that the magnitude depends on error dependence. There is no valid generic subtraction from the observed share.
- The composite reference structurally incorporates the arms’ labels in deciding which cells to adjudicate, and uses Published on non-adjudicated cells. The two sources of error can point in different directions: on adjudicated cases, forced choice may favor one of the three offered labels; on concordant cases, shared model errors survive as “correct” by construction.
- “Concordant with Published” is not random missingness. It selects cells on the joint outputs; model errors are likely correlated because the arms share model family and input evidence, and both may share the same direction of error. Therefore ordinary complete-case reasoning is not justified.
- The consensus target is a human decision process. Disagreement between experts, reliance on the same source material, and reliance on the same RoB framework mean panel errors are plausible and correlated with model errors. Reporting consensus as “truth” would overstate what the design establishes.

### Threatens

- If the manuscript presents 54/62 as “accuracy” or general superiority, Hadgu/Lipman’s selection argument is a direct reviewer objection. Describe it narrowly as *adjudicator-preferred label share among adjudicated discordant cells*.
- If composite-reference κ/accuracy are presented as unbiased estimates, the composite-standard critiques and incorporation/verification bias are direct objections. Keep the planned caveats prominent and do not place composite accuracy in the abstract.
- No method using only the 154 selected cells can identify the error rate among concordant-but-unadjudicated cells without assumptions. A random audit is needed to estimate that component empirically.

## Reviewer objections we should pre-empt

1. **“This is discrepant analysis: adjudicating only cases where the systems disagree with the Published label makes ARBITER look good.”** The analogy is strongest for any general accuracy claim and weaker for the deliberately conditional win-share estimand. Hadgu and Lipman show selection can inflate apparent performance, often favoring the candidate, with magnitude dependent on error rates and dependence. Our inclusion set is the union of ARBITER–Published and Unassisted–Published disagreements; primary win share further restricts to arm-discordant cells and consensus. State these denominators exactly. Do not call the endpoint accuracy or estimate performance over all 510 cells from it.

2. **“The win share is guaranteed to favor one system because adjudicators chose among the offered labels.”** Yes, the choice is constrained and cannot discover a fourth judgment. The independent pre-choice rating reduces but does not eliminate anchoring. Analyze the independent ratings separately (already planned), and report forced-choice results by adjudicator and consensus. Add a sensitivity scoring the pre-choice independent judgment as an unrestricted reference, using each adjudicator independently; explain that adjudicator reliability is not ground truth. Blind labels and randomized label order are important—verify and report whether order was randomized/counterbalanced; if fixed, disclose it.

3. **“The composite reference is circular and assumes concordant systems are right.”** Correct. In 485 retained cells, consensus is used only for selected disagreements and Published is assigned to unadjudicated cases, including cells where both systems agree with Published. This predictably inflates absolute agreement relative to a reference that can detect shared errors. The paired difference may be less affected because both systems are scored on identical cells, but the direction is not guaranteed: shared correctness assumptions affect absolute metrics; differential errors and the selective consensus subset can move the paired contrast either way.

4. **“Partial verification bias makes estimates unusable.”** It invalidates an unconditional accuracy interpretation unless selection is modeled or a sample of unverified cells is checked. It does not invalidate the descriptive conditional estimand. The appropriate repair is random verification of a known-probability sample from concordant cells, stratified by key output pattern, then weighted estimates (Horvitz–Thompson/IPW or poststratification) for the full population. Kohn 2022 describes this sampling logic.

5. **“Differential verification: cells use expert consensus or meta labels.”** True: the composite applies different reference procedures to different cells, and procedure assignment depends on disagreement. The direction is not generally predictable (Whiting et al.). Present metrics as agreement with a specified composite rule, not interchangeable with validated accuracy. The concordant-cell audit would give human review to a probability sample and permit an empirically anchored correction/sensitivity analysis.

6. **“Why not latent class analysis?”** LCA is a principled alternative when enough conditionally informative measurements and defensible dependence structure exist. It is not automatically superior: here the two LLM arms and Published label have correlated errors; expert judgments are also conditioned on the same evidence; the key panel observations are selectively observed; outcome is ordinal; and the sample/pattern counts are limited. An LCA on current data would be weakly identified and assumption-driven. Could fit only as exploratory sensitivity analysis after a random audit, with conditional-dependence models and prior sensitivity, not as the primary neutralizer.

7. **“Consensus/adjudication is itself biased or unreliable.”** Panel diagnosis literature shows methods and inputs vary, and consensus can be affected by conformity, vocal dominance, and shared expertise. The current independent-first, dual-rater sequence is a strength. Report independent agreement and each adjudicator’s standalone results; retain disagreements as uncertainty rather than forcing a consensus in sensitivity analyses. For a new audit, keep reviewers blind to arm identity and Published label, let each give a free independent label first, and have a third reviewer resolve only after preserving both first ratings.

8. **“Could agreement between the two models validate the concordant cells?”** No. Agreement is not independent confirmation, particularly for same-family systems with identical documents. The most important unmeasured cell type is shared error: both systems assign the same label, and both differ from the true expert assessment. Only an audit sample can estimate its frequency.

## Recommended analysis directions for future rounds

1. **MUST — Tighten the estimand language using existing data.** Label the primary result “adjudicator-preferred label share among consensus-adjudicated arm-discordant cells that were eligible because at least one arm differed from Published.” Give numerator and denominator, the neither share, both adjudicators separately, split-cell pessimistic bound, and cluster-bootstrap CI. Explicitly say it estimates conditional preference in the selected subset, not population accuracy. No new data.

2. **MUST — Report the existing independent judgments as a parallel reference analysis.** For each adjudicator, report the pre-choice rating against ARBITER and Unassisted on all eligible adjudicated cells, including arm-discordant and arm-concordant-but-Published-discordant cells. Show raw counts, direction/severity, and paired difference with the planned trial-cluster bootstrap. This tests the forced-choice anchoring concern, though the sample remains selected. No new data.

3. **MUST — Quantify how much of composite results comes from unadjudicated cells.** Decompose the composite metrics into: (a) adjudicated consensus cells and (b) cells assigned Published, with counts and arm-wise agreement in each. Give an explicit “consensus-cell-only” paired comparison alongside the composite result. This exposes the mechanical Published contribution. No new data.

4. **MUST — New small audit of the concordant cells.** Draw a reproducible random sample from all D1–D5 cells where both Sonnet arms match Published (and, separately, the other arm-concordant patterns among the current 154 disputed cells if any remain). Use stratified sampling by domain, Published severity, and model label/severity direction; retain nonzero known inclusion probability for every stratum. A practical starting design is 10–20% per stratum or a fixed 60–100 cells overall, then size from desired CI precision after seeing stratum counts. Have two independent blinded experts review the source packet without seeing any of the three labels; third-party adjudication can resolve disagreement after first ratings are locked. Estimate shared-error prevalence and accuracy with sampling weights and cluster-bootstrap trials. This directly tests the assumption that dominates composite absolute estimates.

5. **SHOULD — Derive a transparent bias interval for the existing composite.** Let `q` be the weighted audit estimate of the proportion of presumed-correct concordant cells that are actually not the Published/model-shared label. Recompute each arm’s metrics under audit-derived labels; for unsampled cells, use stratum-specific uncertainty bounds (all correct vs. error at the observed rate or its CI limits). Report the range in absolute accuracy and paired difference. Without audit, show worst/best-case bounds over plausible shared-error rates (e.g., 0%, 5%, 10%, 20%) as a sensitivity plot/table, clearly labeled assumption-based; do not pick one as a correction.

6. **SHOULD — Test forced-choice anchoring with the current independent-first data.** Cross-tab each adjudicator’s own pre-choice judgment against the subsequently selected label and against each offered label. Among cells where own rating differs from choice, report which model label was selected. If labels were not randomized, the data cannot estimate label-order effects; an audit round should randomize/counterbalance label order and include a genuinely free-text/other category.

7. **COULD — Exploratory latent-class model after audit.** Consider an ordinal latent-variable model with adjudicators’ independent ratings, Published labels, and both system labels, including local dependence between the two systems and sensitivity to dependence between adjudicators and systems. Current selected-only data are inadequate for credible identification. Treat as exploratory; report identifiability, priors/constraints, posterior predictive checks and alternate dependence assumptions.

8. **COULD — Do not use unweighted estimates from an enriched sample as population metrics.** If additional verification oversamples severity or disagreement strata, use known sampling probabilities and cluster-aware variance. The audit is within 102 trials, so resample trials (within meta-analysis as planned), not cells independently.

### How biased are the present estimates, and in which direction?

- **Win share:** numerical bias relative to full-population preference is not identified from current data. Conditioning on arm disagreement and Published disagreement creates discrepant-analysis/verification selection. If systems have correlated errors, selection can preferentially expose cases where one system departs from Published and the adjudicators can recognize that departure; classic methods predict apparent performance is commonly inflated, but the direction for *ARBITER-minus-Unassisted* is indeterminate because relative error dependence and selection by either arm both matter. Therefore 54/62 is a valid conditional descriptive share, not a corrected population win probability. The observed consensus share is about 87% (54/62), but cannot be assigned a numeric bias amount.
- **Composite absolute agreement/accuracy:** expected upward bias for both systems relative to a full expert reference because two mechanisms privilege the system labels: (i) forced choice prevents “neither offered label” responses and (ii) concordant-with-Published cells are imputed as correct. The second effect is exactly the weighted prevalence of shared errors among those presumed-correct cells, propagated through the chosen metric; that prevalence is unknown, so the absolute inflation cannot be numerically quantified today. The paired arm contrast is not guaranteed upward or downward: shared-error cells may cancel when arms agree, but they remain in the denominator and severity-weighted κ/AC2 can shift with where the errors occur.
- **Selection among adjudicated cells:** selection does not mechanically make ARBITER win every time; it can raise either arm’s apparent agreement. Because selection is the union of either arm’s Published disagreement and the primary endpoint conditions further on arm disagreement, an exact direction cannot be inferred from counts alone.
- **Neutralization:** the strongest repair is a probability audit of concordant cells, with blinded independent ratings before any label exposure and design-weighted estimates. Existing independent judgments, split-cell and per-adjudicator analyses, plus composite decomposition, answer part of the concern but cannot estimate unseen concordant shared error.

## Recommended text changes

**Methods.** “We adjudicated cells on which at least one model differed from the published review label. Adjudicators first rated each cell independently, before seeing the three blinded candidate labels, and then recorded a preferred label from those candidates. Accordingly, the primary endpoint estimates the adjudicators’ preferred-label share among eligible, adjudicated cells where the models disagreed; it is not an estimate of accuracy over all trial-domain cells.”

**Methods (composite).** “For a secondary descriptive comparison, we assigned the consensus adjudicator judgment to cells with concordant adjudicator judgments and the published label to all other eligible domain cells; split-adjudicator cells were omitted. This composite assigns published labels to cells not independently adjudicated, including cells where both models agree with the published label.”

**Limitations.** “The adjudication sample was selected using model disagreement with the published labels, and adjudicators could choose only among the three offered labels. The primary win share is therefore conditional on this selected sample and may be affected by discrepant-resolution and anchoring biases. The composite reference assumes that unadjudicated concordant cells are correct; correlated errors shared by the two model arms could inflate absolute agreement. We did not audit these cells, so the magnitude of this inflation is unknown. A probability sample of concordant cells with blinded independent review is needed to estimate it.”

**Discussion.** “The adjudicated result supports a narrow conclusion: in the selected cells where the two systems differed, adjudicators more often preferred ARBITER’s offered label. It does not establish that ARBITER is more accurate across all cells. The agreement values against our composite should be interpreted as descriptive because the reference includes published labels for unadjudicated cells and the adjudicators’ forced-choice judgments for selected cells.”

Do not say discrepant analysis proves the win-share estimate is upward biased by a known percentage; that literature concerns accuracy estimands and shows magnitude depends on test and resolver properties. Do not call panel consensus ground truth. Do not report composite accuracy as unconditional “accuracy.”
