# Structured prompts and algorithmic RoB 2 judgements

## Huang et al 2025, Journal of Medical Internet Research: LLM-assisted RoB 2

- **DOI / IDs**: 10.2196/70450; PMC12238788; PMID 40554779.
- **Full text read**: yes; Europe PMC full-text XML. Multimedia Appendix 1 is cited for the final prompt and full outputs; the XML identifies a DOCX supplement, but the downloadable attachment was not readable in this retrieval. Multimedia Appendix 2 contains supplementary signalling-question accuracy (image). The paper's main-text methods/results and tables were read.
- **Design**: Feasibility/validation study, not reported as preregistered. Prompt refined iteratively on 6 RCTs; 40 remaining trials were internal validation. Published Cochrane ratings were external comparison. Three trained reviewers independently rated all 46, then reconciled in consensus.
- **Sample**: 46 RCTs randomly selected from 86 eligible Cochrane reviews (1,399 RCTs): 23 focused on assignment and 23 on adhering. Final reported performance tables use 20 assignment and 20 adhering trials. Primary outcomes were those in the corresponding Cochrane reviews.
- **Tool**: RoB 2, including the assignment/adhering distinction for Domain 2 and overall judgement. They assessed model domain judgements and signalling answers.
- **Models and dates**: Claude 3.5 Sonnet; assessments October 10, 2023–August 22, 2024. Each RCT assessed twice with same prompt/model version.
- **Inputs**: Main full-text articles converted to Word format. Human reviewers did not assess supplementary appendices or protocols; protocol availability was noted, but registry information was not investigated for completeness. They acknowledge this differs from real review practice.
- **Prompting / system design**: Structured prompt based on public RoB 2 materials. It directed extraction of relevant information, answers to signalling questions, domain judgements, and rationales; chain-of-thought is discussed as the intended reasoning style. Single model assessment interaction; no agent, tools, or retrieval system reported. Prompt-development pilots drew on three examples per category and then six RCTs for iterative refinement.
- **How domain judgements were produced**: Initially the LLM stated domain and overall judgements directly. The authors then also derived domain judgements by applying the RoB 2 decision algorithm to the model's signalling answers. They explicitly say these algorithm-derived ratings were formed “using Excel based on the RoB2 decision algorithm.” This establishes a separate post-processing comparison, but the paper does not describe executable code, an automated implementation, or an immutable rules service; the spreadsheet calculation appears researcher-operated. Do not characterize this as machine-enforced algorithm execution.
- **Rule-consistency checked?**: Partially. They compared direct LLM domain outputs to the ratings obtained by applying the RoB 2 algorithm to the LLM's own signalling responses, because the LLM sometimes departed from the algorithm. They report accuracy gains but no rate/count of logically inconsistent judgements and no systematic rule-consistency metric. Table 4, reviewer / Cochrane accuracy (direct LLM → algorithm-derived): Domain 1 85→85% / 75→75%; Domain 2 assignment 65→67.5% / 57.5→67.5%; Domain 2 adhering 55→95% / 50→77.5%; Domain 3 83.8→83.8% / 70→67.5%; Domain 4 76.3→85% / 67.5→67.5%; overall assignment 65→65% / 57.5→62.5%; overall adhering 70→90% / 70→90%. Thus the marked changes were Domain 2 adhering (+40 percentage points vs reviewers, +27.5 vs Cochrane) and overall adhering (+20 points vs both); Domain 2 assignment improved modestly against both; Domain 4 improved only against reviewers. (The article's prose has an apparent assignment/adhering wording slip; Table 4 is the domain-specific source.)
- **Evidence handling**: Rationales and supporting information were requested, but no passage-level exact quotation requirement or source verification is described. No stored-source binding or audit trail described. The authors report extraction errors and note PDF-to-Word conversion can lose image content.
- **Reference standard**: Consensus judgement of three experienced reviewers is internal criterion standard; Cochrane review judgements are external comparator. Reviewers had no prior exposure to included trials and received one week of training. The study measured inter-rater agreement and Cochrane-versus-reviewer agreement, rather than assuming the two standards identical. Table 1 gives Cochrane vs reviewer marginal distributions, reviewer IRR, and consistency: Domain 1 80% consistent, κ=.67; Domain 2 assignment 85%, κ=.27; Domain 2 adhering 95%, κ=.54; Domain 3 85%, κ=.87; Domain 4 67.5%, κ=.61; Domain 5 77.5%, κ=.58; overall assignment 85%, κ=.57; overall adhering 100%, κ=.89. Agreement was especially limited for Domain 4 and Domain 5 (and κ particularly low for Domain 2 assignment). Counts by category (Cochrane vs reviewers) were: D1 low/some/high 26/12/2 vs 22/17/1; D2 assignment 10/8/2 vs 7/11/2; D2 adhering 1/1/18 vs 1/0/19; D3 24/3/13 vs 28/1/11; D4 32/4/4 vs 20/17/3; D5 18/21/1 vs 22/18/0. The article does not provide a pairwise disagreement case table in its main text.
- **Adjudication of disagreements**: Three reviewers independently judged and resolved differences by consensus, but this was criterion-standard construction, not a blinded adjudication restricted to LLM-versus-publication disagreements. Blinding is not reported.
- **Headline results**: Direct LLM domain accuracy vs Cochrane/reviewer, respectively (Table 2): D1 75/85%; D2 assignment 57.5/65%; D2 adhering 50/55%; D3 70/83.8%; D4 67.5/76.3%; D5 73.8/93.8%; overall assignment 57.5/65%; overall adhering 70/70%. Mean accuracy for the six non-overall domains was 65.2% vs Cochrane (95% CI 58.9–71.4) and 74.2% vs reviewers (65.3–83.1). Signalling-question mean accuracy vs reviewers was 83.2% (77.5–88.9); exceptions below 70% included questions 2.4/2.5 assignment and 3.3/3.4. Direct judgements tended to “Some Concerns”; Table 4 shows algorithm derivation improved selected domains, especially adhering. There is no single overall directional comparison to reference reported beyond this tendency.
- **Repeatability, time, cost**: Two-run mean consistency 85.2% (95% CI 85.15–88.79). Mean human time 31.5 minutes (SD 12.9) versus LLM 1.9 minutes (SD .2); difference 29.6 minutes (95% CI 25.6–33.6), 93.6% reduction. Cost not reported.
- **Authors' stated limitations**: “current LLMs are unable to process extensive appendices in a single iteration”; reviewers did not incorporate supplementary materials such as registration documents and appendices; adherence trials were concentrated in COPD/asthma and largely high risk. They also identify possible training-data bias and conversion-related information loss.
- **Authors' conclusions**: Structured prompts gave commendable accuracy and consistency and may assist RoB 2, but the model was not ready to replace independent researcher assessment.
- **Quotable sentences**:
  - “Consequently, we attempted to form domain judgments using Excel based on the RoB2 decision algorithm applied to signaling question judgments, rather than relying on LLM-generated domain judgments.” (Discussion, Principal Findings)
  - “Results showed significant improvements in Domains 2 (adhering), 2 (assignment), and overall (adhering), which were the domains with lower accuracy in LLM-generated judgments.” (Discussion, Principal Findings)
  - “The mean assessment time of human reviewers was 31.5 (SD 12.9) minutes, while the LLMs completed assessments in 1.9 (SD 0.2) minutes.” (Results, Efficiency)
- **Bearing on G1–G5**:
  - **G1 Algorithm enforcement — partly contradicts / qualifies**: They did apply the official algorithm to signalling answers in Excel and report comparison accuracies. They do not establish automated code enforcement, count violations, or evaluate rule-consistency as an outcome. Avoid claiming no prior algorithm-derived judgement work.
  - **G2 Evidence traceability — supports**: Rationales/evidence requested, but no verified source-bound quotations, preserved immutable copy, or audit record reported.
  - **G3 Reference noise — contradicts a broad version; supports ARBITER's correction**: They explicitly compare Cochrane labels against a three-reviewer consensus standard and report disagreement by domain. This is a meaningful precedent for questioning published labels, although consensus reviewers are not blinded adjudicators of model disagreements.
  - **G4 Adjudication — partly contradicts**: Three reviewers independently rated all trials and reconciled by consensus; however, no blinded adjudication specifically of model-vs-Cochrane discrepant cells is described.
  - **G5 Agentic/tool design — supports**: Prompted web-interface model; no agent/tool execution or independent validating server.
- **Implication for ARBITER manuscript**: Cite Huang as a direct precedent for deriving algorithm-based ratings from LLM signalling responses. Distinguish the spreadsheet-based secondary analysis from automated, deterministic enforcement and quantified contradiction checking. Their Cochrane-versus-consensus-reviewer disagreement makes reference-label noise an established concern, while ARBITER's blinded adjudication of model/reference disagreements remains a distinct design contribution.

## Lai et al 2026, Journal of Medical Internet Research: ROB 2 exploratory feasibility

- **DOI / IDs**: 10.2196/84915; PMC13614218; PMID 42789359.
- **Full text read**: yes; Europe PMC full-text XML, including the embedded Multimedia Appendix 1 prompt. The article also lists three PDF files in Multimedia Appendix 3 (supplementary tables); key described tables and methods/results were available in full-text XML. I did not inspect those PDF attachments separately.
- **Design**: Exploratory feasibility study; no preregistration reported. Prompt iteratively refined against published assessments using three pilot trials, excluded from analysis.
- **Sample**: 29 RCTs from 11 Cochrane systematic reviews, sampled from eligible published reviews; one of 30 selected trials excluded for incomplete risk-of-bias reporting. Five RoB 2 domains assessed.
- **Tool**: RoB 2, outcome-level assessment, five domains.
- **Models and dates**: GPT-o3-mini-high through ChatGPT web interface; study conducted Dec 28, 2024–Feb 28, 2025. Each trial assessed twice in separate new conversations, default settings.
- **Inputs**: Full trial text uploaded; reviewers defined the primary outcome and supplied domain criteria. No protocols or registry records said to have been supplied. ChatGPT PDF parsing was used, but authors call the mechanism uncertain and note possible omission/formatting errors.
- **Prompting / system design**: Structured prompt defined model role and domain definitions, then transformed official RoB 2 decision trees into written logical rules. Five signalling responses per domain were requested along with domain judgement and rationale. The prompt itself is embedded in Multimedia Appendix 1. Single ChatGPT interaction per trial; no RAG, external tools, or agent reported.
- **How domain judgements were produced**: The rules were written in the prompt for the LLM to follow and the LLM itself produced domain judgements. The authors describe the model as extracting/interpreting evidence for signalling questions and “subsequently strictly following instructions by applying the structured logic” of RoB 2. No separate rule engine or code-derived domain ratings reported.
- **Rule-consistency checked?**: No explicit check that each reported domain judgement logically followed from the model's signalling responses; no consistency rate or contradiction count reported. Repeated-run agreement was measured (a different construct). Prompt refinements sought alignment with reference labels, not a formal rule-consistency audit.
- **Evidence handling**: The model gave rationales, but no requirement for exact quotations, quote verification, persistent source copy, answer-to-passage binding, or audit trail. Error analysis categorized extraction mismatches versus judgement differences through two researchers' review of model rationales and Cochrane evidence.
- **Reference standard**: Cochrane review authors' published RoB 2 assessments. The authors explicitly state these “are not definitive or immune to error.” A sensitivity analysis corrected one apparent typographical error in a Cochrane assessment by team consensus after attempted author contact; primary analysis retained original published values. They also removed one subsequently retracted trial in sensitivity analysis.
- **Adjudication of disagreements**: Two researchers independently categorized LLM/reference discrepancies as extraction or judgement differences, resolving disagreements by consensus. This was not blinded expert reassessment of all model-vs-reference disagreements and did not replace the reference standard generally.
- **Headline results**: Aggregate domain accuracy 73.1% (95% CI 64.7–81.5) and 75.9% (66.3–85.4) in the two runs. Domain-specific accuracy (run 1/run 2, Table 1): D1 62.1/62.1%; D2 69.0/82.8%; D3 79.3/75.9%; D4 86.2/89.7%; D5 69.0/69.0%. Across runs mean domain accuracy 74.5%; sensitivity was modest (domain-averaged 61.4% then 53.4%) and specificity higher (75.8% then 81.1%), meaning lower detection of high-risk judgements than identification of low-risk judgements at domain level. At overall trial level, accuracy was 79.3% then 86.2%; across the two runs, 9 false positives and 1 false negative, so overall labels leaned high-risk. There were 74 discrepancies: 55 (74.3%) judgement differences and 19 (25.7%) extraction inconsistencies. Domain 1 had lowest pooled accuracy (62.1%); Domain 4 highest (87.9%).
- **Repeatability, time, cost**: Mean observed agreement across domains 89.0% (SD 7.5%); κ by domains 1–5: .86, .39, .56, .84, .85. Seventeen of 29 trials (58.6%) were identical across all five domains in both runs. Time and cost were not reported.
- **Authors' stated limitations**: Single model and fixed prompt; 29 trials from 11 reviews may not represent all fields; possible pretraining contamination; ChatGPT web interface and uncertain PDF parsing create methodological fragility. Cochrane labels and human RoB 2 assessments themselves can be inconsistent.
- **Authors' conclusions**: The study found moderate accuracy and high repeatability, with expert oversight still needed, particularly in domains relying on implicit narratives and nuanced behavioral outcomes.
- **Quotable sentences**:
  - “systematically transformed the official ROB 2 decision trees into logical rules within the prompt.” (Methods, Model Training and Prompt Development)
  - “first performing semantic reasoning to extract and interpret complex clinical narratives for answering signaling questions and subsequently strictly following instructions by applying the structured logic of the ROB 2 decision tree.” (Discussion)
  - “their assessments are not definitive or immune to error.” (Discussion, Limitations)
- **Bearing on G1–G5**:
  - **G1 Algorithm enforcement — partly contradicts / qualifies**: The decision tree is operationalized as logical prompt instructions and the model applies them. It is not applied by external code, nor do the authors check whether each judgement follows the algorithm. G1 must distinguish prompt-mediated algorithmic reasoning from independently computed judgements.
  - **G2 Evidence traceability — supports**: Rationales and discrepancy analysis exist, but no quote verification, source binding, or auditable record.
  - **G3 Reference noise — contradicts a broad version**: They discuss reference error, correct one Cochrane typo in sensitivity analysis, and remove a retracted study in sensitivity analysis. They do not systematically reassess the reference labels.
  - **G4 Adjudication — partly contradicts**: Two researchers consensually resolved discrepancy categorization; no blinded clinical/methodological adjudication of model-versus-published-label cells.
  - **G5 Agentic/tool design — supports**: ChatGPT web interface with structured prompt; no tool-using agent or external verification server.
- **Implication for ARBITER manuscript**: Treat this as a clear prompt-encoded decision-tree precedent. The defensible distinction is that ARBITER computes ratings independently of the model and verifies evidence/rule consistency, whereas Lai leaves both interpretation and final rating to the LLM and measures run repeatability rather than logical conformity.

## Lai et al 2024, JAMA Network Open: modified Cochrane ROB feasibility

- **DOI / IDs**: 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.12687; PMC11112444; PMID 38776081.
- **Full text read**: yes; Europe PMC full-text XML, including the embedded eAppendix prompt and supplementary content in the article record.
- **Design**: Survey/feasibility study, August 10–October 30, 2023. Prompt piloted on five RCTs; not reported as preregistered.
- **Sample**: 30 RCTs randomly chosen from three meta-analyses/systematic reviews (10 each); all English-language, publication years 1987–2022.
- **Tool**: Modified original Cochrane ROB tool developed by CLARITY/McMaster, not RoB 2; 10 domains including separate blinding roles; four risk response categories.
- **Models and dates**: ChatGPT and Claude, evaluated September 30–October 10, 2023; each RCT twice per model.
- **Inputs**: Trial PDFs converted to text to make model inputs consistent. ChatGPT required sequential text segments due to context limits; Claude took direct text in one step. Primary outcome specified by trial authors or first reported outcome.
- **Prompting / system design**: Detailed structured prompt with role, domain examples/rules, and output format. No agent, RAG, or tools.
- **How domain judgements were produced**: Models themselves selected the domain-level response; no separate algorithm applied in code.
- **Rule-consistency checked?**: No; tool is modified RoB, and formal algorithm conformity was not assessed.
- **Evidence handling**: Model asked to identify information and provide rationale; no exact quote verification, stored-source binding, or audit trail reported.
- **Reference standard**: Three senior experts independently assessed and reconciled by consensus. No comparison against original systematic-review labels or analysis of reference reliability reported.
- **Adjudication of disagreements**: Expert consensus formed criterion standard. Not a blinded disagreement-specific adjudication.
- **Headline results**: Mean correct assessment rate 84.5% for ChatGPT and 89.5% for Claude; overall sensitivity .82/.90 and specificity .96/.97. Lower sensitivity for several rare/high-risk domains (ChatGPT domains 4 and 6: .17 and 0; Claude: .42 and .25). Repeat assessment agreement 84.0% and 87.3%. The models’ high accuracy should be interpreted against the consensus criterion standard and this modified tool, not RoB 2.
- **Repeatability, time, cost**: Average assessment time 77 seconds (ChatGPT) and 53 seconds (Claude). Cost not reported.
- **Authors' stated limitations**: Input length and sequential upload may have influenced ChatGPT's performance and time; model output remained subject to error and was framed as support rather than replacement.
- **Authors' conclusions**: A structured prompt can support rapid, accurate assessments using the modified Cochrane tool; findings are preliminary.
- **Quotable sentences**:
  - “The 3 senior experts independently assessed the RCTs using the criteria, then reconciling differences through consensus.” (Methods, Establishment of the Criterion Standard)
  - “of the overall 155 wrong assessments, 89 (57.4%) involved the models correctly identifying and articulating the appropriate rationale but making an erroneous judgment” (Results, Accuracy)
- **Bearing on G1–G5**:
  - **G1 Algorithm enforcement — supports**: Structured prompt examples guided domain ratings, but no computed algorithm or rule-consistency check.
  - **G2 Evidence traceability — supports**: No evidence verification or auditability.
  - **G3 Reference noise — not addressed**: Expert consensus was the criterion standard; reliability was not analyzed.
  - **G4 Adjudication — partly contradicts**: Consensus among three experts is precedent for expert reference construction, not blinded adjudication of model/reference disagreements.
  - **G5 Agentic/tool design — supports**: Prompt-driven web model only.
- **Implication for ARBITER manuscript**: Briefly acknowledge this earlier modified-tool feasibility study and its expert consensus standard, while distinguishing its tool/version, lack of algorithm enforcement, and lack of evidence verification.

## Worker summary

G1 needs careful wording: Huang et al. already derived RoB 2 judgements from model signalling answers with the algorithm in Excel and showed substantial gains in selected domains, while Lai et al. 2026 put decision-tree logic directly into the prompt for the LLM to execute. Neither reports independent rule-engine computation plus a formal rule-consistency audit. G2 and G5 remain strong gaps across these papers. G3 and G4 are qualified rather than wholly novel: Huang directly measured Cochrane-versus-three-reviewer consensus disagreement; the 2026 Lai paper acknowledged reference error and corrected a known typo, and both have some human consensus work. None performs ARBITER's blinded adjudication specifically on model/reference disagreement cells. The surprising finding is that Huang's algorithm-derived ratings raised adherence Domain 2 accuracy from 55% to 95% against reviewers and overall adherence from 70% to 90%; the structured-decision-tree precedent substantially narrows any broad novelty claim about “algorithm use.”
