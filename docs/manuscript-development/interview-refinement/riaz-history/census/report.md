# Irbaz B. Riaz publication census and writing-study map

**Scope and status.** This is a targeted census of Riaz-linked work published or posted 1 January 2023–23 October 2026. The frame is author-identity searching across BEACON, Mayo, PubMed, publisher pages and preprint records, followed by title/abstract and, where available, full-text screening for (1) evidence synthesis/living reviews/meta-analyses and evidence-methods, (2) automated risk-of-bias or adjacent systematic-review tasks, and (3) clinical AI systems and methodological perspectives relevant to a clinical AI manuscript. It is not a census of all oncology papers by Riaz and is not claimed exhaustive beyond the PubMed author query described below. The evidence is useful for a manuscript interview; it cannot establish approval likelihood or a stable private preference.

**Identity and census frame.** BEACON identifies Irbaz B. Riaz as its director, gives his Mayo email, and explicitly lists evidence synthesis, biomedical informatics, computational oncology and augmented intelligence as lab areas. Mayo’s official faculty record independently corroborates the person and affiliation. PubMed's query `Riaz IB[Author] AND (2023:2026[dp])`, run 23 October 2026 via NCBI E-utilities, returned 72 records. I screened all 72 title/author/date records; exact-title/DOI, PMID/PMCID and author-topic searches then checked the primary record for candidate works. Bibliographic duplicates and online-first/versioned guideline records were treated as one work where they represented the same document, while genuinely new guideline versions are listed as versions/updates. Search expressions are recorded below and the ledger records the paper-level sources.

## What the broader corpus does—and does not—support

The strongest visible pattern is a **task-to-method sequence**, but it appears across genres and should be described at article level: the living-review perspective walks through evidence-synthesis stages; the JAMIA study starts with living-review extraction burden before describing collaborative models; the distillation paper begins with oncology outcome data and privacy/scalability constraints before comparing architectures; and the guideline work makes evidence review and recommendations distinct. This supports a practical editorial choice for ARBITER: lead with the reviewer's evidence-appraisal problem, then explain the new mechanism where it becomes necessary. It does not prove Riaz personally prefers that sequence.

The papers vary in exposition. The 2024 perspective defines an extensive workflow and surveys many tasks; the empirical JAMIA and npj papers detail methods and qualifications; the 2023 living review and 2025 network meta-analysis use disease and treatment strata to navigate results; the guideline and living updates privilege recommendation tables; the sole-author 2026 invited overview adopts a broad “promise and prudence” frame. This variety argues against borrowing one signature voice. Likewise, the title of the 2026 screening preprint and some abstracts use broad efficacy language, while the distillation study's limitation section is explicit about cohort/task boundaries. Such counterexamples make it especially important to separate paper rhetoric from design-supported claims.

Authorship evidence is uneven. One sole-author invited overview is not enough to infer a stable personal voice. The closest risk-of-bias paper explicitly reports Riaz as one of four first-draft writers, but its full text was not accessible here. The JAMIA article lists Riaz last/corresponding without passage-level drafting credit. The npj 2025 paper expressly shares original drafting among three authors. Other byline positions, corresponding status, lab leadership, and seniority are not substitutes for drafting evidence. Therefore all recurring features above are **observed publication-level features**, not personal habits. Genre, journal, coauthors, editor, and contribution role are plausible alternatives, not demonstrated causes.

## Author-confirmed interview context

These are user-supplied author confirmations, not inferred from the corpus: the motivation now covers clinical practice, risk-of-bias judgment and living evidence; the full ARBITER name is used; the title is evidence-linked; the system mechanism is explained step by step; published-label agreement is presented before independent-expert agreement and the exact agreement meaning is stated before kappa; and the conditional endpoint remains the formal primary outcome. The work remains at author-interview stage. The reviewed corpus suggests these choices can be presented as concrete local rationale and reader orientation; it does not justify revisiting them as presumed Riaz preferences or seeking courtesy citations.

## Writing guidance consulted

Read `/home/ali/Documents/Code/skills/clinical-research-writing/SKILL.md` and its `references/manuscript-workflow.md`: use the smallest pertinent evidence base; distinguish findings, inference and interpretation; calibrate the claim to design; put the clinical problem before the technical solution; define unfamiliar terms at point of need; preserve the expert reader's established schemas; and order structure before language. Relevant local book passages were reviewed in the corpus workflow and checked against the passages cited in `docs/manuscript-development/round2/books-how.md`: Schimel, *Writing Science*, chapters 4–7 (story, funnel and exact question), chapters 10–13 (paragraph and flow), chapters 17–18 (SCFL and limitations); Heard, *The Scientist’s Guide to Writing*, chapters 7, 12 and 13 (story selection, quantitative Results and Introduction/Discussion roles); Zinsser, *On Writing Well*, chapters 2, 8, 9 and 15 (simplicity, unity, opening/ending and sequential technical explanation). These books provide editorial lenses, not evidence about Riaz's personal preferences or about what a journal will accept.
