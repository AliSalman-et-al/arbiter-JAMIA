## Pre-draft distillation

Evidence reviewers need to inspect how a risk-of-bias judgment follows from trial evidence and RoB 2 answers. The practical question is whether a workflow that preserves that record and executes decision rules produces more defensible judgments than a defined same-model alternative.

ARBITER was compared with the same Sonnet model given a minimal prompt and identical supplied trial documents. Two experts independently assessed 510 trial-domain cells before a separate blinded forced-choice exercise. The formal conditional endpoint favored ARBITER in 53/60 agreed choices; 20 of the 80 arm disagreements produced split choices. Across all cells, paired agreement gains were 7.6 and 6.5 percentage points against the two experts. Process checks verified rule conformance and source binding, not semantic support.

The conclusion is that ARBITER provides an inspectable assessment record and agreed more often with each independent expert in this benchmark. The complete-workflow contrast does not isolate software, prompting or compute effects, and it does not establish human-time savings or superiority over other structured systems.
