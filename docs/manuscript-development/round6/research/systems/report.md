## Argument to develop

The literature supports a progression from **automating components** to **separating the properties an assessment must satisfy**. ROBOTO2 shows that an LLM workflow can retrieve candidate passages and answer RoB 2 questions, while the evidence retrieval score and its human corrections make clear that surfacing a passage does not show that it supports an interpretation. Structured RoB 2 prompts can produce high item-level agreement and algorithm-derived judgments may improve selected domain agreement, but the result varies by domain and reference. More recent evaluations directly identify three different failure modes: judgments may vary between runs, conflict with the system’s own signaling answers, or lack documentary support even when the categorical decision agrees with a human label. The manuscript can position ARBITER as a workflow that makes these stages inspectable and delegates deterministic aggregation to software. Its own benchmark then tests agreement and rule conformance; it does not test semantic evidence support or reviewer benefit. This argument gives each study a defined role and avoids a single, misleading league table.

The sharpest evidence chain is: (1) retrieval is measurable but incomplete; (2) reference choice changes apparent agreement; (3) faithful rule application is a separate property from question answering; and (4) documentary support is a separate property from label accuracy. A further, appropriately bounded comparison shows that strong results on one prompt-and-reference setup do not establish transportability across reviews or runs.

## Candidate facts and narrative use

1. **Retrieval is a capability, not a validation of interpretation.** ROBOTO2’s evidence retriever reached recall@10 of 0.678 on a gold-evidence subset with 442 question instances across the five domains (sum of the Table 2 `n-oracle` counts: 197 + 124 + 37 + 73 + 11). The strongest end-to-end answer result was micro-F1 0.71 for Claude 3.5 Sonnet given the full paper; that metric is question-answer classification against the dataset labels, not evidence correctness. In 276 interactive assessments, users supplied 42.4% of signaling-question predictions themselves, while the model-originated proportion was 57.6%; this reflects editing/acceptance behavior and should not be described as accuracy or workload saved. These measures support a concrete description of retrieval and reviewer correction, not a broad statement that ROBOTO2 validates its judgments. (Hevia et al., Tables 2, 4, 7; ACL Anthology proceedings pp. 16, 21, 25; DOI 10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-demos.2.)

2. **Structured prompts can improve a specific judgment step, but the apparent gain is narrow.** In Huang et al.’s 40-trial internal validation set, the adherence-focused subset contained 20 RCTs. For Domain 2 (deviations from intended interventions, adhering), the LLM-generated signaling answers mapped through the RoB 2 algorithm agreed with the expert reviewers in 95% (19/20) of trial judgments; direct LLM domain judgments agreed in 55% (11/20). This is the cleanest number for the principle that answer generation and rule application can be separated. It is a small, subgroup-specific comparison from an iteratively refined prompt, judged against the study’s reviewer consensus, not evidence that the same architecture generalizes or that the decisions are substantively correct. Use only if the manuscript needs a concrete precedent for algorithmic derivation. (Huang et al., Results, Table 4, “Accuracy of large language model-generated and algorithm-derived judgment,” JMIR article e70450, 2025; DOI 10.2196/70450.)

3. **Agreement depends on which human reference is chosen.** Gandhi et al. assessed 50 RCTs with ChatGPT-o3, a masked human panel, and ophthalmic surgical research assessors (OSRAs). For the overall RoB 2 judgment, exact agreement was 46% for ChatGPT-o3 versus the panel, 36% for ChatGPT-o3 versus OSRAs, and 54% for OSRAs versus the panel. The denominator is the 50 overall trial judgments in each pairwise comparison; it is not a pooled comparison with one consensus gold standard. This can support the manuscript’s distinction between published labels and independently recorded experts, and the caution that agreement alone is reference-relative. It does not show that one reference is better or permit cross-study performance comparisons. (Gandhi et al., Results, “Interrater agreement,” Table 2 and accompanying paragraph; PLOS ONE 2026; DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0353155.)

4. **Answer-to-rule conformance is empirically distinct from answer accuracy and reproducibility.** Choi et al. repeated each of three configurations on one trial under identical conditions (13 Gemini general runs, 15 Gemini Gems runs, 10 ROBOTO2 runs). For ROBOTO2, the entered domain rating matched the rating implied by that run’s own signaling answers in a mean 60% of the five domain judgments (Table 3; 10 runs × 5 domains, 50 opportunities). ROBOTO2 also had at least one skip-logic violation in all 10 runs, averaging 4.3 violations per run. These estimates directly motivate ARBITER’s deterministic rule layer and separate structural checks. They arise from one RCT and a workshop exercise; they are a pilot signal, not a population rate or a head-to-head estimate for ARBITER. (Choi et al., Methods “Algorithm conformance and skip-logic,” Results 3.3, Tables 1 and 3, Figures 1 and 2; J Evid-Based Pract 2026;2:91–97; DOI 10.63528/jebp.2026.00011.)

5. **High decision agreement does not guarantee evidence support.** Liang et al. evaluated GPT-5, o3-mini, and GPT-3.5 on 97 RCT reports from one Cochrane review. Their retrieve-then-verify judge labeled each generated RoB claim as supported, contradicted, not found, or out of scope, based on retrieved passages. GPT-5 had mean evidence support of 64.3% (SD 5.9%) and strict hallucination of 35.7% (SD 5.9%); Table 1 reports a 37.41 percentage-point difference between GPT-5’s binary accuracy and evidence support for the overall RoB conclusion. Evidence support is a judge-assigned verdict over generated question/assessment outputs; the main article does not provide a raw numerator and denominator for this aggregate percentage. The study is limited to one review, one automated judge and retrieved context, so these rates are not transferable benchmarks for ARBITER. They do, however, sharply substantiate why source links and rule checks must not be called semantic validation. (Liang et al., Results “Accuracy Relative to Human RoB Assessment” and “Evidence Support and Hallucination,” Table 1 and Figure 3; JMIR AI 2026;5:e93761; DOI 10.2196/93761.)

6. **Contemporary-model agreement with published labels can remain low in a different sampling frame.** Nyrhi et al. evaluated four LLMs on 100 RoB 2 RCTs from high-impact-journal meta-analyses, using published human judgments as the reference. Overall RoB 2 Cohen κ was 0.06 (95% CI −0.07 to 0.18) for ChatGPT o3 and 0.13 (−0.04 to 0.31) for Gemini Flash 2.0; across models the reported range was 0.06–0.13. The unit is one overall RoB 2 judgment per each of 100 articles per model. This supports the claim that results are sensitive to cohort, protocol access, reference and workflow, and that automation performance remains unsettled. It is not a direct contradiction of ARBITER’s expert-reference result: tool, prompts, source set, model, target definitions and reference differ. Avoid presenting these κ values as a direct numerical comparison. (Nyrhi et al., Results, Table 1 and RoB 2 domain-level results; eBioMedicine 2026;126:106238; DOI 10.1016/j.ebiom.2026.106238.)

7. **Use ROBOTO2 and RoBGuard for implementation precedents, not the same performance comparison.** ROBOTO2 integrates PDF parsing, passage retrieval, question answering and editable assessments. RoBGuard adds question reformulation, multimodal parsing and multi-role synthesis. RoBGuard’s Table 2 reports 0.91 accuracy for GPT-4o + RoBGuard on item 5.2, versus 0.34 for its Llama baseline; the paper’s RoB-Item Dataset contains 53 source documents, but Table 2 does not make a held-out test denominator for that cell immediately recoverable. Do not use that item-level accuracy as an overall RoB 2 score or as a ranking against ARBITER. The implementation story is useful; the number should be excluded unless the denominator and evaluation split are confirmed from the authors’ materials. (Ji et al., Sections 4.2–5.3.1, Tables 1–3; COLING 2025; DOI 10.18653/v1/2025.coling-main.84.)

## Recommended manuscript placement and wording logic

- **Introduction, prior systems:** Synthesize by function: earlier systems retrieve or classify evidence; recent work adds structured prompts, multimodal document handling and editable/human-in-the-loop workflows. Cite only ROBOTO2 and one complementary example such as RoBGuard. State the remaining problem as the need to distinguish document traceability, support for a signaling answer, and conformity of the derived judgment to RoB 2 rules. Do not imply these prior systems lacked every safeguard.
- **Methods, ARBITER mechanism:** Explain the concrete mechanism in terms of the manuscript’s actual implementation: the model selects evidence and supplies signaling answers; code computes the rating from those answers; stored references let a reviewer inspect the path. Describe source-text authenticity constraints separately from semantic support. Cite Choi only for the general reason rule conformance merits independent evaluation, not as evidence of ARBITER’s performance.
- **Results/Discussion, bounded interpretation:** Put the manuscript’s own paired exact agreement first, then its answer/rule checks and burden. Use Gandhi or Nyrhi only to explain reference dependence, not to normalize or compare headline rates. Use Liang to establish that evidence support is its own outcome and explicitly acknowledge that ARBITER’s structural provenance audit did not measure it. End with no claim of reviewer time saved, reviewer benefit or autonomous readiness.

This follows Schimel’s OCAR logic: make the review decision problem concrete, show how prior work approaches it, state the unresolved challenge, then describe the action and give a bounded resolution. For a specialist audience, preserve the established RoB 2 vocabulary and spend explanatory space on the unfamiliar workflow distinction. Prior writing-exemplar inspection (including the Riaz-linked full texts and the author’s editorial materials) supports a paper-level pattern of task-first framing, methods that explain unfamiliar system operations, and limitations placed next to the claim they constrain. It does not establish a personal style preference for either Ali Salman or Irbaz Bin Riaz; the Riaz papers are collaborative and authorship of particular paragraphs is not assigned. Do not imitate a voice or cite an exemplar as scientific evidence.

## Source and writing limits

All percentage and κ estimates above retain their paper-specific metric definitions. In particular, ROBOTO2 recall@10, answer micro-F1, human edits, Huang algorithm-derived agreement, Gandhi pairwise exact agreement, Choi rule conformance, Liang evidence support, and Nyrhi κ use different units, sampling frames and references. They must not be put in a common comparison table or described as a performance progression. Schimel and the Riaz/Salman exemplars guide organization and audience calibration only; they are not empirical support for clinical claims. The source ledger records individual include/exclude decisions and fact locators.
