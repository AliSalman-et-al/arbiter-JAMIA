### Source accuracy/context: A 3, B 3 — acceptance blocker

Both manuscripts replace Gandhi's original systematic review authors with “ophthalmic surgical research assessors.” This invents a comparator identity and falsely suggests a specialty-specific study. Both ledgers reproduce the contamination. The primary abstract states 50 RCTs sampled from 50 meta-analyses and defines OSRAs as original systematic review authors; Methods repeats that definition. The overall exact agreements are correctly transcribed: model–panel 46%, model–OSRA 36%, OSRA–panel 54%. Correct numbers do not rescue incorrect population/reference context. See the [primary PLOS article](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0353155), abstract Methods, Methods assessment procedure and Results “Interrater agreement.”

The other selected checks were reassuring. Huang explicitly says each RCT was assessed twice. Table 2 reports direct D2 adherence agreement against reviewers as 55%; Table 4 reports algorithm-derived agreement as 95%, with fractional values elsewhere that defeat a naive single-assessment denominator. Neither candidate imports inferred 11/20 or 19/20 counts or the percentages. This omission is a valid response to unresolved run-level accounting; it is not a lost required scientific finding. Both retain the corrected “Evaluation Study” reference title, while the inspected local snapshot predates that correction.

Choi Table 3 confirms 60% mean ROBOTO2 conformance, not general accuracy; both candidates contextualize the repeated single-trial design. Liang confirms 97 reports and mean 64.3% support (SD 5.9%) under automated verification; neither invents an aggregate raw denominator or transfers that rate to ARBITER. Soboczenski confirms 755/824-second raw means, 82 assessments per condition and a separate mixed-model relative time of 0.75 (0.62–0.92). Both correctly distinguish modeled reduction from the ratio of means. Minozzi's sequential calibration/instruction estimates are contextualized without causal attribution; the [primary 2022 abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34537386/) supports the reported sequence. No cross-study numerical leaderboard is constructed.

### Argument, voice and flow

A has a cleaner Introduction scope: the reliability exercise creates an appraisal problem, and two system precedents establish the distinction among source availability, answer support and rule conformity. Its Discussion, however, often completes a useful inference and then appends several boundaries already stated elsewhere. “Reference dependence is substantive rather than a statistical inconvenience” is assertive framing without enough added explanation. Its opening repeats the conditional accounting before developing the reference discrepancy. The progression is coherent but still resembles a careful evaluation report more than a fully developed scientific discussion.

B's Discussion opening goes further: broader targets could make discordant labels defensible for different results, and review conventions could differ. These are useful, explicitly untested explanations. Its D3 paragraph explains what collapsing categories changes, rather than merely preserving the adverse finding. Gandhi, Choi, Liang and Soboczenski each serve a distinct proposition: reference choice changes interpretation; rule execution differs from appraisal; authentic evidence can remain insufficient; human workload requires observation. Those are real paragraph jobs. This merits 4 for argument and flow despite the source repair.

B's opening still moves too quickly from low agreement to a catalogue of capabilities. The living-review sentence and RoBGuard addition interrupt a sharper result-specific argument. Its closing Introduction lists outcomes without stating the unanswered comparative question quite plainly enough. Both candidates use clinical appraisal concepts appropriately and explain unfamiliar source-selection operations concretely, but hashes, audit-file distinctions and command correspondence still occupy disproportionate interpretive space. B is stronger, not finished.

Audit/repository voice no longer dominates every section, but it still dominates the limitations paragraph and parts of the documentary-support discussion. Repeated “cannot,” “did not,” “not established” endings become the paragraph rhythm. Necessary uncertainty is not the problem; repeatedly re-establishing the same boundary is. The EJHF comparison explains a discrepancy through design differences, and the Circulation p.1118 passage distinguishes constructs through examples before drawing its inference. B approaches that standard in its reference and coding paragraphs; neither sustains it throughout. Schimel's paragraph-unit and coherence guidance supports retaining explanatory sentences while consolidating repeated qualifications, not compressing everything into shorter declarations.
