## Manuscript review

**Story as written:** ARBITER preserves an inspectable RoB 2 assessment record and, in this benchmark, agrees more often with two independent experts than a same-model minimal-prompt run. I found no verified contradiction in the reported headline counts. The main repairs concern what the conditional endpoint means, how result targets are described, and whether readers can appraise the reference process and sampling frame. The numerical acceptance audit is prior evidence, not an independent reanalysis by this review.

1. **Warning — “Preference for ARBITER” overstates the forced-choice observation.** [Manuscript, lines 21–25](/home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:21), [75](/home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:75), [95](/home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:95). Experts saw labels without source identities or rationales; credit followed label equality, including when a published label matched an arm. Thus 53/60 records agreement with ARBITER’s *label* in a selected set, not a judgment about its workflow. **Revision:** “Among 60 arm-discordant cells on which the experts chose the same blinded label, that label matched ARBITER in 53 (88%; 95% CI, 80–96%).” Use “conditional label choice” rather than “ARBITER preference” where the distinction matters. **Confidence: high.**

2. **Warning — the result-target sentence is stronger than the benchmark ledger permits.** [Manuscript, line 59](/home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:59) says prompts targeted “the result labeled by the review.” In the 102 included rows of [LABEL-TARGETS.csv](/home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/rob2-meta-benchmark-set/LABEL-TARGETS.csv:1), the recorded `assess_vs_label` status is *partly* or *different* for 16 trials and *unknown* for 10; 11 further “same” rows cover several outcomes. The [source-set account](/home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/rob2-meta-benchmark-set/README-FOR-ALI.md:53) explains these distinctions. The next sentence acknowledges uncertainty, but does not undo the uniform assertion. Because RoB 2 assesses a specified result, this matters particularly for the Published comparison; it does not by itself invalidate comparisons in which both arms and experts assessed the same specified target. **Revision:** “We specified a result using the review’s reported outcome and the available trial result; its correspondence to the published risk-of-bias label was sometimes partial, inferred, or indeterminate.” Give the ledger’s included-set status counts in Methods or a table note, without treating them as a new performance subgroup. **Confidence: high for the wording mismatch; the effect on estimates is unknown.**

3. **Warning — readers cannot yet appraise the independent expert reference fully.** [Manuscript, lines 67–71](/home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:67) describes the valuable independent-first sequence, but leaves qualifications, experience and training as a placeholder. Neither that passage nor the [supplement’s expert-methods text](/home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/analysis/R/manuscript-supplement.R:31) specifies how experts were instructed to identify the assessed result or reconcile ambiguous sources before freezing ratings. With 388/510 agreement overall and 54/102 in D3, those procedural details affect interpretation. **Revision:** Fill the credential field from the experts’ actual records and state what target specification and RoB 2 guidance they received, what they were blinded to at each stage, and whether they used the same target-result brief as the arms. Do not infer credentials or training. **Confidence: high that reporting is incomplete; unknown whether the procedure itself was deficient.**

4. **Warning — the review sampling frame is missing.** [Manuscript, line 59](/home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:59) explains the within-review table order and replacement rule, but not how the 16 reviews entered the source set or what “eligible” meant before report availability was considered. The [benchmark README](/home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/rob2-meta-benchmark-set/README-FOR-ALI.md:3) also starts with those 16 reviews. Readers can see the included specialties, but cannot reconstruct the initial selection frame. **Revision:** Add the actual review identification source, selection criteria, dates, and eligibility rule, or explicitly call the reviews a convenience set if that is how they were assembled. Keep the existing generalizability limit. **Confidence: medium-high; I did not find these details in the manuscript or inspected eMethods source.**

5. **Warning — the knowledge gap remains implicit, and a directly relevant RoB 2 evidence-support study is absent from the bibliography.** [Introduction, lines 39–45](/home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:39) moves from a list of precedents to the study question without stating exactly what this evaluation adds. [Discussion, line 111](/home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:111) cites a generative-search study for the citation-presence/support distinction, while the [2026 Liang et al. RoB 2 study](https://ai.jmir.org/2026/1/e93761/) directly assessed documentary support for generated RoB judgments across 97 trials. The manuscript’s distinction between structural binding and semantic support remains sound; this closer precedent would make its boundary clearer. **Revision:** Cite Liang alongside the closest workflow studies and state the gap as the *combination evaluated here*: an available workflow that binds source records and computes ratings, tested against a same-model minimal-prompt run with independent ratings of every domain. Avoid a first-of-its-kind claim from a targeted literature search. This gives the Introduction the specific unknown urged by [Schimel’s knowledge-gap passage](/home/ali/Documents/Code/skills/book-references/Schimel%20Joshua/Writing%20Science_%20How%20to%20Write%20Papers%20t%20Cited%20and%20Proposals%20That%20Get%20Funded/index.html:297). **Confidence: high on the omission; medium on the preferred gap wording.**

6. **Warning — system capability and observed benchmark oversight are momentarily conflated.** [Manuscript, line 51](/home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:51) says “A reviewer approves the proposed result before domain assessment,” whereas [line 65](/home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:65) says the benchmark harness approved proposed results unchanged. A reader could initially understand the evaluated ARBITER arm as having active human target approval. **Revision:** “In interactive use, a reviewer can approve the proposed result before domain assessment; in this benchmark, the harness accepted that proposal unchanged.” **Confidence: high.**

7. **Warning — the visible draft is not administratively submission complete.** Author details and expert qualifications remain open at [lines 7 and 69](/home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:7); disclosures, ethics, contributions and archival citation remain open at [lines 127–149](/home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:127). The [final acceptance note](/home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/rethink-2026-10-22/final-audit/acceptance.md:15) also identifies pending author-supplied fields. **Revision:** Complete them from attributable author and institutional records before submission. This is a reporting requirement, not evidence of a scientific error. **Confidence: high.**

The draft already states the main unfavorable findings: D3’s expert dependence, the negative five-level signalling contrast, the Published comparison, and higher computation. I would preserve those. I would not require a new model run, new expert panel, or target-matching subgroup to resolve the reporting issues above. The manuscript and analyses were not edited.
