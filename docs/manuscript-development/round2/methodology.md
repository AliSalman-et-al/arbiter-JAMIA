## Review scope and judgment

I reviewed the manuscript source at baseline `3e150f4`, the current analysis plan and glossary, the accepted rebuild brief, second-review blueprint and final audit, the story contract, the supplement’s methods and reporting map, and the exported endpoint and agreement tables. I did not edit files or rerun analyses.

The main scientific claims are carefully bounded. The manuscript distinguishes the conditional forced-choice result from all-cell agreement, reports the two experts separately, and describes what its bootstrap intervals do and do not cover. The checked primary endpoint and pooled agreement contrasts match the stored exports. I found **no clear numerical error or major inferential defect** in those claims.

The most useful criticism is about what the report lets readers reproduce and appraise: prompt development is not described, and the identity and stability of the hosted model across the two run dates are unclear. The expert qualifications are also still a literal author-completion field in the workspace manuscript.

## Findings

1. **Prompt development and selection are not reported in the text I inspected.** The Methods say ARBITER received an assessment skill and guidance and refer readers to eMethods for prompts ([manuscript.md, lines 63–65](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:63>)). The supplement gives the final templates and run details, but I found no account of how the prompts were designed, curated, or selected, or what data informed that process ([supplement-page.md, “Prompt templates and run provenance”](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/analysis/.quarto/supplement-page.md:13772>)). The accompanying reporting map marks TRIPOD-LLM items 9a and 9b as reported, pointing to those same materials. Those items ask about the prompt-design process and the data used to develop prompts; a final template and benchmark table do not, by themselves, answer those questions. [TRIPOD-LLM](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03425-5) is the relevant reporting guideline. This is a **reporting and reproducibility gap**, not evidence that the prompts were tuned on evaluation trials. Clarifying the prompt-development history would let readers assess that possibility without implying it occurred.

2. **The report does not establish whether both arms used the same immutable model snapshot.** It identifies `claude-sonnet-5-5`, medium effort, and Claude Code 2.1.278, and dates the ARBITER and Unassisted runs to 12 and 14 October 2026, respectively ([manuscript.md, line 65](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:65>)). It does not say whether the model identifier resolved to a pinned snapshot or a changing service model, or whether the same underlying model version served both run dates. This matters to interpreting a same-model comparison, even though the manuscript appropriately states that it measured one run per arm and that its intervals do not capture run-to-run variation ([manuscript.md, lines 89 and 119](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:89>)). TRIPOD-LLM recommends reporting closed-model inference dates and, in its explanatory guidance, model-version dates and whether the service remained dynamic. I found **no evidence that a model update occurred**; the concern is that the report does not rule one out. A pinned version or an explicit statement that it could not be recovered would make this limitation assessable.

3. **The expert-qualification field remains unresolved in the workspace manuscript.** The Methods describe independent assessments, freezing ratings before label exposure, and the later blinded choice task, but end the assessor description with `[Expert qualifications, experience and training]` ([manuscript.md, lines 69–71](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:69>)). The reporting map also marks assessor information as partial and author-dependent. TRIPOD-LLM item 7d and GRRAS call for rater characteristics and assessment procedures. This is relevant because readers need context for the expert references; it does **not** invalidate the independent-first design or show that the experts lacked relevant expertise. I am identifying an unresolved field in this source file, not making a claim about the final publisher version or the experts’ actual qualifications.

4. **The workspace source still contains publication-package placeholders.** The front matter leaves affiliations and corresponding-author information incomplete, and the back matter marks AI-use confirmation, funding, competing interests, author contributions, ethics/consent, registration, public involvement, and the archival data identifier as pending ([manuscript.md, lines 7 and 127–149](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:7>)). JAMIA’s current instructions specify the Research and Applications abstract headings and limits, require a Data Availability Statement, and ask authors to disclose AI use in the manuscript and cover letter ([official JAMIA instructions](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/pages/General_Instructions)). The manuscript has a data-availability section and an AI-use statement, but this workspace copy labels key details as unfinished. Treat this as a **source-to-publication verification issue**: the file alone cannot establish whether those items were completed in the published version, and I do not infer any underlying declaration.

## Interrogation of the main inference

The `53/60` result is conditional on 60 arm-discordant cells where both experts made the same choice; the manuscript also reports the 80 discordant cells, 20 split choices, separate expert sensitivities, and a split-choice sensitivity. That is a defensible, clearly described endpoint, but it is not an all-cell preference rate or a measure of correctness. The manuscript keeps that distinction clear ([manuscript.md, lines 75 and 95](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:75>)).

Likewise, the all-cell comparisons support the statement that ARBITER had greater agreement with each expert in this benchmark. They do not establish which expert was right, why the workflow performed differently, or whether the difference will generalize to new experts, reviews, or model runs. The stated bootstrap scope and limitations match that interpretation. The paper also separates source retrievability and rule conformance from semantic evidence support, and computation from human workload. Those boundaries are appropriate; human usefulness remains unmeasured, not disproven.

The study’s run count, fixed review set, possible model exposure to trial reports, ordered availability-dependent sampling, and potential anchoring during forced choices are already disclosed ([manuscript.md, lines 59, 71, and 119](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:59>)). I would not relabel those disclosed limits as new defects. The manuscript also cites precedents for evidence-assisted RoB systems and same-model prompt comparisons, so its contribution is best read as the specific available workflow and this bounded evaluation—not priority for those general ideas.
