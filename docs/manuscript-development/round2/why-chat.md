## Critical review

**Review basis:** the published baseline [3e150f4](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript/manuscript.md:35>), the current manuscript, the accepted rebuild brief, blueprint, final audit and story contract. I used the requested Pstack lenses as review methods: *sharpen* to identify the reader’s central question, *how* to trace the paper’s argument, *interrogate* to challenge its claims and design, and *reflect* to check whether the finished narrative delivers what its own editorial contract asks of it. This was a read-only review; I made no changes.

### Highest-priority findings

1. **The paper’s two contributions compete for the lead.** The opening centers an inspectable basis for appraisal (manuscript lines 37–39); the objective then asks both what pathway ARBITER makes inspectable and how its workflow compares with a minimal-prompt model (45). But the formal primary endpoint is a *conditional forced-choice preference* (75), while the paper’s more general performance evidence is secondary, all-cell agreement with two experts (77, 97). This creates a hierarchy readers must reconstruct: is the paper principally about a reviewable system, comparative judgment performance, or a benchmark of how references affect evaluation? The accepted blueprint’s governing idea combines inspectability and expert agreement, but assigns the all-cell comparisons the main explanatory weight and says the formal primary should not be read as population accuracy ([blueprint, lines 5–11 and 25–33](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/rethink-2026-10-22/second-review/blueprint.md:5>)). The current manuscript reports the conditional endpoint first in Results (95), before the all-cell comparisons (97). **Recommendation:** make the two-part contribution explicit in the Introduction, then signal the distinct roles of the conditional primary and full-cohort agreement results before the reader encounters the 53/60 result. This is a structural clarification, not a recommendation to discard the formally designated endpoint.

2. **The 53/60 headline has a narrow and potentially surprising interpretation.** The endpoint includes only cells where the arms disagree *and* both experts choose the same label; 20 of 80 arm-disagreement cells with split expert choices are excluded from the primary denominator. The reported 88% is therefore conditional on a selected subset and credits label equality with the agreed choice—not performance across all 510 cells, nor the share of all expert preferences favoring ARBITER. The manuscript does disclose the pathway and gives expert-specific and split-as-loss sensitivities (75, 95), which is good disclosure. But the Introduction and Discussion still call it the “primary” or “preference” result without immediately restating what that preference estimates (45, 109). **Recommendation:** describe it consistently as preference *among arm-discordant cells with concordant expert choices*; keep 53/60 beside the 80-cell parent set, 20 splits, and the 53/80 sensitivity. Do not let “88%” travel alone in headings, abstract, or conclusion.

3. **“Formal primary” and “confirmatory” need a traceable timing basis.** The Methods says only the formal primary endpoint was confirmatory (89), while the current approved brief says it retains the endpoint’s formal primary designation ([rebuild brief, lines 25–33](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/rethink-2026-10-22/rebuild-brief.md:25>)). In the manuscript material reviewed, I could not locate a dated study protocol, registration, or analysis plan that establishes when this endpoint and its analysis were fixed. The manuscript’s statement that the stored AVATAR trial documents did not establish a dated analysis plan is specifically about the *assessed trial result* (55), not the ARBITER evaluation plan. The back matter still has a registration placeholder (143–145). **This is an evidence gap, not evidence that the endpoint was selected after results were seen.** Before presenting it as confirmatory, provide the study-level provenance and timing, or make clear what “confirmatory” means if it was not prospectively specified.

4. **The comparator is intentionally weak, so the paper must keep its claim attached to that contrast.** Unassisted receives a task sentence and a fixed output-format sentence, without RoB 2 guidance; ARBITER receives an assessment skill and its guidance in addition to the same trial evidence (63). The manuscript correctly says this compares the complete workflow and cannot isolate guidance, software, or computation, and it distinguishes prior structured-prompt comparators (63, 113). Still, readers may infer “ARBITER improves appraisal” more broadly than the design supports—especially from the unqualified comparative phrasing in the objective and conclusion (45, 123). The approved brief explicitly limits the claim to the complete Sonnet workflow in this benchmark and says the contrast does not establish superiority over structured systems ([rebuild brief, lines 7–11](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/rethink-2026-10-22/rebuild-brief.md:7>)). **Recommendation:** keep the comparator in the main claim whenever stating the performance result, including abstract and conclusion; state plainly that it is a minimal-prompt benchmark, not a test against expert prompting or other systems.

5. **The all-cell expert comparison is stronger context, but two experts are not a stable “truth” reference.** Two experts independently rated all 510 cells before seeing offered labels, then chose among Published, Unassisted, and ARBITER labels; each later gave a revised rating (69–71). That sequence preserves an independent-first reference and separates the independent ratings from potentially anchored choices. It does not establish that the two raters represent a broader expert population, and the forced-choice phase is susceptible to anchoring by the offered labels. The manuscript acknowledges these points (71, 119), and the accepted brief specifically says expert agreement is not a ceiling or error-free standard ([rebuild brief, lines 25–28](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/rethink-2026-10-22/rebuild-brief.md:25>)). **Recommendation:** keep “agreement with these two experts” close to the claim; avoid letting “expert preference” sound like adjudicated correctness. The all-cell paired gains—7.6 and 6.5 percentage points (97)—are the more interpretable evidence of agreement under the chosen references, but remain reference-dependent.

6. **The confidence intervals describe a deliberately conditional uncertainty target.** The bootstrap retains trial counts within 16 fixed reviews; the manuscript says the intervals quantify trial variation conditional on those reviews, two experts, and observed runs, not variation across new reviews, experts, or repeated runs (89). This is a meaningful qualification, not a generic limitation. However, the benchmark selection is review-table ordered and availability-dependent, taking up to seven trials per review (59); the manuscript itself says that selection limits generalization (119). Readers need to understand that the intervals do not repair this selection mechanism or represent uncertainty for a future set of reviews. **Recommendation:** state this limitation near the primary estimate and the all-cell gains—not only in the statistical Methods and late Discussion—and avoid language that could make the CIs sound like broad reproducibility or transportability evidence.

### Other substantive concerns

- **The gap in the Introduction is more asserted than demonstrated.** The paper gives a useful taxonomy of prior work and narrows novelty away from “AI can produce a plausible label” (39–41). But it does not quite say what consequential uncertainty remains after those studies: is it whether an inspectable *complete workflow* can improve agreement, whether published labels and fresh expert assessments yield different evaluations, or whether the evidence-to-answer-to-rating path can be audited? The objective bundles all of these plus process and computational outcomes (45). The blueprint offers a sharper challenge—an inspectable record does not itself validate judgment, so the system and the judgment need separate evaluation ([blueprint, lines 9–11 and 37–45](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/rethink-2026-10-22/second-review/blueprint.md:9>)). Make that question the Introduction’s explicit hinge; avoid expanding it into a general validation claim.

- **The assessment target is an important measurement limitation, but arrives densely in Methods.** The benchmark targets outcomes labeled by published reviews rather than automatically selecting a trial’s primary outcome; published labels may cover a broader, inferred, or different target, and target uncertainty is considered qualitatively (59). That caveat matters to interpreting all reference comparisons, not just the Published-label analysis. It is also unusually easy to miss inside a long sampling paragraph. **Recommendation:** give the target definition and its consequence a short, standalone explanation before the comparator/results, then remind readers at the Published-reference finding. The approved brief explicitly prohibits filtering by target matching or claiming disagreement proves published labels wrong ([rebuild brief, line 33](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/rethink-2026-10-22/rebuild-brief.md:33>)); the manuscript appropriately follows that constraint.

- **“Inspectability” is demonstrated as traceability and reproducibility, not semantic validity or usability.** Source binding, quoted spans, image-region links, executable rules, and reviewable overrides make a path inspectable (51–53, 111). The paper explicitly says these checks do not establish that evidence supports an interpretation. It also says human checking time, reviewer decisions, adoption, and automation bias were not measured (119). The conclusion’s “lets reviewers inspect” is supported by the exported record, but could be mistaken for demonstrated reviewer benefit (123). Keep the distinction visible: this study shows a record *available for inspection*, not that reviewers successfully inspected it or made better decisions because of it. This is consistent with the blueprint’s warning not to equate source occurrence with semantic support ([blueprint, lines 27–33](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/rethink-2026-10-22/second-review/blueprint.md:27>)).

- **Several results are scientifically informative but compete for narrative space.** The Results move from reference distributions and the conditional endpoint to full-cohort agreement, domain differences, answer-coding sensitivity, rule conformance, and computational burden (93–105). The accepted blueprint says each display should answer a distinct reader question, the main text should state patterns rather than recite every estimate, and D5 and D3 should explain both gain and uncertainty ([rebuild brief, lines 35–50 and 52–58](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/rethink-2026-10-22/rebuild-brief.md:35>)). The current sequence largely honors this, but the opening Results paragraph spends space on prevalence before the formal primary result, while the Discussion’s fifth-level versus collapsed D3 result (115) is technical and may interrupt the main system/evaluation arc. **Recommendation:** retain the D3 diagnostic because it qualifies the apparent gain, but make its reader-facing meaning explicit before the coding detail. Keep the primary and all-cell comparison visibly distinct in Figure 3/Table 2/Figure 4 as specified.

- **The Conclusion’s final sentence shifts from an empirical result to an affordance.** It closes by saying the workflow “lets reviewers inspect” evidence and decisions alongside measured agreement and disagreements (123). That is a reasonable description of the tool, but it ends on availability/affordance rather than resolving the paper’s paired question about what is inspectable and what the benchmark establishes. Consider ending with one bounded synthesis: the workflow exposes the assessment path, and its agreement advantage was observed against these two experts under this comparator, with domain/reference dependence. The current story contract calls for returning to the appraisal problem at the supported scope ([story contract, lines 7–11 and 21–30](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/manuscript-development/story-contract.md:7>)). This is a resolution issue, not a request to add a new implication.

### Publication-readiness issue

The manuscript contains unresolved author-only fields for expert qualifications and training, acknowledgments, funding, competing interests, contributions, ethics/consent, registration, patient/public involvement, and archival DOI/data citation (69, 127–149). The final audit explicitly says those details remain pending and that the package is not administratively submission-complete ([acceptance, lines 5–15](</home/ali/Documents/Code/arbiter-JAMIA/docs/rethink-2026-10-22/final-audit/acceptance.md:5>)). If “newly published” refers to the manuscript baseline being reviewed, these placeholders need resolution before treating this version as a final submission-ready record. I did not infer any missing declarations.

### Synthesis and confidence

**Directly supported by the inspected manuscript and accepted editorial record:** the study evaluates a complete Sonnet workflow against a minimal-prompt same-model comparator; the primary endpoint is conditional on arm disagreement and expert agreement; the full-cohort comparisons are secondary; intervals are conditional on fixed reviews, two experts, and observed model runs; human-use effects are unmeasured; and administrative fields remain pending.

**Review judgments:** the contribution hierarchy needs clearer signaling; the primary endpoint’s conditional interpretation should stay attached to every headline; “confirmatory” needs study-level timing provenance; and the comparator/reference boundaries should remain adjacent to performance claims. These are editorial and methodological judgments based on the disclosed design, not claims about author intent.

**Unknown:** the Slack search yielded no exact-project evidence for why the approved narrative and analysis choices were selected. The inspected artifacts do not establish prospective timing for the study-level endpoint specification. I have not treated missing documentation as proof of post hoc selection.
