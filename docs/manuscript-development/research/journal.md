## Official JAMIA requirements checked

The current [JAMIA Instructions to Authors](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/pages/General_Instructions) were checked on 23 October 2026. This is the live official instructions page, not an abstract or secondary summary. For Research & Applications, it specifies a structured abstract with **Objective, Materials and Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion**, maximum **250 words**, main text maximum **4,000 words**, maximum **4 tables** and **6 figures**, and unlimited references. The main text must include **Background and Significance**. The page says word limits exclude acknowledgments and references; the title-page word-count convention excludes title page, abstract, references, figures and tables. Double spacing is required. (Page lines 91–102, 169–194.)

Other concrete requirements on that page:

- Title page: title; corresponding author full contact details; every coauthor’s name, department, institution, city, country and degree; up to five keywords; word count (lines 169–177).
- Submit a **Word document**; main-text tables are Word tables placed at first citation and cited in numerical order; figures are separate uploaded files, cited in order, with legends at manuscript end; supplementary files are cited in text (lines 178–194, 206–222).
- Alt text is required for every main-article image/figure/illustration/photograph and should appear below the legend, prefixed “Alt text:” (lines 209–218).
- References numbered by first appearance; the reference list must use consecutive numbering; journal abbreviations follow Medline; website sources need URL and access date (lines 227–259).
- AI use (including content, images, code, data processing or translation) must be disclosed in the **cover letter and Methods or Acknowledgments**; AI is not an author. JAMIA expects CRediT roles (lines 79–89).
- A **Data Availability Statement is required**. Public datasets must also be cited in the reference list using a dataset citation and accession/unique identifier, preferably DOI (lines 283–293).
- Funding must be declared in submission, including an explicit no-funding answer if applicable (lines 166–168). Conflicts are declared in submission and published statement (lines 75–78). The instructions also require submission of related published materials for overlap review (lines 91–94).

DOI: [10.1038/s41591-024-03425-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03425-5). The [publisher full-text page](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03425-5) states the guideline contains 19 main items/50 subitems across title, abstract, introduction, methods, open science, patient/public involvement, results and discussion, with modular applicability by task/design. The [EQUATOR record](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/the-tripod-llm-reporting-guideline-for-studies-using-large-language-models/) links the checklist and separate abstract checklist. Sources checked were full article/page descriptions, not merely an abstract.

Treat checklist completion as **ISSUE / needs final audit**, not as passed. The manuscript substantively reports task, model/harness versions, prompts, input parity and limits, one run, metrics, expert reference, human oversight, public code/data pathways, and limitations. Verify the final draft and checklist against the correct modular **LLM evaluation / classification** pathway, including: model/tool identity and version/provider/configuration; exact system prompts and inference environment; case/input selection and provenance; output handling/failed or missing runs; intended-use/population and reference construction; prespecification/deviations; performance and uncertainty; human evaluation/blinding/rater characteristics; data/code access; and patient/public involvement. ### GRRAS

Primary sources: Kottner J, Audigé L, Brorson S, et al. “Guidelines for Reporting Reliability and Agreement Studies (GRRAS) were proposed.” *J Clin Epidemiol.* 2011;64(1):96–106. DOI: [10.1016/j.jclinepi.2010.03.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2010.03.002). The [EQUATOR one-page checklist PDF](https://www.equator-network.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/GRRAS-checklist-for-reporting-of-studies-of-reliability-and-agreement.pdf) was inspected in full. It covers rationale, target subject/rater populations, sample-size rationale, sampling, rating process/blinding/independence, statistical methods, actual rater/object counts, rater/subject characteristics, estimates with uncertainty, practical relevance, and detailed supplemental results.

The draft performs well on unit/population counts, blinding and order of expert ratings, independent ratings before label exposure, the forced-choice process, agreement estimates and intervals, and detailed online supplements. **GRRAS issues:** the manuscript’s `[Expert qualifications, experience and training]` placeholder is material because GRRAS asks for rater characteristics; the basis for sample size/selection of 102 trials from the 16 reviews should be stated or linked explicitly; participant/case sampling needs to be transparent; and practical relevance should remain bounded by the stated absence of a human-workload study. The study’s experts are reference assessors, not a general rater population; describe them accurately once authors supply the facts.
