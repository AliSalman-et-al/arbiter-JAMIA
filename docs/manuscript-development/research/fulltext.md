**Status: ISSUES.** Choi and ROBoto2 full-text PDFs were retrieved from their publishers and inspected. RoB2-Evaluator’s primary medRxiv full-text HTML and its included supplementary prompt appendix were inspected through indexed full-text output, but direct medRxiv PDF/HTML retrieval was blocked (HTTP 403 / Cloudflare), so a local copy could not be saved. ## Choi et al. (2026)

**Primary sources:** [Publisher article page](https://e-jebp.org/journal/view.php?doi=10.63528/jebp.2026.00011); [publisher PDF](https://e-jebp.org/upload/pdf/jebp-2026-00011.pdf); DOI [10.63528/jebp.2026.00011](https://doi.org/10.63528/jebp.2026.00011). Retrieved PDF is at `/tmp/arbiter-fulltexts/choi-2026.pdf`; extracted inspection text at `/tmp/arbiter-fulltexts/choi-2026.txt`.

**Inspected:** Full article, pp. 91–97: Methods, “Study Design and Data,” “RoB 2.0 Algorithm Implementation,” “Analysis Metrics”; Results, “Algorithm Conformance and Internal Consistency,” “Effect of Recomputation”; Tables 1–3; Discussion and Limitations. Publisher abstract and article metadata were cross-checked against the PDF.

- The sample is **38 assessments of one RCT** in a conference workshop: Gemini general (13), Gemini Gems (15), and Roboto/ROBoto2 (10). Gemini general and Gems share a base model, manual, and prompt; their difference is full manual in context versus the manual provided as Gem RAG reference knowledge. ROBoto2 is the purpose-built pipeline. This is not a three-arm same-model workflow-on/off comparison (pp. 92–93, Table 1).
- One experienced reviewer supplied the reference. The authors detected and corrected three transcription errors in that reference using the algorithm-conformance check; the corrected reference was algorithm-conformant in all five domains (p. 92). Do not characterize this as a multi-rater independent expert panel.
- The authors implemented RoB 2 answer-to-domain mapping in code, with Domains 1, 2, and 5 from official decision tables and 2019 final structures for Domains 3 and 4 (p. 92). They compared each tool-entered domain rating with the algorithm-implied rating from that run’s own signaling answers. Mean five-domain conformance was 43% Gemini general, 51% Gems, and 60% Roboto; per-run skip-logic violations occurred in 69%, 80%, and 100%, respectively (pp. 94–95, Table 3).
- Recomputing ratings improved expert agreement for Gemini general (mean Δ +0.34) and Gems (+0.24). For ROBoto2, recomputation was **explicitly uninterpretable** because the system was designed to use flowchart aggregation, yet displayed answers and judgments were sometimes decoupled. Its per-run skip-logic violations and answer–judgment discrepancy must not be simplified into “ROBoto2 has no rule engine” (pp. 94–95, Table 3).
- This is a descriptive, single-target pilot: no formal between-configuration hypothesis tests, and its authors caution that generalization is limited (pp. 93, 96). Safe manuscript wording: “A single-trial pilot found substantial variability and answer–judgment nonconformance across three configurations, including a purpose-built ROBoto2 pipeline; recomputation from signaling answers improved agreement for the two Gemini configurations.” Do not claim ARBITER is first to measure RoB 2 rule inconsistency or to recompute judgments from answers.

## Joseph et al., RoB2-Evaluator (medRxiv, 2026)

**Primary source:** [Version 1 full-text page](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.22.26363693v1.full); DOI [10.64898/2026.09.22.26363693](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.22.26363693). Article is posted as a **medRxiv preprint and has not undergone peer review**. MedRxiv page reports posted September 23, 2026. Direct page/PDF requests returned 403; the title, abstract, full Methods/Results/Discussion, and supplementary Appendix B were retrieved and inspected via indexed primary full-text output, but not saved locally. The medRxiv page says supplementary material contains operating instructions, standardized prompts, output templates, and version control; Appendix B (“RoB2-Evaluator Operating Instructions, Prompting Guide, and Output Templates”) was available in the indexed full text.

**Citation metadata:** Joseph Jithin Thekkelkuthiyathottil, Rashmi Vishwanath, Jyothi Vettiyattusserril Sisirkumar. *RoB2-Evaluator: Development and Technical Evaluation of a Rule-Constrained Large Language Model System for Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 Assessment*. medRxiv. 2026. doi:10.64898/2026.09.22.26363693. Cite as a preprint, not a peer-reviewed publication.

**Inspected:** Abstract; Methods/system development and calibration; post-freeze evaluation; Results (“Agreement with Human Reviewers,” “Conceptual Alignment,” “Methodological-Rationale Stability”); Discussion and Limitations; Data and Materials Availability; supplementary Appendix B, especially A1–A5 and the “RoB2-Evaluator Study Assessment Prompt.”

- The system used GPT-5.2 with a fixed RoB 2 methodological knowledge base and locked instruction framework. Seven trials were used for calibration; the framework was frozen at Version 3; ten separate, purposively selected RCTs were used for post-freeze evaluation. The supplied system prompt directs application of RoB 2 signaling questions and decision pathways and to derive Overall from domain judgments. The supplement documents textual instructions and a custom GPT configuration; the inspected material does **not establish a separate deterministic engine computing domain judgments from structured answers**. Do not equate “rule-constrained” prompt/knowledge-base design with code-enforced answer-to-judgment computation.
- Two independent human reviewers supplied consensus ratings for the 10 evaluation trials; overall agreement was 9/10 (90%), unweighted κ 0.85 (95% CI 0.56–1.00), weighted κ 0.93 (0.64–1.00). The paper reports 85.7% full conceptual alignment (48/56 domain comparisons) and 94.3% rationale consistency across repeat assessments (50/53). The latter is temporal rationale consistency, not human accuracy. The system was reassessed after one week. Three studies per design-specific extension limit those estimates (Abstract; Results; Limitations).
- The source says trial full texts and any available protocol, SAP, registry, and supplements were supplied; external web browsing/database access was not used (Appendix B A2–A3). This supports a claim about supplied source materials, not quotation-level source-span verification or an immutable source audit bundle.
- I did not locate a statement in the inspected text that the human reviewers were blinded to AI output. Therefore “independent human consensus assessment” is supported; “blinded assessment” / “assessed without AI output” should not be asserted without checking the publisher-hosted supplement directly. No same-model unconstrained comparator was used, and the authors say this prevents isolating the effect of the architecture (Discussion).
- Safe manuscript wording: “A 2026 medRxiv preprint reports a GPT-5.2 system using fixed RoB 2 guidance and a locked prompt framework, calibrated on seven RCTs and evaluated after freezing on ten separate trials against consensus ratings from two independent reviewers.” Qualify its reported agreement as preliminary preprint findings. Avoid “first rule-constrained system,” “first independently evaluated system,” or claims that it uses deterministic code execution or blinded reviewers.

## Hevia et al., ROBoto2 (EMNLP 2025 System Demonstrations)

**Primary sources:** [ACL Anthology article page](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-demos.2/); [full proceedings PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-demos.2.pdf); DOI [10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-demos.2](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-demos.2). Retrieved PDF: `/tmp/arbiter-fulltexts/roboto2-2025.pdf`; extracted inspection text: `/tmp/arbiter-fulltexts/roboto2-2025.txt`.

**Citation metadata:** Anthony Hevia, Sanjana Chintalapati, Veronica Ka Wai Lai, Thanh Tam Nguyen, Wai-Tat Wong, Terry P Klassen, Lucy Lu Wang. “ROBOTO2: An Interactive System and Dataset for LLM-assisted Clinical Trial Risk of Bias Assessment.” In *Proceedings of EMNLP 2025: System Demonstrations*, pp. 12–25. ACL, 2025.

**Inspected:** Full 14-page PDF. Key passages: pp. 12–13 (abstract, introduction, system logic); pp. 14–15 (pipeline and manual annotations); p. 15 (inter-rater reliability); pp. 16–17 (experimental evaluation and use); Appendix D, p. 23 (human consensus procedure); Appendix C, p. 22 (prompt); ACL metadata page.

- ROBoto2 combines PDF parsing, within-document retrieval, LLM answers/rationales for signaling questions, and RoB 2 logic. The paper explicitly says final risk is produced by implementing the RoB 2 flowchart (pp. 13–14). It is therefore a direct precedent for an interactive, retrieval-based, human-in-loop RoB 2 system with algorithmic aggregation.
- The released dataset contains 521 trial assessments: 245 manually completed with the Cochrane Excel tool (macros implement RoB 2 logic) and 276 annotated using ROBoto2 assistance. Five expert annotators had relevant graduate degrees and systematic-review experience (p. 15). The model benchmarking and human reference use the 245 manual subset; the 276 LLM-assisted cases were withheld from ROBoto2 evaluation because of the assistance used to create them (pp. 15–16).
- Twenty reports (440 signaling questions) were independently double-annotated to estimate inter-rater agreement: four-class Cohen’s κ=0.40. Reviewers then met, revisited Cochrane Handbook/official guidance, and reached full agreement across those questions/domains (p. 15 and Appendix D, p. 23). The full manual-reference set was produced by five annotators; it is not accurate to describe all 245 as independently double-rated or consensus-adjudicated.
- The system’s interactive annotation counts show annotators supplied their own answer 42.4% of the time and edited rationales 28.7% of the time; they used the model answer 57.6% and rationale 71.3% of the time (p. 17). This characterizes user interaction, not a blinded independent human panel evaluating final system judgments.
- Safe manuscript wording: “ROBoto2 is a public interactive RoB 2 workflow combining PDF parsing, within-document retrieval, LLM-generated signaling answers/evidence, implemented RoB 2 logic, and expert review/editing.” Its paper does not establish ARBITER-like identical-input same-model minimal-prompt comparison, nor source-span immutability/verification; do not infer those absences more broadly than the paper allows.

## Cross-source claim corrections and safe framing

1. **Rule computation is not novel in the broad sense.** Choi explicitly implements the answer-to-domain algorithm in code for auditing/recomputation, and ROBoto2 explicitly implements RoB 2 flowchart logic for final assessment. Choi’s ROBoto2 observations also show why implementation claims and behavior claims should be distinguished: in their runs, the displayed answers, skip logic, and domain judgments were not always coherent.
2. **Rule-constrained systems and human consensus precede ARBITER.** RoB2-Evaluator reports a locked instruction/knowledge-base system and two-reviewer consensus on a 10-trial evaluation. Its inspected supplementary prompt describes textual constraints and supplied sources, not verified deterministic computation from structured answers. The paper does not support a claim of blinded human ratings as far as inspected.
3. **Do not call the comparator “all prior structured prompts.”** Choi’s comparison is a one-trial repeated-run design with two same-base Gemini configurations plus ROBoto2; ROBoto2 is not a plain same-model prompt-only control. RoB2-Evaluator has no same-model no-system comparator. Each is a precedent for selected pieces, not an equivalent head-to-head evaluation.
4. **Most defensible ARBITER distinction:** describe its implemented behavior and its particular evaluation bundle (same model and supplied source files under ARBITER vs deliberately minimal-prompt condition; frozen independent ratings and blinded label-choice assessment if supported by ARBITER records). State clearly that this compares complete workflows and does not isolate the rule engine or establish superiority to ROBoto2 or RoB2-Evaluator.
