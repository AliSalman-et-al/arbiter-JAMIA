# Literature research: automated RoB 2 and LLM-assisted risk-of-bias assessment

**Status: ISSUES — useful primary literature and full texts are available, but the manuscript’s contribution must be framed narrowly.** The main issue is a directly relevant 2026 preprint, *RoB2-Evaluator*, which describes a frozen, rule-constrained RoB 2 system and independent human comparison. Accordingly, avoid claiming ARBITER is the first rule-constrained or human-evaluated RoB 2 system. Its defensible contribution is the particular combination of a same-model minimal-prompt comparison on identical trial-source files, independently frozen ratings plus blinded label-choice assessment, and a workflow whose rule engine checks recorded answers while preserving source-bound audit records. These features are not all unique individually, and the existing studies differ in populations, inputs, references, and system design.

This is a literature and claim audit, not a manuscript edit. I read `GLOSSARY.md`, `docs/manuscript/manuscript.md`, `docs/manuscript/references.md`, `analysis/data-curated/prior-work-comparison.csv`, `analysis/data-curated/prior-work-comparison-notes.md`, `docs/research/llm-rob2/SYNTHESIS.md`, and the underlying `docs/research/llm-rob2/` study memos. ## Synthesis and recommended claim boundaries

1. **Avoid absolute novelty.** ROBoto2 already supplies an interactive, tool-oriented RoB 2 workflow with retrieval and human correction. Choi et al. explicitly implemented the RoB 2 algorithm in code and measured answer-judgment conformance, skip-logic errors, and post hoc recomputation; they also compared the same base Gemini model under two manual-delivery workflows. RoB2-Evaluator describes a frozen rule-constrained workflow with an independent-human assessment followed by consensus. Say ARBITER *combines* particular operational safeguards and *evaluates* a complete system against a same-model minimal-prompt condition across 102 trials, with a distinct forced-choice endpoint. Do not claim first algorithm implementation, rule-conformance evaluation, recomputation, or independent expert comparison.
2. **State the algorithm distinction precisely.** Eisele-Metzger checked Overall-level compliance; Huang embedded decision-tree logic in prompts and post hoc derivation; Choi et al. coded RoB 2 aggregation and evaluated whether outputs conformed to that code; ROBoto2 is designed to apply flowchart aggregation, but Choi observed that its entered judgments could diverge from its own answers; RoB2-Evaluator used locked instruction constraints. ARBITER’s narrower distinction is that its server computes a proposed domain judgment from recorded signaling answers and records any override, within the evaluated same-model workflow. The manuscript correctly qualifies that model interpretation is not made infallible and that overrides are permitted. Distinguish rule implementation, observed conformance, source-bound evidence checks, and benchmark design instead of treating “rules in code” as a single binary feature.
3. **Separate evidence occurrence from evidence support.** RobotReviewer highlights rationale text; ROBoto2 retrieves supporting passages; newer systems produce rationales or snippets. ARBITER’s source-span existence check is a meaningful, inspectable property, but it proves that text occurs in a stored source, not that it semantically supports the answer. Keep this explicit in the Introduction and Methods.
4. **Treat published review labels as imperfect references.** Eisele-Metzger explicitly discusses imperfect Cochrane references; Sahu’s 2026 methodological letter and response discuss the issue; Gandhi and the RoB2-Evaluator preprint use new human assessment/reference procedures. Therefore say prior studies *often* relied on published labels and that this benchmark adds separately collected expert ratings and a forced-choice comparison; do not claim reference noise was previously unrecognized or that this study establishes a universal gold standard.
5. **Keep the comparison conditional.** The ARBITER-vs-Unassisted contrast uses the same Sonnet model and supplied files but different instructions, tool access, runtime, and processing. It evaluates the whole workflow. It does not isolate the rule engine, evidence retrieval, prompt skill, or individual components, and it does not show superiority to structured-prompt systems such as Huang, interactive systems such as ROBoto2, or RoB2-Evaluator.
6. **Do not rank study percentages.** Different RoB versions, trial populations, units, reference standards, source availability, prompts, and scoring rules make raw accuracy/kappa non-comparable. The manuscript’s paired benchmark results and explicit reference dependence are more informative than cross-study rankings.
