# Editorial contract and initial diagnosis

Audience correction is direct user evidence: readers are medical research experts familiar with risk of bias, systematic reviews and meta-analysis, with less specialist AI/software knowledge. It does not mean every technical term needs replacement. Precision is part of readability.

## Format

Use body H2 sections Introduction, Materials and methods, Results, Discussion. Introduction contains H3 Background and significance and Objective, satisfying the components requested by JAMIA's official Research and Applications instructions (checked 23 October 2026, https://academic.oup.com/jamia/pages/General_Instructions, article-type paragraph). Discussion retains H3 Conclusion. Preserve front matter, five-part structured abstract and back matter. This gives an IMRaD reading sequence without claiming that JAMIA requires exactly four headings.

## Initial diagnosis to test against exemplars

The opening currently defines what a risk-of-bias judgment is for readers who already know. Repeated statements that evidence links, rules and agreement establish different properties are defensible but give the article a tutorial cadence. Definitions of tokens and the five RoB domains add elementary material. The literature often occupies separate descriptive sentences rather than developing the specific uncertainty the study addresses. These are editorial hypotheses, not permission to remove methodological qualifications.

Retain terms such as signalling questions, domain judgments, interrater agreement, conditional estimand, bootstrap, calibration, source provenance and algorithm conformance when they are the precise established term. Define AI-specific concepts briefly where needed. A cryptographic hash can be named and its role stated once rather than relabeled throughout as a digital fingerprint. Software detail earns space when it explains reproducibility, the comparison or inspection of an assessment.

Do not force long sentences into an arbitrary word limit, strip all passive constructions or convert this research article into a tutorial. Vary sentence length while keeping referents, agents and conditions clear. Follow clinical-writing SCFL order: structure, clarity, flow, language, then structure again. Schimel's abstraction guidance supports grounding unfamiliar concepts, not explaining concepts already shared with the reader. Heard's clarity goal is transfer of the scientific argument; Zinsser's pruning applies to redundancy, not loss of technical meaning.

## Scientific invariants

Formal primary label-choice match remains conditional: 80 arm-discordant cells, 60 agreed choices, 20 split choices, 53/60 matching ARBITER; individual and split-loss sensitivities remain. Do not describe source-blinded label choice as endorsement of the system or rationale. Independent expert agreement across all 510 cells is secondary and carries explanatory weight. Preserve both experts, D3 adverse five-level contrast and coding reversal, smaller D5 gain against Expert2, Published-reference direction, and computational burden. Complete-workflow comparison cannot isolate components or validate reviewer benefit. Structural evidence checks do not assess semantic support. Fixed-review resampling is conditional on the observed experts/runs. No claim of prospective confirmatory status.

Preserve all unresolved method/declaration fields. Evaluated and public releases are distinct; command correspondence does not establish complete-release identity. No new experiments, subgroup analyses, statistical values or figure changes. Source-specific comparisons must identify design differences; never rank studies by headline performance.

## Literature and style targets

Use actual full texts with verified identities. Select 3–5 exemplars after the swarm reports. A writing model supplies structure and register, not wording to copy or an obligatory citation. Riaz authorship roles are evidence about collaboration, not certainty about his personal preferences. Review the existing corpus before adding papers; new references must materially support a needed proposition.

## Book-grounded register check

Parent inspection of Schimel chapters 14.3.2 and 15.1/15.1.1 distinguishes a useful technical concept from jargon according to the reader's existing schema. His ecology example shows that replacing a precise established term with a general everyday phrase can confuse specialists. That is directly relevant to the user's correction. The revised draft should therefore keep established RoB and statistical terms and define unfamiliar computational roles selectively. The clinical-writing revision guide makes the same distinction for fixed scientific concepts and cautions against sacrificing meaning for word count. These are substantive register decisions, not a synonym blacklist.
