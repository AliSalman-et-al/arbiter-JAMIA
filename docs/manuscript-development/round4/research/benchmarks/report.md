## Search and citation trail

Fresh searches on 23 October 2026 covered RoBBR dataset/paper, ROBIN dataset/paper, RoBGuard and RoB-Item/RoB-Domain, and LogiMed-RoB; publisher/proceedings/arXiv records and primary full texts were checked. Citation trails in LogiMed-RoB's related-work table and references led back to ROBIN (Dias et al.), RoBBR (Wang et al.), RoBGuard (Ji et al.), ROBOTO2, and RobotReviewer. RoBBR's own references and appendices were followed for source review labels, support-judgment annotation, and sample construction. ROBIN was checked through the publisher record, PubMed and its arXiv v1; RoBGuard and RoBBR through ACL Anthology proceedings PDFs; LogiMed-RoB through arXiv v1. The search was targeted, not systematic or exhaustive.

The most consequential distinction is not a score comparison. “Reference” and “evidence” mean different things across these resources: labels copied from review authors, review-domain/item annotations, deterministic derivations from signalling answers, and evidence spans or sentence matches are not interchangeable criterion standards. The datasets also mix RoB 1 and RoB 2, domains and items, manual and distant/inferred annotation, and different sampling units. Their reported results therefore cannot be ranked against ARBITER's paired workflow estimates.

## Findings and claim-specific recommendations

### 1. RoBBR is a relevant lineage, but its task is mostly RoB 1 and its label is inherited

Wang et al., “Measuring Risk of Bias in Biomedical Reports: The RoBBR Benchmark,” *Proceedings of EMNLP 2025*, pp. 3220–3248, distinguish a main risk classification task from support sentence retrieval and support judgment selection. The main task combines Cochrane RoB 1 and RoB 2 labels, with harmonized low/high/unclear-or-some-concern classes; its sentence-retrieval and support-selection tasks are explicitly limited to Cochrane RoB 1 because RoB 2 reviews do not require the same written support judgments. Their support judgments are review-authored explanations, decomposed into aspects; GPT-4-assisted mapping of aspects to sentences was manually checked, and a small sampled subset was double annotated. Thus this is neither a uniform RoB 2 item-answer benchmark nor a human adjudication of whether each ARBITER answer is semantically supported.

**Recommendation (Introduction):** If space allows, acknowledge RoBBR alongside the existing RobotReviewer/ROBOTO2 lineage as a benchmark that separates risk classification from support retrieval/selection. Specify that the present question concerns a retained RoB 2 answer-to-rating workflow and its paired comparison. Do not state that ARBITER is the first system to expose evidence or assess support.

### 2. ROBIN's dataset is distant supervision from Cochrane, not fresh item-level human reference

Dias et al., “RoBIn: A Transformer-based model for risk of bias inference with machine reading comprehension,” *Journal of Biomedical Informatics* 166 (2025), article 104819, DOI `10.1016/j.jbi.2025.104819`, describe a public dataset constructed by mapping Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews assessments to open-access PubMed trial reports. Full arXiv v1 text was inspected. Although the paper uses RoB 2 terminology for domains/signalling questions, its task is a binary low versus high/unclear classification based on legacy bias-type categories; it should not be represented as a RoB 2 signalling-answer dataset. The Cochrane support text may be paraphrased or reviewer commentary; matching uses cosine similarity thresholding, followed by one-author curation. The paper describes an 80/20 split after validation and supplies no fresh per-item expert adjudication. Treat the labels as transferred/distant labels, not a newly adjudicated per-question reference. The journal's publisher-indexed full-text sections were inspected; full arXiv v1 is locally captured. I could not verify a standalone DOI for the dataset itself.

**Recommendation (Discussion):** This supports the existing caveat that published-review labels differ from fresh independent assessments. If named, use it to illustrate label transfer and evidence extraction under a legacy/binary classification setup; avoid presenting it as a matched RoB 2 reference or a validation of semantic support.

### 3. RoBGuard already combines item/domain resources, reformulation, multimodal parsing, and model collaboration

Ji et al., “RoBGuard: Enhancing LLMs to Assess Risk of Bias in Clinical Trial Documents,” *Proceedings of COLING 2025*, pp. 1258–1277. ACL Anthology metadata lists no DOI; the article PDF was downloaded and inspected in full. The authors collect Cochrane reviews from July 2019 to June 2024 and identify 12 reviews with public detailed assessment data on Zenodo. Their final datasets include RoB-Item records for 53 RCTs and RoB-Domain records for 319 RCTs retrieved from PMC (the source review pool is larger). Labels/rationales originate in those review assessments; they are not a fresh independent expert panel collected by the RoBGuard paper. RoBGuard adds expert-question reformulation, multimodal document parsing, and a researcher/clinician/reviewer multi-expert model module. The experiments use the RoB-Item dataset for item-level outputs and review-provided domain labels for domain-level assessment. Its paper reports accuracy but does not establish that linked or generated evidence semantically supports each answer.

This omission in the manuscript's short system lineage is material: RoBGuard makes a broad first/novelty claim about evidence-linked or item-level RoB 2 systems untenable. It does not negate the narrower benchmark question because its target labels, source construction and comparison differ.

**Recommendation (Introduction):** Add one concise citation clause after ROBOTO2 naming RoBGuard as another item/domain RoB 2 resource/system using multimodal parsing and model collaboration. Then retain the specific gap as evaluation of this complete ARBITER implementation against the minimal-prompt arm and independent experts. Avoid an inventory of its modules or any cross-study performance comparison.

### 4. LogiMed-RoB separates RoB 2 rule hierarchy from RoB 1 evidence faithfulness

Huang et al., “Can LLMs Follow Medical Expert Logic? A Benchmark for Hierarchical Logical Consistency in Risk-of-Bias Assessment,” arXiv:2609.11185, v1 (10 September 2026; no DOI shown on the arXiv record). Full v1 PDF inspected, with local copy at `/tmp/arbiter-r4-fulltexts/benchmarks/LogiMed-RoB-v1.pdf`. Track A uses 626 outcome-level RoB 2 instances (467 from Cochrane and 159 from Figshare), with signalling questions and outputs assessed for answer activation, domain derivation and overall aggregation. Track B is expressly RoB 1: 1,048 evidence-faithfulness items drawn from ROBIN (713) and RoBBR (335), with a window-cropping approach and overlap-based evidence metrics. The paper says the RoB 2 source assessments were manually extracted by authors including licensed physicians and calibrated to published conclusions; those are existing review assessments, not a new de novo independent panel. It says roughly 20% of the RoB 1 windows were rechecked, with 93.5% agreement on window accuracy. Its model experiment uses a single run at temperature zero and acknowledges residual provider nondeterminism and no repeated-run variance estimate.

This is direct counterevidence to any broad claim that RoB 2 hierarchy/conformance has not been audited. But its *construct* differs from ARBITER's software audit: LogiMed-RoB assesses whether model-generated answers and direct labels cohere under the decision hierarchy; ARBITER checks its persisted domain outputs against executable mapping rules. Its RoB 1 evidence track is not semantic validation of ARBITER's RoB 2 evidence-answer pairs.

**Recommendation (Discussion):** Keep a clear distinction between algorithm conformance and model cross-level consistency. A single sentence can acknowledge LogiMed-RoB's newer hierarchy audit and clarify that ARBITER's all-510 rule result verifies the answer-to-rating transition, not answer correctness or evidence support. Also retain the one-run limitation; the newer benchmark itself does not supply repeated-run stability evidence.

### 5. Evaluation designs vary in unit, sampling and repeat structure

Across these papers the unit ranges from RoBBR study/domain labels and sentence/aspect mappings, through RoBGuard review-derived RCT item/domain records, to LogiMed-RoB outcome-level trial instances and individual question queries. RoBBR's development/evaluation partitions are organized around review data; RoBGuard is limited by the subset of Cochrane reviews that publicly expose item-level data and PMC retrieval; LogiMed-RoB pools publicly available sources and separately reports its RoB 1 and RoB 2 tracks. RoBIn transfers Cochrane labels to open-access papers. These selection processes create different target populations from ARBITER's ordered, availability-dependent sampling of 102 trials from 16 fixed reviews. Most cited system papers evaluate one model output per test instance; that is distinct from ARBITER's paired arms and trial-cluster bootstrap, which is conditional on one run per arm. LogiMed-RoB explicitly states it lacks repeated-run variance estimates.

**Recommendation (Methods/Discussion):** Do not add a dataset-size or headline-score comparison. Keep ARBITER's own review/trial selection and one-run conditions adjacent to its estimate. If the literature is summarized, state the differences in source labels, task unit, instrument and repeated-run design rather than describing these benchmarks as interchangeable validation sets.

## Strongest counterevidence and boundary

Strongest counterevidence to broad novelty or an unexamined-process claim comes from (a) RoBGuard's RoB 2 item and domain datasets and multimodal/model-collaborative system and (b) LogiMed-RoB's explicit RoB 2 atomic/domain/aggregation consistency evaluation. RoBBR also defeats a broad assertion that separating final classification from evidence retrieval/support tasks is new, though its support tasks are RoB 1. These sources do **not** evaluate ARBITER's exact persisted evidence-answer-rating record, compare its full workflow with the specified same-model minimal prompt, or establish that its citations semantically support its answers. The defensible contribution remains the bounded implementation and its paired benchmark, with expert agreement, published-label comparison, rule checking and structural evidence checks kept as distinct outcomes.

## Exemplar passages by locator (paraphrased)

1. **RoBBR, §4.1, pp. 3222–3223; Appendix B, annotator training.** The authors first state why support-judgment retrieval is limited to RoB 1, then specify that its main task includes both RoB versions and that non-Cochrane reviews supply a different label-only test set. The useful practice is to place instrument and source-label boundaries next to the dataset claim, rather than presenting one aggregate benchmark as homogeneous.
2. **LogiMed-RoB, §3.3.1 and Table 2, PDF pp. 4–5; Limitations, PDF p. 9.** The authors separate the RoB 2 hierarchy track from RoB 1 textual-faithfulness track, then identify the source and provenance of each. Later they explicitly limit interpretation of a single T=0 run and note residual API nondeterminism. This is a useful model for reporting distinct constructs and run limitations without collapsing them into a single “accuracy” claim.

These are structural examples only; do not reuse their language or unsupported promotional claims.
