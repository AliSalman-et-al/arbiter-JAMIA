## Synthesis by claim

### D3 disagreement: plausible interpretive variability, but no case-specific diagnosis

Minozzi et al. 2020 is directly relevant RoB 2 evidence: four raters applied RoB 2 independently to primary outcomes of 70 RCTs; the abstract reports slight overall Fleiss kappa (0.16), fair agreement for D3 (0.22, 95% CI 0.14–0.30), and difficulties related to subject-matter knowledge, unfamiliar terminology, changed domain approaches and signaling-question formulation. Because the article is closed in the verified repositories checked, this is **abstract-only**, not a full-text conclusion. It contextualizes that disagreement, including D3 disagreement, is not unusual in RoB 2 application; it cannot explain the current study’s Expert 1/Expert 2 pattern or say which assessor was right.

The stronger full-text account of possible mechanisms is Bertizzolo et al. 2019, which studied repeated RoB 1 judgments for the same RCTs in different Cochrane reviews. Among the disagreements they examined, the most common source was different interpretation of the same information, often where reporting was incomplete or unclear. This provides evidence that such a mechanism occurs in review practice. It does not establish that it caused this manuscript’s D3 disagreement: different studies, different reviewers and the earlier tool were involved. In the present benchmark, the collected evidence does not identify whether the two experts differed over missingness magnitude, reasons, prognostic dependence, result target, external information, prior calibration, or judgment thresholds. Those are hypotheses only and should not be narrated as findings.

Minozzi et al. 2022 (abstract-only) reports that after five-study calibration and creation of a review/intervention-specific implementation document, overall agreement rose in subsequent blocks of studies and completion time fell. The sequence is promising but not a randomized comparison of training exposure; it bundles calibration, a tailored document and accumulated experience. It supports the statement that review-specific instructions/calibration are empirically studied and may matter in context, not that ARBITER's instructions caused its agreement advantage or that the experts in this manuscript lacked training.

### Probability-category collapse: technically aligned to one RoB 2 recommendation, but it changes the estimand

The Cochrane Handbook says “Yes” and “Probably yes” have the same implications for the RoB 2 algorithm, as do “No” and “Probably no”; it explicitly recommends collapsing each pair when agreement measures are calculated for signalling answers. This supports describing a directional binary/collapsed agreement analysis as methodologically motivated. It does not make a five-level measure dispensable: the five-level scale retains distinctions between firm evidence and assessor inference that may be substantively useful. In this manuscript, D3's five-level contrast against Expert 1 is unfavorable (−10.3 percentage points), while the collapsed comparison is favorable (+8.5); the coding changes the question and the sign. The revision should present both, name the coding, and avoid saying collapse “corrects” or resolves D3 disagreement. It is sensible to say the reversal is consistent with differences concentrated in within-direction probability categories only if that is directly supported by the manuscript's component-count analysis; otherwise don't infer the item-level composition from the aggregate sign reversal.

### Conditional forced label choice: legitimate narrow estimand, not preference or workflow endorsement

The full-text methods literature located in this pass did not validate this exact forced-choice procedure. Its interpretation follows principally from the study design and endpoint definition already documented in the baseline. The 53/60 estimand is conditional on arm-discordant cells where both experts made the same blinded offered-label choice. It answers which offered label equals their agreed choice in that selected subset. It does not estimate all-cell preference, independent accuracy, preference for an arm's reasoning, or endorsement of a system. Because labels are presented after independent ratings and choices, anchoring/contrast effects remain possible; source blinding does not remove those effects. Report the 80 arm-discordant cells, 60 agreed choices and 20 split choices beside the conditional numerator/denominator, and retain each expert's separate sensitivity analyses and the split-loss analysis. The endpoint's conditioning and ties make the denominator a defined estimand, not a defect, as long as the prose does not generalize it beyond that subset.

### Fixed-review bootstrap: matches the stated target, conditional inference only

The baseline Methods and `analysis/R/bootstrap.R` show trial-cluster resampling within each of 16 reviews, keeping each review's trial count and carrying paired arms/domains together. This preserves within-trial dependence and the observed review allocation while quantifying trial-level sampling variation **conditional on the 16 selected reviews**. It is coherent for that conditional target; it does not represent resampling a population of reviews, assess between-review variation, or broaden inference to new review domains. No primary full-text statistical-methods paper located in this targeted pass specifically establishes this exact fixed-strata scheme as uniquely preferable for the current estimand. GRRAS is cited in the manuscript, but the local mirror attempt was wrong and the article body was not inspected. Avoid adding an unverified methodological citation or calling the bootstrap generally “representative.” The manuscript's existing interval scope statement is appropriately cautious.

## Claim-specific manuscript recommendations

1. **Introduction / D3 context:** If retaining the Minozzi sentence, specify that prior RoB 2 reliability work found limited interrater agreement and application difficulty, and that a review-specific implementation document/calibration was associated with higher agreement in one study. Keep this as context for why expert references are imperfect and guidance is material; no causal link to the present contrast.
2. **Results / D3:** State the two experts' agreement pattern and the five-level and collapsed answer contrasts together. Say the coding changes what agreement means and that the favorable collapsed estimate does not erase the unfavorable five-level result. Do not assign the disagreement to missingness mechanisms or assessor training without records.
3. **Discussion / disagreement:** Bertizzolo is the strongest source for the general possibility that interpretation of the same reported information contributes to RoB disagreement, but because it is RoB 1 and cross-review, phrase as precedent, not explanation. A next study could record assessor calibration, instructions, target-specific rationale, consulted sources, and blinded item-level adjudication to distinguish mechanisms.
4. **Methods / label-choice endpoint:** Keep the conditional denominator and label-equality scoring adjacent. Use “agreed expert choice matched ARBITER” (or similarly literal wording); do not use “experts preferred,” “endorsed,” or “validated.” Keep selection due to arm disagreement explicit.
5. **Statistical analysis / bootstrap:** Preserve the exact unit and conditioning: sample trials within each fixed review, carrying both arms and five domains together; inference is conditional on these reviews, experts and runs. Do not claim the scheme captures review-level generalization.
6. **Agreement summaries:** Exact agreement and weighted kappa answer different questions. If discussing kappa values, mention that coefficients depend on category marginals/prevalence and weighting conventions; don't replace the paired exact-agreement differences or treat kappa cutoffs as universal quality grades. A potentially relevant 2016 primary method paper was only surfaced in a search snippet, and its DOI/full text was not verified; it is deliberately not recommended for citation.

## Strongest counterevidence and limits

- **Against attributing observed disagreement to calibration:** RoB 2 incorporates assessor judgment by design; Minozzi 2022's calibration and review-specific instructions were bundled and sequential, not randomized, and the current experts' training records are pending. We cannot infer a training deficit or causal component.
- **Against using D3 as a unique weakness of the system:** Minozzi 2020's abstract reports relatively low D3 reliability across expert raters, so D3 disagreement can reflect task/reference difficulty. This does not adjudicate ARBITER or either expert.
- **Against treating the collapsed result as primary truth:** RoB 2's coding equivalence concerns algorithm implications and recommended agreement coding; it does not mean probability categories carry no information. The present sign reversal makes reporting both scales essential.
- **Against a general bootstrap claim:** the resampling targets trial variation in a fixed review set. Alternative hierarchical estimands would require review-level sampling assumptions and enough sampled reviews; the fixed bootstrap cannot answer that question.
- **Against a correct-label reading of forced choice:** the choice compares presented labels in selected disagreements, after the experts already froze independent ratings. It is vulnerable to anchoring and does not supply an external gold standard.

## Medical-research writing exemplars (method-precise practices)

These examples illustrate communicative choices; none is a template for wording.

- **Minozzi et al. 2020, abstract Methods/Results/Conclusion:** The abstract moves from four independent raters and a defined outcome set to one overall reliability estimate, domain estimates, then concrete reported application difficulties. This keeps design, estimates and explanation separate. Access here is abstract only, so do not claim to have examined its full article structure.
- **Minozzi et al. 2022, abstract Methods/Results/Conclusion:** It places the calibration block, tailored document, subsequent assessment blocks, agreement and time changes in one traceable sequence. That is helpful for reporting what was bundled; the design itself cautions against attributing change to one component.
- **Bertizzolo et al. 2019, full text, Abstract and Methods/Results:** It distinguishes disagreement caused by different information from disagreement about the same information, then explains that a subset of disagreements underwent duplicate review. That separation is a strong model for the present Discussion: name a plausible mechanism and its evidence without claiming it was measured here.
- **Cochrane Handbook Chapter 8, §§8.2, 8.5, 8.7:** It defines the assessment target before domain guidance, then distinguishes answer categories and decision implications. Its expert readers need the operational boundary, not elementary definitions of risk of bias.

These are practices to adapt at paragraph level; do not copy source phrasing. The most useful writing lesson is to state what was rated, by whom, under which target and coding before discussing agreement, then mark observed patterns separately from possible mechanisms.
