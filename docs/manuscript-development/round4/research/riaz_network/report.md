## Article-level IMRaD techniques for expert medical readers

These are transferable reporting practices observed in the papers, not claims about an individual's preferred style. The first two are the strongest direct matches to evidence-synthesis readers; FD-SCoPE is a preprint and is included as a current citation-trail example, not a vetted writing authority.

1. **Separate distinct evidence-table tasks before introducing the system.** FD-SCoPE, v1, Introduction paragraphs 1–3 (PDF pp. 2–3): the authors distinguish questions answered by querying recorded fields from questions requiring derivation of an unrecorded clinical attribute, then state the evaluation gap. This gives expert readers a familiar task taxonomy before system names and components. For ARBITER, borrow only a compact contrast where needed—RoB judgment agreement, rule conformance, and evidence-link integrity answer different questions. The current Discussion already makes these distinctions, but its opening frames complete-workflow agreement first; it could preview the distinct evaluands without explaining basic RoB concepts or adding a system-mechanics tutorial.

2. **Define evaluation levels and units in Methods, then keep the named outcome tied to the unit.** FD-SCoPE, Methods §4.1 (PDF p. 11): trial identification, returned-table accuracy, and derived-attribute accuracy are distinguished as separate evaluation levels, with the question as the unit. Its Results §2.4 (PDF pp. 6–7) then keeps feedback-set size, held-out question count, and the type of outcome adjacent to the reported change. ARBITER already reports the 80 discordant cells, 60 agreed choices, and 20 split choices next to the conditional primary result and explicitly identifies its selected subset. Preserve that compact denominator discipline; do not borrow FD-SCoPE's benchmark numbers or imply comparable tasks.

3. **Make the Discussion return to the intended user task and state what the study did not test.** FD-SCoPE, Discussion §3 (PDF p. 9): after a short result synthesis, it defines the system as an interface to curated evidence rather than a clinical decision system and distinguishes its computational evaluation from clinical decision making. This is a useful boundary move, although the manuscript's intended users are reviewers and the evaluated output is RoB 2 assessments, not trial-table queries. ARBITER already ends its human-use paragraph by saying reviewer time, acceptance, and checking require direct observation, and its Conclusion limits the claim to an inspectable pathway and observed agreement. Retain those claims; no new limitation paragraph is needed.

Across these examples, the actionable difference from current prose is small: name each distinct evaluand and target population before interpreting a favorable result, then state the inference boundary in the paragraph where it matters. The current draft substantially does this already, especially in the primary-endpoint, evidence-support, and human-use paragraphs. Avoid importing the broader, more explanatory register of the JCO overview: that single-author special-series essay is aimed at a general oncology audience and is not a matched model for this specialist research article.
