## What the current argument needs to absorb

The manuscript already makes three useful distinctions: a source link versus whether the cited passage supports an answer; rule application versus answer validity; and agreement versus the choice of reference. These are defensible distinctions, but the nearby literature is now richer than the current Introduction implies. The practical contribution should remain this implementation and benchmark, not the general idea of evidence-linked assessment, answer-to-rating rules, or separate process checks.

**Systems already cover more than the two named in the Introduction.** [RobotReviewer](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocv044) coupled an automated RoB 1 judgment with retrieved supporting sentences and evaluated sentence relevance separately from label agreement. [ROBOTO2](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-demos.2) combines document parsing, retrieval, question-level LLM answers/rationales, RoB 2 logic and reviewer correction. [RoBGuard](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.84/) (COLING 2025) adds reformulated RoB 2 items, multimodal parsing, multiple model “experts,” and item/domain datasets; its RoB-Item resource includes answers and supporting rationales. These are not the same retained record or the same paired evaluation as ARBITER, but they make any implication that ARBITER is the first evidence-linked, item-level, multimodal, or reviewer-correctable RoB 2 workflow untenable. **Recommended Introduction adjustment:** keep the concise RobotReviewer/ROBOTO2 lineage, then add one clause or sentence acknowledging RoBGuard's item-level rationale and dataset contribution. Use the remaining space to specify the narrower implementation/evaluation question.

**Conformance is now an explicit evaluation target, and “conformance” names different tests.** [Choi et al.](https://doi.org/10.63528/jebp.2026.00011) directly compare a tool's reported domain judgment with the judgment implied by its own answers, while also measuring repeated-run variability and expert agreement. Their workshop pilot is one trial, one expert and 13/15/10 manually entered runs across configurations. The ARBITER rule audit instead verifies software outputs against its executable mapping, while its comparator is audited separately. [LogiMed-RoB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11185) (arXiv, v1, September 2026) makes a broader hierarchy explicit: valid/active signalling answers, domain derivation, overall aggregation, and evidence-related metrics. The methods table matters: its 860 total includes **201 RoB 2 trials and 659 RoB 1 trials**; the paper's quoted human-evidence passages and its RoB 1 faithfulness track must not be described as a RoB 2 human semantic-support validation. Its RoB 2 track nevertheless directly challenges a broad claim that cross-level consistency is unexamined. It also distinguishes answer-level consistency from domain and overall consistency. **Recommended Discussion adjustment:** contrast ARBITER's deterministic software conformance audit with model-level cross-level consistency evaluations, and state that conformance validates the answer-to-rating transition only. Do not compare study percentages: definitions, models, datasets, reference construction and run designs differ.

**Source location, retrieval quality and semantic support are not interchangeable.** [RobotReviewer](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocv044) is a useful early warning: a blinded panel could rate retrieved text relevance separately, and the later machine-versus-human comparison by [Armijo-Olivo et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/jrsm.1398) reported substantial proportions of irrelevant quotations in several RoB 1 domains (the latter claim was checked at PubMed abstract level only in this pass). [ROBOTO2](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.emnlp-demos.2) asks reviewers to rate retrieved passages and analyzes retrieval, but it does not make a source URL or retrieval hit equivalent to a defensible answer. [Liang et al.](https://doi.org/10.2196/93761) now directly study RoB 2 generated claims against retrieved trial text, but their supported/contradicted/not-found verdict is assigned by another LLM; the corpus is one Cochrane review's 97 RCTs with human RoB 2 annotations. LogiMed-RoB further asks whether performance tracks overlap with expert-selected passages and whether answers still fail when retrieval is strong; its evidence formulation is a distinct overlap/conditional-error diagnostic, not a human semantic adjudication of each ARBITER answer. These approaches expose complementary failure modes and do not supply a common criterion standard for “support.” **Recommended Discussion adjustment:** keep Liang as a close precedent, add a brief RoB 1 quotation-relevance precedent (Marshall 2016 or Armijo-Olivo 2020) and, if space permits, note the newer hierarchical audit. State the next step narrowly: independently adjudicate whether each stored passage supports its linked answer, including image-derived answers. Do not call source binding evidence validity or claim these prior metrics validated ARBITER's semantic support.

**Expert-reference work reinforces reference dependence rather than a universal “gold standard.”** [Huang's corrected 2025 JMIR study](https://doi.org/10.2196/70450) compares model output with both published review assessments and human reviewers; [Gandhi's 2026 validation](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0353155) compares o3 with original review authors and a masked panel; [Nyrhi's 2026 comparative validation](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2026.106238) uses published assessment labels; the early RobotReviewer work also uses published assessments as a proxy. Reference populations and adjudication procedures differ. The manuscript's two independent-first experts, frozen before label exposure, are a distinct and useful reference construction; they are not error-free truth, especially given expert disagreement, unequal source access and the forced-choice stage. **Recommended Discussion adjustment:** retain its existing reference qualification and connect it to this literature in one sentence. Do not call the conditional 53/60 endpoint accuracy or preference for a workflow, and do not infer that it validates reasoning.

**Prompt and system comparisons are not a new category.** [Xiong et al.](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjebm-2025-114055) compare a direct instruction with progressively constrained prompts using RoB 1. [GEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01452) is an additional 2025 preprint: it programmatically optimizes prompts on RoB 1 and compares the result with three manually designed prompts using Claude 3.5 Sonnet. These are not ARBITER's same-model, complete-workflow contrast; nevertheless, the current Introduction should not imply that same-model minimal-versus-more-structured comparisons are unprecedented. ARBITER's arms differ in instructions, tool access and processing, so the result remains package-level. [Joseph et al.'s RoB2-Evaluator](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.22.26363693) is another rule-constrained RoB 2 implementation, but it is a preprint with seven calibration trials and ten post-freeze evaluation trials; its repeat assessments and two-reviewer consensus address questions ARBITER's single run cannot. Keep its publication status and scale explicit.

A [2026 feasibility study of LLMs with ROBUST-RCT](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-44303-z) is adjacent but uses a distinct, newer risk-of-bias instrument rather than RoB 2. It should not be grouped as a direct RoB 2 system comparator. A [2026 RoB 1 human–AI collaboration paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12874-025-02763-3) reports intervention modes, so it reinforces the distinction between offline agreement/runtime and observed reviewer use; it does not establish effects for ARBITER or RoB 2.

## Two writing exemplars for this manuscript

1. **Marshall, Kuiper and Wallace, RobotReviewer, JAMIA 2016 — Background and Significance, paragraphs beginning “Our preliminary work…” through the paragraph ending with the expert-panel procedure.** This is the strongest Introduction example in the close-system set. It turns an established automated approach into a specific unresolved evaluation problem: label accuracy is compared with human-review consistency, while relevance of extracted support is separately judged by blinded reviewers. That paragraph sequence gives each endpoint a reason to exist and makes the reference construction visible before presenting the system. It is useful for a RoB/evidence-synthesis reader because it anticipates the validity question instead of listing technology. Borrow the separation of questions, not its RoB 1 content or its now overconfident workload/replacement implications.

2. **Hevia et al., ROBOTO2, EMNLP System Demonstrations 2025 — §7 “Results & Discussion,” subsection “Potential gains in quality or efficiency,” and §8 “Conclusion,” pp. 17–18.** This is the strongest Discussion example for the human-use boundary. It reports what annotators accepted/edited and what they rated as relevant, then explicitly says the hypothesized effect of repurposed effort on assessment quality was not measured. It ends with a concrete reason that a system-plus-dataset matters without converting a qualitative workflow impression into a time-saving result. That distinction maps directly to ARBITER's runtime versus reviewer workload. Do not borrow its optimistic “quicker completion” projection as an observed effect.

The selection is about passages, not authors' personal styles. It follows the clinical-writing guidance to use an Introduction to define the knowledge boundary and a Discussion to separate observation from implication (Schimel, *Writing Science*, ch. 6, especially §6.3; Heard, *The Scientist’s Guide to Writing*, ch. 10 “The Introduction Section,” p. 84, and ch. 13 “The Discussion Section,” p. 120). The Introduction should synthesize what changes the question rather than inventory papers; the Discussion should make the design boundary part of its interpretation.

## Gaps and limits

The strongest substantive update is LogiMed-RoB: it weakens any broad novelty implication around hierarchical rule verification and evidence-related auditing, while remaining a preprint and using distinct measures/reference tracks. RoBGuard is a peer-reviewed, relevant omitted system. Liang and the older RobotReviewer relevance studies show that “evidence support” currently spans different evaluators and constructs; a human semantic judgment of the passage-to-answer relation remains a meaningful ARBITER evaluation gap. Study-specific evidence is too heterogeneous for a performance ranking. The current draft's core claims remain supportable if it acknowledges RoBGuard, qualifies the novelty of separate checks, and maintains the explicit boundaries around semantic support, repeatability, expert-reference validity, and reviewer benefit.

The search did not establish every emerging RoB dataset/system's public release state or every 2026 article's final version. No assertion of exhaustive coverage, firstness or comparative performance is warranted.
