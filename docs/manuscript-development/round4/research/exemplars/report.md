## JAMIA format finding

Current JAMIA Research and Applications instructions require a structured abstract with **Objective, Materials and Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion** headings (up to 250 words) and a main-text section describing **Background and Significance**. They say the main text should include sections corresponding to the abstract headings plus Background and Significance; they do not prescribe the literal main-text labels “Introduction,” “Methods,” “Results,” and “Discussion.” A conventional IMRaD body is compatible when it supplies those functions and keeps Background and Significance identifiable. A conventional unstructured abstract would not satisfy the stated Research and Applications requirement. The current manuscript's structured abstract and Background and Significance heading follow this requirement. The explicit body Objective and Conclusion headings also mirror the journal examples and avoid ambiguity.

## What the exemplars do

| Paper | Why it is a useful model | Observed narrative practice and audience calibration |
|---|---|---|
| Marshall, Kuiper & Wallace, “RobotReviewer” (JAMIA, 2016) | Direct systematic-review system evaluation and a JAMIA paper; an earlier RoB instrument, so it is a task/model rather than a RoB 2 evidence precedent. | Defines the system's two evaluated functions—judgment classification and supporting-sentence identification—then explains the machine-learning approach only as needed to understand those functions. It makes the separation between judgment agreement and sentence relevance visible in the evaluation design. The clinical/evidence-synthesis premise is brief; methods explain distant supervision and noisy labels because those are specific to the system. It does not teach confidence intervals or clinical trial appraisal from first principles. |
| Hevia et al., “ROBOTO2” (EMNLP System Demonstrations, 2025) | Closest RoB 2 interactive evidence-retrieval system paper. | Opens with systematic-review task context, then states what the system contributes. The body explicitly describes the new pipeline—PDF parsing, passage retrieval, question answering, reviewer edits—and its dataset. It explains the five RoB 2 domains, response options and hierarchical mapping, but those explanations serve an NLP/technical readership less familiar with RoB 2. For ARBITER's expert evidence-synthesis audience, reuse the concise explanation of unfamiliar retrieval/model operations; omit textbook-level RoB 2 recapitulation. |
| Soboczenski et al., “Machine learning to help researchers evaluate biases in clinical trials: a prospective, randomized user study” (BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making, 2019) | Closest human-use/evidence-synthesis evaluation; distinct from the earlier RobotReviewer model-performance paper. | Starts with evidence-synthesis burden, identifies the gap between retrospective model evaluation and reviewer utility, then introduces a randomized within-person user study. In Methods it says what this already-described tool does, then narrows to the four domains and the workflow actually tested. Participants' interaction and the primary time outcome are introduced before the modeling details. Standard review practice is assumed; only the tool's specific suggestion/accept-or-amend behavior and the study's use of it are unpacked. |
| Livingston et al., “Reproducible generative artificial intelligence evaluation for health care: a clinician-in-the-loop approach” (JAMIA Open, 2025) | Strong, current clinician-rated evaluation of a deployed generative AI product; 633 queries and 41 external-to-development subject-matter experts, with a stated author/product relationship. | The Introduction explains unfamiliar AI concepts (RAG, stand-alone LLMs, hallucination) only to motivate why source grounding does not settle clinical correctness. The Methods define the study's five evaluation dimensions and rating scales because those constructs are specific to this study, then detail query sampling and two-reviewer/third-reviewer disagreement handling. The structured abstract and body signpost the same evaluation arc. Its Discussion states strengths and limitations and discloses the commercial connection; those are useful transparency models, not a transferable performance benchmark. |
| Kumar et al., “OrderRex clinical user testing” (JAMIA, 2020) | JAMIA randomized evaluation of a clinical ML decision-support interface using simulated clinical cases. | Opens with the clinical workflow problem, moves to what earlier offline evaluations leave unanswered, then states the user-facing question. It introduces the recommender through a familiar clinical task before explaining what suggestions appear and how they update. Methods describe the simulated interface and randomization sufficiently to understand the contrast; results lead with the clinical-appropriateness endpoint and precision estimates. The discussion distinguishes acceptability, order quality, clicks and case time rather than treating one as a proxy for another. This is an example of explaining the system behavior and decision context without an algorithm tutorial. |

Across the two closest RoB papers, the RoB 2 concepts are unpacked because the target readership includes NLP/system builders. The clinical informatics papers assume more of the clinical workflow and common statistical machinery, while spelling out the model's role, what clinicians see, how ratings are generated, and what the evaluation actually measures. For this manuscript, the reader should be told that the model interprets trial reports and answers signalling questions, that software applies the RoB 2 algorithms, and what evidence/record checks do. RoB 2 itself, the five familiar domains, exact agreement, confidence intervals, and routine paired comparisons need not be taught as introductory concepts. Keep the custom estimand, trial-within-review resampling, conditionality, and structural checks precise because they materially affect interpretation.

## Specific diagnosis of over-explanation and repetition

The current manuscript is already substantially more expert-facing than the round 3 teaching-style draft. The recommendations below target duplicated work, not technical depth or sentence length for its own sake.

1. **Background and Significance, paragraphs 1–3, followed by Objective (lines 37–45).** Paragraph 1 explains the familiar RoB 2 chain of signalling questions and algorithms, then adds reliability context and states the preservation problem. Paragraph 2 reviews relevant system precedents and concludes that the question now concerns a specified implementation. Paragraph 3 restates the same gap as three properties (source occurrence versus support; algorithm execution versus answer quality; agreement versus reference quality), then says ARBITER preserves the pathway and asks whether it agrees more. The Objective repeats that ARBITER-preservation/comparison question and lists its outcomes. The exact relation between source integrity, semantic support, procedure and agreement is important, but the reader is made to traverse it multiple times before Methods. **Revision direction:** preserve one paragraph of RoB 2/reliability context and one paragraph synthesizing prior systems by function, then make the last Introduction paragraph state the bounded gap and question once. Keep the Objective compact, naming the design/outcome hierarchy without re-arguing the rationale. Remove the three-property mini-tutorial if the key distinction can be integrated into the prior-work synthesis and revisited where the actual process check is reported.

2. **System design and process outcomes (lines 51–55 and 85).** The Methods correctly explain unfamiliar operations: source fingerprints, direct quotation extraction, source locations, image linking, the model's image descriptions, and proposed versus adopted ratings. The last sentence of system design (“This record makes the basis ... traceable without establishing...”) recaps both the traceability claim and the semantic-support boundary already stated in the Introduction. Methods later repeats the semantic limitation in the process-outcomes definition (“These structural checks did not assess whether the evidence justified the answer”). These have distinct jobs only if the first says what the system records and the latter defines what the audit measured. **Revision direction:** retain the operational account and one precise Methods statement delimiting audit scope; remove the system-design summary sentence. Do not remove how proposed and adopted ratings differ or how images are handled; these are unfamiliar implementation details, not basics.

3. **Discussion opening (lines 113–117) replays Results.** The first paragraph repeats both expert-specific 510-cell denominators and paired gains; the next replays the 80 disagreements, 60 consensus choices, 20 splits and 53/60 estimate, then states the conditional endpoint's limitations and that full-cohort results add context. A Discussion opening should synthesize the principal finding and distinguish the endpoints, but need not reproduce every Results count and explain that the second result is broader again. **Revision direction:** make one opening paragraph carry (a) the direction of the full-cohort expert comparisons, (b) the conditional primary result with its exact eligible population, and (c) the boundary that chosen-label matching is not endorsement. Leave detailed rates in Results and avoid separately restating their hierarchy.

4. **Repeated procedural distinction across Discussion paragraphs 3–5 and Methods (lines 85, 121–129).** The AVATAR paragraph says a reviewer can inspect evidence and states how quotations, source links, rules, proposed ratings and overrides work. The following paragraph again says rule-conformance checks passed but semantic support was not assessed, then introduces Liang as evidence-support precedent. Both distinctions are consequential, but system behavior belongs in Methods; Discussion should interpret the observed check and the unresolved support question. The next paragraph again says rule conformance does not identify which component caused the agreement difference, a point already explicit in the Methods arm description and first Discussion limitation paragraph. **Revision direction:** keep the AVATAR example as a concise illustration of an actual evidence interpretation; summarize the audit's limited result once; then use the evidence-support paper to motivate the remaining evaluation gap. Remove operational replay and avoid repeating the component-attribution caveat in adjacent paragraphs.

5. **Domain/reference and bootstrap caveats (lines 131–139).** The D3 paragraph appropriately interprets a materially discordant result and a coding-sensitive signalling comparison. The Published paragraph repeats its exact Results difference before offering possible explanations; the following paragraph repeats expert imperfection and explains that separate interval overlap does not test the contrast. The Generalization paragraph restates the Methods' conditional bootstrap scope nearly verbatim. **Revision direction:** keep the D3 result and the warning that collapsing categories changes the question. In the Published-label paragraph, lead with the interpretive contrast and uncertainty without reprinting every Results value. State the fresh-reference and target-binding caveats once, close to their interpretation. In Generalization, retain fixed-review selection, two experts, single run and availability constraints, but do not repeat the CI's bootstrap definition already given in Statistical Analysis.

6. **Conclusion (lines 131–133) echoes Abstract and Discussion.** The last lines restate inspectability, greater expert agreement and domain/reference dependence after the Discussion has already established each. This normal end-position reinforcement need not become a second abstract. **Revision direction:** reduce to the bounded implication that an inspectable workflow had higher expert agreement in this benchmark, with no claim of semantic validity or reviewer benefit.

7. **Methods concepts to assume versus explain.** The outcome/statistical Methods explain “exact agreement” as a proportion and “paired percentage-point differences” as matches in the same cells, and the Process outcomes list the five RoB 2 domains. For this stated readership, those are familiar clinical and statistical concepts. They can be named without didactic definition or a complete domain catalogue, unless an exact operational choice needs them. Preserve the custom definition of a component, excluded answer categories, the primary denominator, Published-label target caveat, and bootstrap conditioning: those details determine what the estimates mean. The sentence defining a token is also expendable if “token counts” remains intelligible from the API cost method; alternatively explain the model-call basis only once. Do not simplify away number, denominator or uncertainty.

## Writing-principle grounding

The cited books are craft guidance, not empirical evidence about JAMIA readers. I inspected the local full-text passages rather than relying on the skill's summaries:

- **Schimel, *Writing Science*, opening/audience and OCAR passages:** specialist-journal readers are described as patient and wanting to evaluate the argument and evidence as they develop; a short article for a narrow expert audience can remind readers briefly of an already-known problem. His schema discussion warns that explaining a schema experts already hold can feel like writing below their knowledge. That supports cutting basic RoB 2 exposition while preserving the exact gap and evidence chain. The point is audience calibration, not “make every sentence short.”
- **Heard, *The Scientist's Guide to Writing*, reader-centered clarity and reading-as-a-writer passages:** writing clarity is effort saved for the reader; he cautions against converting general advice into a long mechanical rule list and recommends noticing how actual papers guide readers. I use this to diagnose needless re-traversal of the same distinction, not to prescribe fragmentary paragraphs or remove detail that supports reproducibility.
- **Zinsser, *On Writing Well*, “Clutter” and “The Audience”:** his useful test is whether a sentence or phrase repeats the preceding sentence or supplies information the reader already knows; he also rejects over-trimming a natural train of thought into tiny paragraphs. That test identifies the repeated source-support, rule-conformance, comparator, and endpoint explanations above. It does not justify a universal sentence-length or paragraph-length formula.

These principles align with the observed exemplars: retain routine domain knowledge, define the unfamiliar system operations and study-specific estimands, and make each paragraph advance the argument by a distinct step.

## Limits and gaps

The focused set is not a comprehensive survey of clinical informatics writing. ROBOTO2 is a conference system demonstration rather than a journal article, and its more technical RoB explanations reflect a different audience. The ClinicalKey AI study is from JAMIA Open and has a disclosed product-development relationship; use it for methodological structure and transparent limitations, not neutral external replication. The RobotReviewer user study assesses RoB 1 and cannot be treated as an estimate of human benefit for ARBITER. None of these articles licenses cross-study performance ranking or an ARBITER utility claim. The main remaining editorial judgment is how much implementation detail can move to eMethods while preserving a readable Methods account; that is outside this exemplar slice.
