## What earns manuscript space

1. Retain the RoB 2 instrument and human reliability context. Keep RoB 1 findings explicitly identified as earlier-instrument evidence. Do not suggest that any assessor is an error-free reference.
2. Acknowledge RobotReviewer and ROBoto2 specifically for evidence display, interactive review and answer-derived ratings. These are established features, not ARBITER priority claims.
3. Discuss Choi's existing citation as a direct conformance/repeated-assessment precedent. Its one-trial repeated-run design and ARBITER's multi-trial single-run design answer different questions. Avoid percentage comparisons and blanket claims that recomputation improves all configurations.
4. Replace the general generative-search analogy with Liang (10.2196/93761) for assessing evidence support separately from rating agreement. Its support classifications use an LLM judge and retrieval, not independent human adjudication. Armijo-Olivo (10.1002/jrsm.1398) is a second task-specific precedent on irrelevant quotations, but abstract-only access supports only a narrow qualitative statement.
5. Jardim (10.1186/s12874-022-01649-y) provides direct observations of reviewers using RobotReviewer snippets to locate/check evidence in ongoing reviews. This involved RoB 1 and no manual-only comparison; do not infer time savings. It is more useful for the clinical reader than another broad AI warning.
6. Riaz/Hasan's ROBINS-I framework (10.1136/bmjebm-2023-112597) may support a carefully labeled precedent for task-specific human checking in a different risk-of-bias instrument. The Riaz evidence-synthesis perspective (10.1016/j.mcpdig.2024.05.023) is relevant context but not empirical evidence of utility. Include either only if it supports a needed claim. No courtesy citations.

## Argument to develop

A risk-of-bias rating is a conclusion about a particular trial result. For a reviewer to evaluate that conclusion, the supporting material, answers and rule-based transition need to be visible. Earlier tools already aid this process, but the existence of a quotation and agreement with a reference do not establish the same property. ARBITER makes its particular assessment record available and tests the complete workflow against a defined minimal-prompt comparator. Separate expert judgments show an agreement advantage within this benchmark; conditional offered-label choice is a separate formal endpoint. D3 and Published-reference findings qualify the advantage. The next unanswered question is whether reviewers can use these records to identify unsupported interpretations and improve their work; this study did not measure that benefit.

The narrative should make these relationships easier to follow, not repeat every caveat or list every prior paper.
