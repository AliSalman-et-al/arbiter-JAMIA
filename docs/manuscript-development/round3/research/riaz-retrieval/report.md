## Useful practices observed in original text

These are article-level practices in selected, often coauthored work—not assertions about Riaz's personal prose style. The two added journal studies identify Riaz as corresponding author, and the JAMIA paper also lists him last. That does not isolate who wrote particular passages.

- **Explain the operational problem before naming the approach.** In the 2025 JAMIA article, Introduction, opening paragraph, data extraction is introduced as a recurring bottleneck in living reviews; the proposed collaborative LLM workflow follows that rationale. The 2026 EviSearch preprint similarly starts with the recurring cost and error risk of trial-data extraction before laying out system design (Introduction, first two paragraphs). Useful for preserving a clinical/evidence-synthesis reason for the work before introducing software.
- **Organize the system description around review tasks and human decisions.** JAMIA, Methods and Discussion, distinguishes single-model extraction, concordance, cross-critique and human review for discordant outputs. EviSearch, Introduction and System Overview, groups its design around attribution/verifiability, data governance and learning from corrections, then maps components to those needs. This makes a workflow auditable at the level where reviewers make decisions.
- **Keep performance denominators, outcomes and comparator adjacent.** JAMIA, Results, separates prompt-development from held-out test data and reports concordance and accuracy for each; EviSearch, Abstract and Results, distinguishes autonomous accuracy from accuracy after human review and identifies the fraction of cells routed to review. This supports clear description of workflow-conditioned estimates; these studies do not establish ARBITER performance.
- **State where automation stops.** JAMIA, Discussion and conclusion, treats discordance and remaining errors as reasons for human review, not as evidence that extraction is fully autonomous. EviSearch, Introduction and Results, routes disagreements to targeted human verification and reports the effort fraction. That is a useful pattern for describing inspectability and fallback without implying that a citation or extracted value is semantically correct merely because it has provenance.
- **Use a task-specific error taxonomy to connect process errors to clinical consequences.** The 2026 npj Digital Medicine paper, Results (Taxonomy development and error evaluation) and Discussion, defines reasoning failure categories and relates them to task and clinical-impact assessments. This supports naming the observable error class and outcome before making broader safety interpretations.
- **The solo JCO article supports only a limited observation.** The indexed publisher full text presents a special-series overview with broad oncology framing and thematic headings; it is one invited overview, not a stable personal preference. Its fulltext was inspected online, not saved locally. No Riaz-specific voice or sentence-pattern prescription follows from it.
