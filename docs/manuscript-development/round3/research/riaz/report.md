## Observed writing practices in the inspected papers

These are recurring tendencies in this small, selected corpus, not authorial rules.

- **The opening often moves quickly from a practical constraint to a bounded task.** The 2024 evidence-synthesis perspective opens on the manual effort and delay of systematic reviews before asking what current automation can do (Introduction, opening paragraphs). The 2025 distillation study moves from scarce longitudinal clinical outcomes to privacy/scalability constraints and then to the study's question (Introduction, paragraphs 1–4). This problem-to-task sequence may help ARBITER retain the reader's clinical reason for caring before introducing its architecture.
- **A section or paragraph is often organized around a workflow stage or decision.** The evidence-synthesis perspective divides the discussion by review tasks (search, study selection, extraction, risk-of-bias assessment, synthesis, certainty and narration), then proposes a workflow (Introduction, subheadings). The living review groups recommendations by prior treatment and biomarker context (Discussion, paragraphs 2–9), making the evidence navigable for clinical decisions. For ARBITER, keep each paragraph attached to one reader question: what is inspectable, what was compared, what agreement means, or what remains untested.
- **Numerical results are tied to named outcomes, comparator, and cohort.** The *npj Digital Medicine* report gives AUROC/AUPRC/F1 values with outcome and validation/test set named (Results, teacher-model and student-model subsections); it also puts subgroup and training-strategy findings under explicit subheads. This is a useful reporting habit for ARBITER's paired gains: retain each expert comparator, full denominator, percentage-point unit and interval next to the estimate; do not turn separate domain values into a cross-study ranking.
- **Limitations are stated as design boundaries and linked to claims.** The *npj Digital Medicine* discussion says the study was not intended to replace RECIST annotation, then lists lack of external validation and other limits (Discussion, first paragraph and limitations paragraph). The living review distinguishes strengths from limitations and identifies imprecision and inconsistent trial reporting (Discussion, final two paragraphs). The cancer-care review's conclusion names validation, privacy, bias and workflow integration as prerequisites (Conclusion). ARBITER already does this well when it explicitly distinguishes structural source binding from semantic evidence support and says human workload was not measured; preserve that specificity rather than adding generic AI caveats.
- **Technical terms are generally introduced beside the task they describe, but some review passages are dense.** The sampled works expand EHR, RECIST, and disease-context abbreviations in context, and use subheadings to segment complex material. The single-author 2026 overview has broader field language and longer thematic paragraphs; it shows why a polished clinical manuscript should define terms once and keep each paragraph's job visible instead of borrowing its review density.

Short excerpts, included only as locating cues (each under 10 words): “manual human effort” (2024 evidence-synthesis perspective, Introduction, first sentence); “the choice of public dataset” (2025 distillation study, Discussion, second paragraph); “not to derive models that can formally replace RECIST annotations” (same study, Discussion, first paragraph; 11 words); “by prior therapy and relevant biomarkers” (living review, Discussion, opening paragraph). No manuscript wording should be copied from these excerpts.

## Application to ARBITER

1. Keep the accepted system-and-evaluation arc. Open with the reviewer need to follow evidence into a defensible RoB 2 judgment; state the specific unknown about this implementation and comparison at the end of the Introduction. Do not replace that gap with a broad claim that no structured AI risk-of-bias system exists.
2. Use clinically intelligible paragraph jobs. Define a trial-domain cell before its first denominator. Explain D3 as missing outcome data and D5 as selection of the reported result on first use. Present architecture in terms of what the reviewer can inspect, then describe how the paired benchmark answers a separate performance question.
3. Keep evidence levels visible in the Results. Put ARBITER and Unassisted counts, paired difference, CI and reference side by side. Maintain the 53/60 conditional agreed-choice result with the 80 disagreements and 20 split choices immediately visible. Keep independent-expert comparisons distinct from published-label comparisons. A significant difference against one reference and a nonsignificant difference against another is not evidence of a difference between those references.
4. Keep observable process claims separate from clinical interpretation. Rule conformance and retrievable source spans establish computational/record properties; they do not establish that a cited passage supports the answer. Continue to state that human review time and downstream decisions were not measured.
5. Use limitations to calibrate this benchmark's inference, not as an undifferentiated list: one model configuration and run per arm; fixed reviews and two experts; ordered availability-dependent sample; possible prior model exposure; label anchoring in forced choice; semantic support and workload not measured. Each should connect to the claim it bounds.
6. Add no courtesy citations to Riaz. These papers are useful as writing examples, but author familiarity is not a scientific reason to cite them. Cite only a paper that directly supports an existing scientific statement after checking its design and relevance. The 2024 ROBINS-I study is a distinct nonrandomized-tool framework and should not be cited as evidence that ARBITER-like RoB 2 implementations are novel.
