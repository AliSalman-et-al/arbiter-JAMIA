## Consequential findings for ARBITER

### 1. Evidence traceability is established prior art; support validity is a distinct problem

RobotReviewer explicitly paired automated risk judgments with extracted supporting sentences. In its 2016 evaluation, sentence evidence was assessed by 20 blinded experienced reviewers against Cochrane review quotations, while the domain-level comparison used other published assessments as a proxy reference. Marshall et al. found that the retrieved text was rated comparably to human-selected text in their task, while the automated risk judgments were less accurate than the published-review benchmark (71.0% vs 78.3%). These are results under different tasks and reference constructions, not a score comparable with ARBITER. A 2020 RobotReviewer comparison then directly found high proportions of irrelevant supporting quotations in several older-tool domains and advised human checking of evidence. This is a close, task-specific basis for the manuscript’s distinction between source occurrence/binding and whether evidence supports an answer; the broad generative-search analogy can be supplemental or replaced by this closer precedent.

ROBoto2 is the closest predecessor in workflow shape: a public web platform performs PDF parsing, retrieval, RoB 2 prompting, provides evidence and proposed answers, and allows experts to accept, change, or override outputs. Its dataset and LLM benchmarking establish that interactive human review and source-linked answers are not exclusive ARBITER features. It does not make ARBITER’s particular hash/source-location/export pathway redundant; describe that implementation precisely and avoid claiming auditability as an unprecedented concept. Its reported benchmark also emphasizes model conservatism and evidence retrieval effects, but differences in dataset, models, references, and workflow prevent percentage ranking.

The 2026 Liang et al. study is exceptionally close to ARBITER’s unresolved evidentiary question: it generates RoB 2 answer/judgment claims over 97 trials, retrieves source passages, then uses an LLM verification step to assign supported/contradicted/not-found/out-of-scope verdicts. This supports adding a RoB-specific citation to the Discussion. Its key limitation for ARBITER’s framing is that the support verdict is LLM-assessed, not a human-validated semantic evidence standard; cite it as an evaluation precedent, not proof that automatic support scores establish validity. The 2020 Armijo-Olivo work reinforces that a cited or extracted sentence can be irrelevant. Thus “all answers cite a passage” and “the passage supports the interpretation” must remain separate statements.

### 2. Rule execution and interpretation are separable, and prior studies test both

Several studies ask the model to answer signalling questions and obtain ratings from a structured prompt or by applying the RoB 2 algorithm. Huang et al. explicitly investigated discrepancies between model-reported judgments and judgments implied by the model’s own signalling answers; Lai et al. 2024 used structured prompts with RoB guidance and repeat assessments. Rule-derived ratings are therefore not a novel concept by themselves. ARBITER’s implementation-specific contribution is that executable software applies the formal mapping and preserves answer/evidence/source records, while the model is assigned interpretation. In the present benchmark, conformance is evidence about that procedural transition only. It does not imply that source selection, answers, or adopted ratings are semantically defensible.

Choi et al. provide the most direct recent comparator on conformance: in a small, single-trial workshop pilot (13/15/10 runs for three configurations), they measured repeatability, expert-reference agreement, and whether reported domain ratings matched the ratings implied by each run’s own answers. They also checked skip logic and recomputed ratings. Their findings of variability and conformance violations motivate treating these as distinct evaluation targets, but the single article, small samples, one expert, workshop data-entry process, and descriptive design sharply limit generalization. ARBITER has the complementary design limitation: broad trial coverage but one run per arm, so it cannot characterize repeatability. The studies should be described as complementary; do not compare headline agreement percentages.

Joseph et al.’s 2026 RoB2-Evaluator preprint is another close rule-constrained implementation, including a repeated-run component. Its preliminary status and technical-evaluation design warrant the word “preprint” and restrained interpretation. The existence of another rule-constrained system means avoid exclusive claims about separating LLM interpretation from formal algorithms. Discuss its detailed system and observed repeatability only if the final journal version has room; the stronger must-cite additions are Choi (conformance/repeatability) and Liang or Armijo-Olivo (evidence support).

### 3. Prompt design / comparator is not itself the novelty

Lai et al. 2024 (JAMA Network Open) used structured RoB guidance and multiple runs on 30 RCTs; Huang et al. 2025 used structured RoB 2 prompting and twice assessed each trial with the same Claude version; Xiong et al. 2026 explicitly compares direct commands with progressively constrained prompts, albeit for the earlier RoB 1 tool. The latter is a particularly important counterexample to a claim that a same-model minimal-versus-structured comparison is new. ARBITER’s contrast is also not a component experiment: its arms differ in guidance, tool access/use, and processing, although supplied evidence and model family/settings were aligned. The result estimates a complete-workflow difference under this benchmark; it does not identify the rule engine’s causal contribution or establish superiority to structured comparators such as Huang/ROBoto2.

Prompt-development provenance is a separate reporting issue. The current Methods make the final prompt available, but the prior methodology audit found no account of how instructions were drafted, curated, or selected, or what data informed them. This search found no basis to infer tuning or benchmark contamination. Under TRIPOD-LLM claim discipline, report the known development history and overlap status, or say these details cannot be reconstructed; do not convert missing provenance into an allegation.

### 4. Expert references and published labels answer different questions

Prior RoB 2 studies use materially different references: Huang’s expert review process and Cochrane labels; Gandhi’s masked panel alongside original review authors; Nyrhi’s large multi-model study against published ratings; Lai’s small expert-reconciled sample; and RobotReviewer’s older published-review proxy. These establish that reference choice and label prevalence affect what agreement means. ARBITER’s independent-first dual expert ratings, with assessors freezing judgments before exposure to offered labels, is a useful design feature, but two experts are not an error-free standard. Its later forced choice measures which anonymous offered label an expert selected when labels disagreed; credit by label equality does not measure preference for the method or reasoning. Keep the conditional endpoint and all-cell comparisons distinct, report the two experts separately, and do not call the 53/60 share accuracy.

The corrected Huang article must be cited in its current corrected form. The 2025 JMIR correction (DOI 10.2196/80519) says editing changed the study design erroneously and corrects the article’s title/record from “Usability Study” to “Evaluation Study.” It does not authorize a change to the reported methods. The manuscript’s Huang citation is appropriately an evaluation-study title; retain the correction trail in bibliography/research notes where relevant.

### 5. Material positioning for Introduction and Discussion

The strongest concise literature synthesis groups prior work by function: (a) automated or interactive systems already expose candidate evidence (RobotReviewer, ROBoto2); (b) RoB 2 structured prompts and answer-to-rating derivation already have precedents (Lai, Huang); and (c) recent studies separately examine reference dependence, repeated outputs/conformance, and evidence support (Gandhi/Nyrhi, Choi/Joseph, Armijo-Olivo/Liang). Then state the implementation/evaluation gap narrowly: this paper evaluates one available evidence-retaining workflow end to end against the same model given minimal instructions, while keeping two independent-first expert references, conditional label-choice, full-cohort agreement, rule conformance, and structural binding analytically distinct. Avoid saying prior work has not evaluated evidence, algorithms, prompts, repeated runs, or fresh human references.

The Discussion should make three distinctions visible: (1) retrievable citation/source binding vs semantic evidence support; (2) answer-to-rating rule conformance vs quality of answers and judgment; (3) offline agreement/runtime vs effects on reviewer time, workload, or decisions. A short Choi comparison explains why one-run ARBITER results do not address repeatability. A short Liang/Armijo comparison grounds the unresolved evidence-support boundary. The benchmark’s one run per arm and minimal-prompt control are limits to interpretation, not a defect remedied by comparing unlike studies’ percentages.

## Saturation, gaps, and limitations

The search reached practical saturation for primary studies directly naming RobotReviewer, ROBoto2, RoB 2/LLM risk appraisal, rule conformance/repeatability, or source-evidence support: forward and backward trails from the newest close system/pilot and the manuscript’s citations repeatedly led to the 15-source ledger below. We found no additional direct RoB 2 system that changes the central story beyond those listed. Saturation is a judgment about this targeted search, not proof of exhaustiveness or novelty. Coverage of non-English, non-indexed, newly posted, or differently named systems can be incomplete.

Unresolved gaps: (i) exact prompt drafting/freeze provenance remains undocumented in source materials reviewed; (ii) Choi and Joseph are small/pilot or preprint evidence and neither establishes broad run variability; (iii) direct semantic-support assessment for ARBITER has not been performed; (iv) human workload and adoption benefits remain unmeasured; (v) the user-specified round2 story-contract path is absent, though an equivalent current contract exists. Some 2026 articles are very recent and full-text delivery differed by platform. No cross-study performance ranking is defensible because populations, input documents, outcomes, models, references, and repeated-run designs differ.
